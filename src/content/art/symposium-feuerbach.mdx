# Symposium

_Symposium_ is a famous painting by the German artist Anselm Feuerbach. He painted it in the late 19th century, with the best-known version completed in 1874. The painting is inspired by the ancient Greek philosopher Plato's work _Symposium_. It shows a group of people gathered together for a discussion and celebration.

The original _Symposium_ by Plato is a philosophical dialogue about love, beauty, and the search for truth. Feuerbach used this ancient story as the subject of his painting. He imagined the scene in a grand classical setting, filled with figures wearing ancient Greek clothing.

The painting shows several people sitting and reclining together. Among the figures are important characters from Plato's dialogue, including Plato himself and the famous poetess Sappho. The figures are not shown as ordinary people from Feuerbach's time. Instead, they appear idealized and timeless. Their calm poses and classical clothing create a connection with ancient Greek culture.

Feuerbach was strongly influenced by classical Greek art and culture. He spent many years in Italy, where he studied ancient art and Renaissance painting. His interest in the classical world can be clearly seen in _Symposium_. The large composition, soft colors, and peaceful atmosphere give the painting a feeling of harmony and dignity.

The importance of _Symposium_ comes from its connection between ancient philosophy and 19th century art. Feuerbach did not simply copy an ancient story. He used it to explore ideas about beauty, love, friendship, culture, and intellectual life. The painting shows how artists could look back at the ancient world to find inspiration for modern art.

The artwork also reflects the 19th century interest in classical Greece. During this period, many European artists and writers admired Greek ideas and art. Feuerbach's _Symposium_ became one of his most important works and helped establish his reputation as an artist inspired by classical subjects.

Today, _Symposium_ is valued for both its artistic beauty and its philosophical background. It connects painting with literature and philosophy. Like Plato's original work, it encourages viewers to think about love, beauty, human relationships, and the search for higher knowledge.
