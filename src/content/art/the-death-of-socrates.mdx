# The Death of Socrates

_The Death of Socrates_ is one of the most famous paintings by the French artist Jacques-Louis David. It was completed in 1787. The painting shows the final moments of the Greek philosopher Socrates before his death. It is based on the story of Socrates as described by the ancient Greek philosopher Plato.

Socrates was a famous philosopher in ancient Athens. He believed that people should question their beliefs and search for truth through reason and discussion. His ideas made him unpopular with some people in Athens. In 399 BCE, he was accused of corrupting the young and not respecting the city's gods. He was found guilty and sentenced to death.

The painting shows Socrates sitting on a bed while preparing to drink a cup of poison called hemlock. His friends and students surround him. Some are crying and showing sadness. Socrates, however, remains calm and confident. He raises one hand while speaking, as if he is still teaching his students.

David carefully arranged the figures to make Socrates the main focus. His body is strong and his posture is firm. This makes him look almost heroic. The use of light also draws attention to him. In contrast, the people around him show strong emotions. This creates a powerful difference between Socrates' calmness and the sadness of his followers.

The painting has an important connection to the ideas of the Enlightenment. During this period, many thinkers supported reason, knowledge, and freedom of thought. Socrates became a symbol of these values because he refused to give up his beliefs even when faced with death.

The painting was also created shortly before the French Revolution. Its message about courage, duty, and standing by one's principles had strong meaning for people living through political change. David used an ancient story to communicate ideas that were important to his own society.

_The Death of Socrates_ is important because it presents death not only as a tragic event but also as a moment of moral courage. Socrates chooses to follow the law and remain loyal to his principles. The painting reminds viewers that seeking truth and defending one's beliefs can require great courage.

Today, the artwork remains a powerful example of Neoclassical painting. Its clear composition, strong figures, dramatic emotions, and historical subject show how art can communicate philosophical and political ideas.
