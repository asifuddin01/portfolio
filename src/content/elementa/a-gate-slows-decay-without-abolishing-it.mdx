## The state is a pair

The LSTM's state at step $t$ is the pair $(\vec c_t,\vec h_t)$. A perturbation of
either changes the next step, so the one-step Jacobian has four blocks.
Differentiate (I.10.1) and (I.10.2) with $\sigma'=\sigma(1-\sigma)$ and
$\tanh'=1-\tanh^2$:

<Eq id="I.10.4" gloss="Only the top-left block is free of recurrent weights.">

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mat J_t&=
\begin{bmatrix}
\operatorname{diag}(\vec f_t) & \mat M_t\\
\mat D_t\operatorname{diag}(\vec f_t) & \mat N_t
\end{bmatrix},\\[4pt]
\mat M_t&=\operatorname{diag}\big(\vec c_{t-1}\odot\vec f_t\odot(1-\vec f_t)\big)\mat W_f\\
&\quad+\operatorname{diag}\big(\tilde{\vec c}_t\odot\vec i_t\odot(1-\vec i_t)\big)\mat W_i\\
&\quad+\operatorname{diag}\big(\vec i_t\odot(1-\tilde{\vec c}_t^2)\big)\mat W_c,\\[4pt]
\mat D_t&=\operatorname{diag}\big(\vec o_t\odot(1-\tanh^2\vec c_t)\big),\\
\mat N_t&=\operatorname{diag}\big(\tanh(\vec c_t)\odot\vec o_t\odot(1-\vec o_t)\big)\mat W_o
 +\mat D_t\mat M_t.
\end{aligned}
$$

</Eq>

The rows are $\vec c_t$ and $\vec h_t$; the columns are $\vec c_{t-1}$ and
$\vec h_{t-1}$. The top-left block is the carry line of (I.10.3). The block
$\mat M_t=\partial\vec c_t/\partial\vec h_{t-1}$ collects the three ways the
preceding hidden state reaches the cell: through the forget gate, the input
gate and the candidate. The bottom row passes the cell through the output
nonlinearity and adds the output gate's own dependence on $\vec h_{t-1}$.

Now multiply $\mat J_T\cdots\mat J_{s+1}$ and read the top-left block, which is
$\partial\vec c_T/\partial\vec c_s$. Expanding the product of block matrices
gives a sum over paths, one block chosen per step. The path that stays in the
top-left block at every step contributes
$\operatorname{diag}(\vec f_{s+1}\odot\cdots\odot\vec f_T)$. Any other path
leaves the cell row at some step. Since it must end on the cell row, it has
to return, and the only block from the hidden state to the cell is some
$\mat M_k$. Every other term therefore contains at least one recurrent matrix.
That proves I.10.T1.

The theorem guarantees that a weight-free path exists. It does not say that
path dominates. When the gates and candidate are saturated, the factors
$\vec f\odot(1-\vec f)$, $\vec i\odot(1-\vec i)$ and $1-\tilde{\vec c}^2$ are
small, $\mat M_t$ is small, and the carry line carries most of the signal.
That is the regime the 1997 truncation assumed. Away from it, the other paths
can be as large as the carry line or larger.

## The carry line still multiplies

Even on its own, the carry line is a product. Suppose a coordinate's forget
value is the same number $f$ at every step. After $n$ steps its factor is
$f^n=e^{n\ln f}$. Define the memory horizon $m=-1/\ln f$, the number of steps
over which the factor falls by $e$.

Apply $\ln x\leq x-1$, with equality only at $x=1$, at $x=f$ and at $x=1/f$.
The first gives $-\ln f>1-f$; the second gives $-\ln f<(1-f)/f$. Inverting,

<Eq id="I.10.5" gloss="A forget value sets a horizon within one step of 1/(1−f); a bias sets the forget value.">

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^n&=e^{-n/m},\qquad
\frac{f}{1-f}<m<\frac{1}{1-f},\\
f=\sigma(b)&\implies\frac{1}{1-f}=1+e^{b}.
\end{aligned}
$$

</Eq>

The second line uses $1-\sigma(b)=\sigma(-b)=1/(1+e^{b})$. With $b=\ln(m-1)$
the right-hand side is exactly $m$. That proves I.10.T2.

The figure plots $f^n$ for $f=0.9$, $0.99$ and $0.999$ over a thousand steps.
The first is gone below $10^{-4}$ before step ninety. The second keeps
$0.366032$ of its signal after a hundred steps and $4.3\times10^{-5}$ after a
thousand. The third reaches $1/e$ near the right-hand edge. Each curve is
still a straight line on the logarithmic scale: the decay is geometric, as
in I.9. What changed is the rate. It is a learned quantity, set coordinate by
coordinate, which can sit close to one without asking a weight matrix to have
norm close to one and without asking any unit to stay unsaturated.
[I.10.B03](/elementa/neural-networks/gating/problems/b03) works the limit.

## Where the forget bias starts

At initialisation the weights are small, so a forget gate's value is close to
$\sigma(b_f)$. A zero bias gives $f=1/2$: a horizon of $1/\ln2\approx1.44$
steps, which forgets almost everything before training has started.
A bias of one gives $f=0.731059$ and $1/(1-f)=1+e\approx3.72$.

Jozefowicz, Zaremba and Sutskever found that adding a bias of one to the
forget gate closed the gap they had measured between the LSTM and the GRU.
Keras adds one to the forget bias by default through `unit_forget_bias`.
PyTorch does not: it draws every weight and bias uniformly from
$(-1/\sqrt H,1/\sqrt H)$, and its two bias vectors both have a forget slice,
so the effective bias is their sum
([I.10.X02](/elementa/neural-networks/gating/problems/x02)).

Tallec and Ollivier went further. Their chrono initialisation sets
$b_f=\ln u$ with $u$ drawn uniformly between $1$ and $T_{\max}-1$, and
$b_i=-b_f$. By (I.10.5) the initial horizons then range from two steps to
the longest dependency expected, and $i=1-f$ makes each cell coordinate an
exponential moving average of its candidates
([I.10.X08](/elementa/neural-networks/gating/problems/x08)).
All of these choices set where training starts, not where it ends.

## Growth is still possible

The other paths of (I.10.4) can amplify. Take a scalar cell whose gates are
constants, $i=1/2$ and $f=o=3/4$, and whose candidate reads the hidden state
through a weight of two. Then $h_t$ is a function of $c_t$ alone, and each
step's total factor is
$f+i(1-\tilde c_t^2)\,w_c\,o(1-\tanh^2c_{t-1})$. Along the trajectory of
[I.10.B02](/elementa/neural-networks/gating/problems/b02) the factors are
$1.221326$ and $1.078827$, so $\partial c_3/\partial c_1=1.317600$. The
carry line alone contributes $0.75^2=0.5625$. More than half the derivative
came from paths through $w_c$, and the total exceeds one although every
forget value is three quarters.

The same mechanism can make a small perturbation grow.
In [I.10.X09](/elementa/neural-networks/gating/problems/x09) a cell with forget
value one half amplifies a perturbation of the zero state by $1.625$ per step.
Gating changes which paths exist; it does not bound their sum. Gradient norm
clipping, (I.9.7), remains a standard part of training gated networks.

The cell value itself is not bounded by a squashing function. Since
$|i\,\tilde c|<1$, each coordinate satisfies
$|c_t|\leq f_t|c_{t-1}|+1$. If every forget value is at most $\varphi<1$,
summing the geometric series gives

$$
|c_t|\leq\varphi^t|c_0|+\frac{1-\varphi^t}{1-\varphi},
$$

and with $\varphi=1$ the bound is $|c_0|+t$. That is the LSTM half of I.10.T3,
and the reason for the forget gate. A large cell value also saturates
$\tanh(c_t)$, which makes $\mat D_t$ small: the gradient from the output into
the cell vanishes while the carry line remains open.

## Backward through one cell

Write $\bar{\vec g}_{h,t}$ for the total derivative of the loss with respect to
$\vec h_t$, including the local readout and everything returned from step
$t+1$, and $\bar{\vec g}_{c,t}$ for the total derivative with respect to
$\vec c_t$. Then

<Eq id="I.10.6" gloss="The cell's gradient adds what it showed at this step to what it carried forward.">

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar{\vec g}_{c,t}&=\bar{\vec g}_{h,t}\odot\vec o_t\odot(1-\tanh^2\vec c_t)
 +\bar{\vec g}_{c,t+1}\odot\vec f_{t+1},\\
\vec\delta_{o,t}&=\bar{\vec g}_{h,t}\odot\tanh(\vec c_t)\odot\vec o_t\odot(1-\vec o_t),\\
\vec\delta_{f,t}&=\bar{\vec g}_{c,t}\odot\vec c_{t-1}\odot\vec f_t\odot(1-\vec f_t),\\
\vec\delta_{i,t}&=\bar{\vec g}_{c,t}\odot\tilde{\vec c}_t\odot\vec i_t\odot(1-\vec i_t),\\
\vec\delta_{c,t}&=\bar{\vec g}_{c,t}\odot\vec i_t\odot(1-\tilde{\vec c}_t^2),
\end{aligned}
$$

</Eq>

with $\bar{\vec g}_{c,T+1}=0$. Each $\vec\delta$ is the derivative with respect
to a gate's pre-activation. The hidden state one step back receives
$\sum_k\mat W_k^{\mathsf T}\vec\delta_{k,t}$ over the four blocks, plus its own
readout term; the cell one step back receives
$\bar{\vec g}_{c,t}\odot\vec f_t$. Shared weights accumulate exactly as in
(I.9.4): $\nabla_{\mat W_k}\mathcal L=\sum_t\vec\delta_{k,t}\vec h_{t-1}^{\mathsf T}$,
and likewise for $\mat U_k$ with $\vec x_t$.

The first line of (I.10.6) is the carry line in the backward direction: the
future's cell gradient arrives multiplied by a forget value, with no matrix in
between. [I.10.B02](/elementa/neural-networks/gating/problems/b02) derives
every line, and its script checks all twelve parameter blocks and both initial
states of a two-unit cell against finite differences.

## Reproduce the horizons

~~~python
from math import log

for f in (0.9, 0.99, 0.999):
    horizon = -1/log(f)
    print(f"{f}: f^100 = {f**100:.6f}, horizon {horizon:.3f},"
          f" bounds {f/(1-f):.1f} to {1/(1-f):.1f}")
~~~

It prints horizons $9.491$, $99.499$ and $999.500$, each just under
$1/(1-f)$, with hundred-step factors $0.000027$, $0.366032$ and $0.904792$.
