## The problem

Renal length is a routine clinical measurement and an awkward one to automate on
thick-slice non-contrast CT. Voxels are anisotropic — slices sit 8mm apart — and
kidneys are not axis-aligned. They sit at an angle, so counting axial slices
measures the projection of the kidney onto the scanner's z-axis rather than the
kidney.

## Approach

Segment the kidneys with a pretrained model, then compute length as the
**principal-axis extent of the mask in world-millimetre coordinates**. The
measurement follows the organ's own long axis, and it is expressed in millimetres
derived from the affine rather than in slices.

Reference values were parsed from free-text radiologist reports. The validation
cohort was stratified deliberately to over-represent the cases where the
measurement is hard and matters: hydronephrosis, staghorn calculi, atrophic
kidneys.

## The dataset is part of the contribution

De-identified, with each case linking a volumetric NIfTI scan to its DICOM-derived
metadata and its report, and carrying multi-label annotations for urolithiasis,
hydronephrosis, renal cysts, pyelonephritis, cystitis, nephrocalcinosis, renal
masses, and normal studies.
