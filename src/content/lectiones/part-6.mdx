One idea runs through all five: put images and text into a space where distance means the same thing for both.

CLIP builds that space. The four papers after it are increasingly cheap ways to reuse it — from training the bridge, to freezing both towers and training only the connector, to LLaVA, which is a linear projection and some generated data and which you can reproduce on hardware you own.

The trend is worth naming while you read. Over three years the trainable fraction of these systems falls by two orders of magnitude and the performance goes up.
