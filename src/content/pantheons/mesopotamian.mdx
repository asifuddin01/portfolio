The literature of the land between the Tigris and the Euphrates survives because
its people wrote on clay and clay does not burn. Sumerian scribes, and the
Akkadian, Babylonian and Assyrian writers who inherited their script, left the
earliest sustained mythology in any language.

Its preoccupations are civic. Gods hold offices — Anu the sky, Enlil the storm
and the assembly, Enki the fresh water and the wit — and the world is
administered rather than merely inhabited. *Enuma Elish* is a constitutional
document as much as a creation story: Marduk is granted supremacy by a vote,
and he earns it by killing Tiamat and building the sky out of her. The *Epic of
Gilgamesh* asks what a king is for once he understands he will die.

Nothing here is complete. Tablets break, columns are lost, and a scholar's
brackets stand where a line should be. Read it knowing that the gaps are part
of the text.
