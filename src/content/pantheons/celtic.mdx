Nearly everything survives at one remove. The Gauls left almost nothing of
their own; what we have of them is Caesar, who had reasons to describe them a
particular way, and archaeology. Irish and Welsh myth was written down between
the eighth and fourteenth centuries by monastic scribes who were preserving a
tradition they did not share, and who fitted its gods into biblical history as
early inhabitants rather than divinities.

Read past that and the material is extraordinary. The *Lebor Gabála Érenn*
gives Ireland six successive peoples, of whom the Tuatha Dé Danann — Lugh, the
Dagda, Brigid, the Morrígan — are the gods in all but name. The *Táin Bó
Cúailnge* turns on a cattle raid and a seventeen-year-old who cannot be
stopped. The Welsh *Mabinogi* is stranger and quieter, and its logic is closer
to dream.

The Otherworld is never far off, and it is not the land of the dead. It is
adjacent, reachable, and dangerous chiefly because time runs differently
there.
