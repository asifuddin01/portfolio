Almost all of it comes from Iceland, and almost all of it was written by
Christians. The *Poetic Edda* preserves older verse; the *Prose Edda* was
composed around 1220 by Snorri Sturluson, a politician and poet who wanted to
teach skaldic technique and needed to explain the myths the metaphors depended
on. Without him we would have very little.

What distinguishes it is that the end is known in advance. Ragnarök is not a
threat but a schedule: the wolf gets loose, Thor kills the serpent and walks
nine paces, Odin is swallowed, and the gods who know this go on anyway. Odin
spends the whole mythology gathering intelligence about a defeat he cannot
avert.

Yggdrasil holds the nine worlds; the Norns are at its root and outrank
everybody. And Loki — bound at last under a serpent's venom, with his wife
holding a bowl — is neither an enemy nor a friend, which is the most
interesting thing about him.
