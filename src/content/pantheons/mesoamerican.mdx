This is the mythology of the Maya lowlands and highlands, of the Mixtec and
Zapotec, and behind them of the Olmec, whose iconography everyone afterwards
inherited. Its survival is an accident of what was not destroyed: four Maya
codices out of a library, a handful of Mixtec screenfolds, inscriptions on
stone that were only deciphered within living memory.

The *Popol Vuh* is the great text — a K'iche' Maya account written in
alphabetic script in the sixteenth century, telling of the Hero Twins who
defeat the lords of Xibalba at their own ballgame, and of the several failed
attempts to make people, from mud and from wood, before maize succeeds.

Time is the recurring subject. The Long Count is a calendar built to hold
millions of days, and the great cycles of creation and destruction are counted
in it exactly.
