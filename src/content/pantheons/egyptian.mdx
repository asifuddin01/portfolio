Egypt has no *Theogony*, no one book that settles what happened. What it has is
the Pyramid Texts, the Coffin Texts, the compositions later collected as the
Book of the Dead, temple inscriptions, and a habit of letting incompatible
accounts stand side by side. Ra sails the sky and is also swallowed nightly by
Nut and born again from her; Atum creates by speech at Heliopolis and by hand
at Memphis. Neither cancels the other.

The persistent story is Osiris: a king murdered by his brother Set,
reassembled by Isis, and made sovereign of the dead rather than restored to
the living, while his son Horus contests the throne. It runs for two millennia
and carries the political theory with it — every pharaoh is Horus alive and
Osiris once buried.

*Ma'at* is the load-bearing idea: order, balance and truth, which the gods
maintain and the king is obliged to. The alternative is not chaos as disorder
but *isfet*, the active undoing of what holds.
