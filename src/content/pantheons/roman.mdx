Rome's own religion is not chiefly a body of stories. It is a body of practice —
which rite, performed by whom, at what hour, in what words, on which of the
year's marked days. Getting it wrong invalidates it. The characteristic Roman
text is not an epic but a calendar.

The stories that do survive are largely Greek, absorbed and re-labelled:
Jupiter for Zeus, Juno for Hera, Venus for Aphrodite. Ovid's *Metamorphoses*
is the great collection and is Greek in substance throughout. What is properly
Roman is elsewhere — Janus, who has no Greek equivalent; Vesta and her hearth;
the *numina*, the presences in a doorway, a boundary stone, a river.

The one indigenous mythology Rome made for itself was about Rome: Aeneas out
of Troy, the she-wolf, Romulus and the furrow, the rape of the Sabines. It is
national foundation myth, and Virgil wrote it to order.
