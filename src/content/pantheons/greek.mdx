We know Greek myth well because the Greeks wrote it down early, argued about it
in public, and put it on stage. Homer assumes it, Hesiod systematises it in the
*Theogony*, the tragedians use it to think with, and by the Hellenistic period
mythographers are collating variants the way a modern editor collates
manuscripts.

Its gods are anthropomorphic to an extent that embarrassed later philosophers:
they lie, they are jealous, they lose their tempers and take it out on cities.
Xenophanes complained about this in the sixth century BCE and the complaint has
never really stopped. The compensating idea is that the universe has a shape
they do not control — the Moirai spin, and Zeus does not overrule them.

Watch for the local variants. "Greek mythology" as a single system is a
convenience of textbooks; Argos, Athens and Thebes each told it differently,
and often with an eye to who ought to be in charge.
