A cell without a forget gate, as in 1997, has $f=1$. Its input gate and
candidate are constant at $i=1$ and $\tilde c=0.1$, its output gate at $o=1$,
and $c_0=0$. Find $c_{200}$ and the derivative $\partial h/\partial c$ there,
both by the formula $1-\tanh^2c$ evaluated in float64 and by
$\operatorname{sech}^2c$. Repeat with $f=0.9$.

<Hint>
Without forgetting, the cell adds the same amount every step. With $f<1$ it
approaches $i\tilde c/(1-f)$.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

With $f=1$, $c_t=0.1\,t$, so $c_{200}=20$. Mathematically
$\partial h/\partial c=1-\tanh^2(20)=\operatorname{sech}^2(20)=1.699342\times10^{-17}$.
In float64, $\tanh(20)$ rounds to exactly one, because it differs from one by
about $8.5\times10^{-18}$, less than half the spacing of doubles below one.
The formula $1-\tanh^2c$ then returns exactly $0.0$.

Either way the output path into the cell is closed. The carry line still
passes gradients from cell to cell with factor one, but no loss on $h$ can send
anything into the cell. On a long unsegmented stream the cell keeps growing,
which is the failure Gers, Schmidhuber and Cummins identified.

With $f=0.9$, $c_t=0.1(1-0.9^t)/(1-0.9)=1-0.9^t$, so $c_{200}=1.000000$ to
six places, and $\operatorname{sech}^2(1)=0.419974$. The bound of I.10.T3,
$1/(1-\varphi)=10$, is not tight here, but it is finite.

</SolutionToggle>
