<Statement>
Evaluate binary cross-entropy at four logits by two routes: the naive one that
forms a probability first, and the stable identity that never does. Derive the
identity, show the naive route failing on two ordinary inputs, and state the
logit at which each failure begins.
</Statement>

<Given>
A logit $z \in \R$, a label $y \in \{0,1\}$, and $p = \sigma(z) = 1/(1+e^{-z})$.
The naive computation is

$$\mathrm{BCE}_{\text{naive}} = -\big[\,y\log p + (1-y)\log(1-p)\,\big]$$

Test at $(z, y) \in \{(2,1),\ (0,1),\ (-5,1),\ (-50,1),\ (-800,1),\ (800,0)\}$.
IEEE double precision: $e^{x}$ overflows for $x > 709.78$, and $\sigma(z)$ rounds
to exactly $1.0$ once $e^{-z} < 2^{-53} \approx 1.11\times10^{-16}$.
</Given>

<Find>
The stable identity; both routes' values at each test point; and the two
thresholds at which the naive route breaks.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Derive the identity by substituting $\sigma$ into the definition and simplifying
until no probability appears — only $z$ and a logarithm of something safely near
one. Then evaluate both routes and watch where they part.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

### Part 1 — deriving the stable form

**Step 1 — substitute for $y = 1$.** With $p = 1/(1+e^{-z})$:

$$
-\log p = -\log\frac{1}{1+e^{-z}} = \log\left(1 + e^{-z}\right)
$$

using $-\log(1/a) = \log a$.

**Step 2 — substitute for $y = 0$.** First simplify $1 - p$:

$$
1 - p = 1 - \frac{1}{1+e^{-z}} = \frac{(1+e^{-z}) - 1}{1+e^{-z}} = \frac{e^{-z}}{1+e^{-z}}
$$

so

$$
-\log(1-p) = -\log e^{-z} + \log\left(1+e^{-z}\right) = z + \log\left(1+e^{-z}\right)
$$

**Step 3 — one formula for both.** Combining Steps 1 and 2:

$$\mathrm{BCE}(z,y) = -zy + z(1-y)\cdot 0 + \dots$$

More carefully — write the two cases and look for the pattern:

$$
\mathrm{BCE} =
\begin{cases}
\log(1+e^{-z}) & y = 1\\
z + \log(1+e^{-z}) & y = 0
\end{cases}
\;=\; z(1-y) + \log\!\left(1+e^{-z}\right)
$$

**Step 4 — the remaining danger.** This is exact, but $e^{-z}$ still overflows
for $z < -709.78$. Fix it by pulling out the larger of the two terms inside the
logarithm. For $z < 0$ write $1 + e^{-z} = e^{-z}(1 + e^{z})$, so

$$\log(1+e^{-z}) = -z + \log(1+e^{z})$$

Substituting into Step 3's formula for $z < 0$ and combining with the $z \ge 0$
case gives the symmetric form

$$
\boxed{\ \mathrm{BCE}(z,y) = \max(z,0) - zy + \log\!\left(1 + e^{-|z|}\right)\ }
\tag{I.4.5}
$$

**Why this one is safe.** The exponential's argument is $-|z| \le 0$, so
$e^{-|z|} \in (0, 1]$ and can never overflow. It can underflow to $0$, but then
$\log(1+0) = 0$ exactly, which is the correct limit rather than an error. Every
other term is elementary arithmetic on $z$ itself.

### Part 2 — the two routes side by side

| $z$ | $y$ | $\sigma(z)$ | naive | stable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $2.0$ | $1$ | $8.807971\mathrm{e}{-1}$ | $0.126928$ | $0.126928$ |
| $0.0$ | $1$ | $5.000000\mathrm{e}{-1}$ | $0.693147$ | $0.693147$ |
| $-5.0$ | $1$ | $6.692851\mathrm{e}{-3}$ | $5.006715$ | $5.006715$ |
| $-50.0$ | $1$ | $1.928750\mathrm{e}{-22}$ | $50.000000$ | $50.000000$ |
| $-800.0$ | $1$ | overflow | **OverflowError** | $800.000000$ |
| $800.0$ | $0$ | $1.000000\mathrm{e}{+0}$ | **ValueError** | $800.000000$ |

**Step 5 — verifying agreement where both work.** At the three safe points the
two routes agree to ten decimal places, with $|{\text{diff}}| \le 2.78\times10^{-17}$
— one unit in the last place of a double. So (I.4.5) is not an approximation; it
is the same number computed differently.

**Step 6 — the first failure, $z = -800$, $y = 1$.** The naive route needs
$\sigma(-800) = 1/(1 + e^{800})$. But $e^{800}$ exceeds the largest double
($1.798\times10^{308}$) and overflows, raising `OverflowError` before any
logarithm is reached.

The stable route computes

$$
\max(-800, 0) - (-800)(1) + \log\!\left(1 + e^{-800}\right) = 0 + 800 + \log(1) = 800.000000
$$

which is correct: a logit of $-800$ with target $1$ is wrong by $800$ nats.

**Step 7 — the second failure, $z = 800$, $y = 0$.** Here
$e^{-800}$ underflows to $0$, so $\sigma(800)$ evaluates to exactly $1.0$. The
naive route then needs $\log(1 - 1.0) = \log(0) = -\infty$, and Python raises
`ValueError`. In a framework that returns $-\infty$ silently instead, the loss
becomes `inf`, every gradient becomes `nan`, and the entire model is destroyed
in one step with no message.

The stable route gives $\max(800,0) - (800)(0) + \log(1+e^{-800}) = 800 + 0 = 800.000000$.

**Step 8 — the thresholds.**

*Overflow.* $e^{-z}$ overflows when $-z > 709.78$, so the naive route fails for
$z < -709.78$.

*Rounding to one.* $\sigma(z)$ becomes exactly $1.0$ once $e^{-z}$ falls below
the spacing of doubles near $1$, that is $e^{-z} < 2^{-53}$, giving
$z > 53\ln 2 = 36.74$. **This is the more dangerous of the two**, because
$z = 37$ is an entirely ordinary logit — it appears whenever a model becomes
confident — and the failure is a silent $-\infty$ rather than a raised
exception.

In float32 the corresponding threshold is $z > 24\ln 2 = 16.6$, which is
reached routinely within the first epoch of ordinary training.

</Solution>

<Answer>
$$\mathrm{BCE}(z,y) = \max(z,0) - zy + \log\!\left(1+e^{-|z|}\right)$$

Both routes agree to within $2.78\times10^{-17}$ where the naive one works, and
it fails at $z = -800$ (overflow) and $z = 800$ (log of zero), where the stable
form returns $800.000000$ in both cases.

Thresholds in double precision: overflow below $z = -709.78$; silent saturation
to $p = 1$ above $z = 36.74$. In float32 the second is $z = 16.6$.
</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
**The identity gives the right answer at $z = 0$.** There $p = 0.5$ and the loss
should be $-\log 0.5 = \log 2 = 0.693147$. The formula gives
$\max(0,0) - 0 + \log(1+e^{0}) = \log 2$ ✓.

**Large-$|z|$ behaviour is linear, as it must be.** For $z \to -\infty$ with
$y=1$, $-\log\sigma(z) = \log(1+e^{-z}) \approx -z$. The table confirms it:
$z = -50 \Rightarrow 50.000000$ and $z = -800 \Rightarrow 800.000000$, both equal
to $|z|$ to six decimals. A loss growing linearly rather than exponentially in
the logit is exactly why cross-entropy's gradient stays bounded.

**The two failures are the two ends of the same problem.** Underflow of
$e^{-|z|}$ is harmless — $\log(1+0) = 0$ is correct. Overflow of $e^{+|z|}$ is
fatal. The identity's whole content is arranging that only the harmless one can
occur.

**Symmetry check.** $\mathrm{BCE}(z, 1) = \mathrm{BCE}(-z, 0)$: predicting logit
$z$ for a positive should cost the same as predicting $-z$ for a negative. From
the formula at $z = 2$: $\mathrm{BCE}(2,1) = 2 - 2 + \log(1+e^{-2}) = 0.126928$
and $\mathrm{BCE}(-2,0) = 0 - 0 + \log(1+e^{-2}) = 0.126928$ ✓.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
The identity is exact in exact arithmetic and *nearly* exact in floating point —
the $2.78\times10^{-17}$ discrepancy is real, not a display artefact. It comes
from $\log(1+x)$ losing precision when $x$ is tiny: the addition $1 + x$ discards
most of $x$'s bits before the logarithm sees it.

The remedy is `log1p(x)`, which computes $\log(1+x)$ accurately for small $x$ by
a series expansion rather than by forming the sum. Every serious implementation
uses it. The point generalises: this problem removed one catastrophic failure and
left a benign one, and knowing which is which is the actual skill
(<ApparatusRef ref="0.NU.04" />).
</Breaks>
