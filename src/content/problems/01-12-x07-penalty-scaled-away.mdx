On the four points of
[I.12.B01](/elementa/neural-networks/autoencoders/problems/b01), a
width-one linear autoencoder has $\mat E=(0.8,\,0.6)/c$ and
$\mat D=c\,(0.8,\,0.6)^{\mathsf T}$ for a constant $c>0$. Its objective is
the reconstruction loss plus $\beta$ times the mean of $|z|$ over the
data, with $\beta=0.5$.

(a) Evaluate the objective at $c=1$, $10$ and $1000$. What is its infimum,
and is it attained?

(b) Now hold the decoder at $(0.8,\,0.6)^{\mathsf T}$ and let each input
take whichever code is best for it. Find that code for a corner of the
rectangle, and the objective.

(c) For which $\beta$ is the best code in (b) zero?

<Hint>
Each corner has a score of $\pm2$ along $(0.8,0.6)$ and $\pm1$ along
$(-0.6,0.8)$. Use (I.12.9) for (b).
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**(a)** The product $\mat D\mat E$ does not depend on $c$. It is the
projection onto $(0.8,0.6)$, with reconstruction loss $1$. The codes are
$\pm2/c$, with mean absolute value $2/c$. So the objective is

$$
1+\frac{0.5\cdot2}{c}=1+\frac1c,
$$

which is $2$, $1.1$ and $1.001$ at the three values of $c$.

Its infimum is $1$, the unpenalised loss, and no finite $c$ attains it.
Along the way the encoder shrinks towards zero and the decoder grows
without bound. Nothing about the function computed has changed. The
penalty has selected for large decoder weights and done nothing else.

**(b)** With a unit-norm decoder the error of one input splits along the
two eigenvectors. For a corner with scores $a=\pm2$ and $b=\pm1$, a code
$z$ gives

$$
(a-z)^2+b^2+\beta|z|.
$$

By (I.12.9) the best code is
$\operatorname{sign}(a)\,(|a|-\beta/2)=\pm1.75$. The objective is

$$
0.25^2+1+0.5\cdot1.75=1.9375,
$$

against $2$ for the unshortened code $\pm2$. The penalty now trades
something real: codes an eighth shorter, and a reconstruction error of
$1.0625$ where it was $1$.

**(c)** The code is zero when $\beta/2\geq|a|=2$, that is, $\beta\geq4$.
The unit is then never active, the reconstruction is the origin for every
input, and the reconstruction loss is the total variance, $5$.

**The fix.** Hold each decoder column at unit norm, or weight $|z_j|$ by
the norm of its column, so that the product of the two scales is what is
penalised. A penalty on the encoder's Jacobian has the same weakness, and
tied weights remove it there.
