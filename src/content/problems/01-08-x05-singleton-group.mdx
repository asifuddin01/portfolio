Using (I.8.1), show what happens to a training normalisation group with one
scalar $x$. Find the output and its derivative with respect to $x$.
Explain why “batch normalisation fails at batch size one” is too broad,
and why a large batch does not rescue layer normalisation over width one.

<Hint>
Count entries per group, including any spatial axes being reduced.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

For $m=1$, the mean is $x$ and the variance is zero. With
$\varepsilon>0$, $\hat x=0$ and $y=\beta$ for every $x$.
Therefore $\partial y/\partial x=0$ and $\partial y/\partial\gamma=0$.
Only beta receives an upstream gradient from this scalar output.
Substituting $m=1$ and $\hat x=0$ into (I.8.3) also gives zero.

For dense batch normalisation on a matrix, batch size one means one value
per feature group. In spatial batch normalisation, the group instead contains
$NHW$ values per channel. With $N=1$ and multiple spatial positions, it need
not be a singleton. Highly correlated positions may still give poor
statistics, but that is a different failure.

Layer normalisation over a feature width of one has the same algebraic
degeneracy regardless of how many examples are in the batch: examples do
not share its statistics. A framework may reject a degenerate training
configuration before performing this calculation.

</SolutionToggle>
