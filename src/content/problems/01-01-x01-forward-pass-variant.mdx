Using the network of I.1.B01 unchanged, compute the forward pass for
$\vec{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$. Before computing, predict how
many hidden units will be switched off, and say what that prediction rests on.

<Hint>
The first hidden unit's pre-activation is $x_1 - x_2 + 0.5$. Ask what sign that
takes when $x_1 > x_2$.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**Prediction.** The first unit computes $x_1 - x_2 + 0.5$, which is positive
whenever $x_1 > x_2 - 0.5$. Here $2 > 0.5$, so it stays on. The second computes
$2x_2 - 1$, positive when $x_2 > 0.5$; here $x_2 = 1$, so it also stays on.
Prediction: **no dead units**, unlike I.1.B01 where the first died.

**Step 1 — pre-activations.**

$$
z^{(1)}_1 = (2)(1) + (1)(-1) + 0.5 = 2 - 1 + 0.5 = 1.5
$$
$$
z^{(1)}_2 = (2)(0) + (1)(2) - 1 = 0 + 2 - 1 = 1
$$

$$\vec{z}^{(1)} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Step 2 — ReLU.** Both entries are positive, so ReLU is the identity here:

$$\vec{a}^{(1)} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Step 3 — output.**

$$\hat{y} = (1.5)(2) + (1)(1) - 1 = 3 + 1 - 1 = 3$$

**Answer.** $\vec{a}^{(1)} = (1.5,\ 1)$, $\hat{y} = 3$, and no unit is dead —
as predicted.

**What this shows.** The set of active units is a function of the input, not of
the weights alone. The same network is, for each input, effectively a *different*
linear map — the one obtained by deleting the dead units. A ReLU network is a
piecewise-linear function, and which piece you are on is decided at the input.
That observation is the whole of Proposition I.3.P01.
</SolutionToggle>
