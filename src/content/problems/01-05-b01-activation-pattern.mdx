<Statement>
Push three inputs through the same two-layer network. Report the
pre-activations, the activations and the output for each, and then answer the
question the numbers are really for: which of the three inputs are processed by
the *same* affine map, and how you can tell without computing the output at all.
</Statement>

<Given>
Two inputs, three hidden ReLU units, one output. Row-major throughout
(<ApparatusRef ref="0.LA.01" />), so a layer is applied on the right.

$$
\mat{W}^{(1)} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & 0.5 \\ 0.5 & 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\vec{b}^{(1)} = \begin{bmatrix} -0.5 & 0.25 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
$$

$$
\mat{W}^{(2)} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \\ 1.5 \end{bmatrix},
\qquad
b^{(2)} = 0.75
$$

The three inputs:

$$
\vec{x}_a = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
\vec{x}_b = \begin{bmatrix} 1.2 & 2.5 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
\vec{x}_c = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
$$
</Given>

<Find>
For each input: $\vec{z}^{(1)}$, $\vec{a}^{(1)}$, the activation pattern, and
$\hat{y}$. Then the effective affine map $\hat{y} = \vec{x}\vec{w} + c$ that the
network applies on each pattern.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Compute the pre-activations first and read the pattern off their signs, before
touching the second layer. The pattern is what decides everything that follows,
so getting it in hand early turns the rest into arithmetic.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**Step 1 — the pre-activations for $\vec{x}_a$.** This is the first half of one
step of (I.5.1): each entry of $\vec{z}^{(1)}$ pairs $\vec{x}$ with one
*column* of $\mat{W}^{(1)}$.

$$
z_1 = (1)(1) + (2)(0.5) - 0.5 = 1.5, \qquad
z_2 = (1)(-1) + (2)(1) + 0.25 = 1.25
$$

$$
z_3 = (1)(0.5) + (2)(-1) + 0 = -1.5
$$

So $\vec{z}^{(1)} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & 1.25 & -1.5 \end{bmatrix}$ and the
**activation pattern is $110$** — units one and two on, unit three off.

**Step 2 — activation and output.**
$\vec{a}^{(1)} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & 1.25 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$, and

$$\hat{y} = (1.5)(2) + (1.25)(-1) + (0)(1.5) + 0.75 = 3 - 1.25 + 0.75 = 2.5$$

**Step 3 — the second input.** $\vec{z}^{(1)} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.95 & 1.55 & -1.9 \end{bmatrix}$,
pattern **$110$ again**, and $\hat{y} = 3.9 - 1.55 + 0.75 = 3.1$.

**Step 4 — the third input.**
$\vec{z}^{(1)} = \begin{bmatrix} -1.5 & 1.25 & -0.5 \end{bmatrix}$, pattern
**$010$**, $\vec{a}^{(1)} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1.25 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$, and
$\hat{y} = -1.25 + 0.75 = -0.5$.

**Step 5 — the map each pattern selects.** Write the pattern as a diagonal
matrix $\mat{D}$ of ones and zeros. Then ReLU has been replaced by a fixed
linear map, and the whole network collapses on that region into
equation (I.5.3):

$$
\hat{y} = \vec{x}\left(\mat{W}^{(1)}\mat{D}\,\mat{W}^{(2)}\right)
        + \left(\vec{b}^{(1)}\mat{D}\,\mat{W}^{(2)} + b^{(2)}\right)
$$

For $\mat{D} = \mathrm{diag}(1,1,0)$:

$$
w_1 = (1)(2) + (-1)(-1) = 3, \qquad
w_2 = (0.5)(2) + (1)(-1) = 0
$$

$$
c = (-0.5)(2) + (0.25)(-1) + 0.75 = -0.5
$$

so on that region $\hat{y} = 3x_1 + 0\,x_2 - 0.5$. For
$\mat{D} = \mathrm{diag}(0,1,0)$: $w_1 = 1$, $w_2 = -1$, $c = 0.5$, giving
$\hat{y} = x_1 - x_2 + 0.5$.

**Step 6 — the answer to the question actually asked.** $\vec{x}_a$ and
$\vec{x}_b$ share the pattern $110$, so they share one affine map;
$\vec{x}_c$ does not. You can tell from the signs of $\vec{z}^{(1)}$ alone,
which is three inner products — the second layer never enters the decision.

</Solution>

<Answer>

| input | $\vec{z}^{(1)}$ | pattern | $\hat{y}$ | map on that region |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\vec{x}_a$ | $(1.5,\ 1.25,\ -1.5)$ | $110$ | $2.5$ | $3x_1 + 0x_2 - 0.5$ |
| $\vec{x}_b$ | $(1.95,\ 1.55,\ -1.9)$ | $110$ | $3.1$ | $3x_1 + 0x_2 - 0.5$ |
| $\vec{x}_c$ | $(-1.5,\ 1.25,\ -0.5)$ | $010$ | $-0.5$ | $x_1 - x_2 + 0.5$ |

The first two inputs lie in one linear region and the third in another.
</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
**The effective map reproduces the output.** On the region $110$,
$3(1) + 0(2) - 0.5 = 2.5$ and $3(1.2) + 0(2.5) - 0.5 = 3.1$. Two different
inputs, one formula, the right answers. That is the content of a linear region
stated as an arithmetic check.

**The dead unit cannot matter.** Unit three is off for $\vec{x}_a$, so the
third entry of $\mat{W}^{(2)}$ — the value $1.5$ — cannot influence $\hat{y}$.
Change it to $150$ and recompute: still $2.5$.

**The second coordinate genuinely drops out.** On region $110$ the coefficient
of $x_2$ is exactly zero, so moving $\vec{x}_a$ to $(1, 5)$ should not change
the output as long as the pattern holds. Check the pattern first:
$\vec{z}^{(1)} = (3, 4.25, -4.5)$, still $110$, and
$\hat{y} = 6 - 4.25 + 0.75 = 2.5$. It does not change.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
The whole calculation assumes the pattern is read at the given input. It is not
a property of the network — it is a property of the network *and* the input
together. A common mistake is to speak of "the dead units" of a model as though
the set were fixed; unit three is off here and on for $\vec{x} = (2, 0)$, where
$z_3 = 1$. Only a unit off for *every* input in the dataset is dead in the sense
of I.3, and that is a much stronger and much rarer claim.
</Breaks>
