Prove that the constant $c$ minimising $\mathbb{E}[(Y - c)^2]$ is
$c^\star = \mathbb{E}[Y]$, and that the minimum value is $\mathrm{Var}(Y)$. Then
state the conditional version and say what it implies about what a
squared-loss-trained network is estimating.

<Hint>
Add and subtract $\mathbb{E}[Y]$ inside the square, then expand and use
linearity of expectation.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**Step 1 — decompose.** Write $\mu = \mathbb{E}[Y]$ and insert $\mu - \mu$:

$$
\mathbb{E}\big[(Y-c)^2\big] = \mathbb{E}\Big[\big((Y - \mu) + (\mu - c)\big)^2\Big]
$$

**Step 2 — expand the square.**

$$
= \mathbb{E}\Big[(Y-\mu)^2 + 2(Y-\mu)(\mu-c) + (\mu-c)^2\Big]
$$

**Step 3 — take expectations term by term**, using linearity
(<ApparatusRef ref="0.PR.02" />) and noting $(\mu - c)$ is a constant:

$$
= \underbrace{\mathbb{E}\big[(Y-\mu)^2\big]}_{\mathrm{Var}(Y)}
+ 2(\mu-c)\underbrace{\mathbb{E}[Y-\mu]}_{=\,0}
+ (\mu-c)^2
$$

The middle term vanishes because $\mathbb{E}[Y - \mu] = \mathbb{E}[Y] - \mu = 0$
by the definition of $\mu$. That cancellation is the whole proof.

**Step 4 — read off the minimum.**

$$\mathbb{E}\big[(Y-c)^2\big] = \mathrm{Var}(Y) + (\mu - c)^2$$

The first term does not involve $c$; the second is a square, so non-negative,
and is zero exactly when $c = \mu$. Hence

$$c^\star = \mathbb{E}[Y], \qquad \min_c \mathbb{E}\big[(Y-c)^2\big] = \mathrm{Var}(Y)$$

$\blacksquare$

**Step 5 — the conditional version.** Applying the same argument at each $x$
separately, with all expectations conditioned on $X = x$:

$$f^\star(x) = \mathbb{E}[Y \mid X = x]$$

**What this says about a trained network.** Three things, in order of how often
they are missed.

*The target of training is the conditional mean, not a sample.* Given a dataset
with two identical inputs and different labels — say $y = 0$ and $y = 10$ — the
squared-loss optimum predicts $5$, a value that appears nowhere in the data and
may be impossible. On a bimodal conditional distribution, the mean can sit in a
region of zero density.

*The irreducible loss is the conditional variance.* $\mathrm{Var}(Y \mid X = x)$
cannot be reduced by any model whatsoever. A training loss that has plateaued at
a nonzero value may be at the optimum, and no architecture change will move it.
Knowing that number — estimable from repeated measurements at the same input —
tells you when to stop trying.

*The optimum is over all functions, not over the hypothesis class.* The network
approaches $\mathbb{E}[Y|X]$ only insofar as that function lies in its class and
the optimiser finds it. I.4.T2's scope note says exactly this, and it is the same
gap I.1.T1 identified between existence and reachability.

**The connection to blurry generative outputs.** A model trained with squared
loss to produce images predicts the pixel-wise conditional mean. Where several
sharp outputs are equally plausible, their mean is a blur. This is not a failure
of capacity or of data — it is the loss doing exactly what this proof says it
does, and no amount of training fixes it. Changing the loss does.
</SolutionToggle>
