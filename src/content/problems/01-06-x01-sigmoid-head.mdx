Take the network of I.6.B01 and replace the linear head with a sigmoid, so
$\hat{y} = \sigma(z^{(2)})$. Everything else is unchanged. Predict which of the
nine gradients move and in what proportion *before* recomputing, then verify.

<Hint>
The change adds exactly one node to the graph, between $z^{(2)}$ and the loss.
By (I.6.2), what does an extra node on the path from the sink do to every
adjoint below it?
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**The prediction.** A new node sits between $z^{(2)}$ and $L$. Every adjoint in
the graph is reached through it, so every one is multiplied by the same scalar —
gradients change, but all by a common factor. Nothing changes shape and nothing
changes sign relative to the others.

**The numbers.**

| quantity | linear head | sigmoid head |
|---|---|---|
| $\hat{y}$ | $0.05011480$ | $0.51252608$ |
| $L$ | $0.45114095$ | $0.11881541$ |
| $\delta^{(2)}$ | $-0.94988520$ | $-0.12179199$ |
| $\partial L/\partial \mat{W}^{(2)}$ | $-0.59126491,\ -0.73001032$ | $-0.07581056,\ -0.09360017$ |
| $\delta^{(1)}$ | $-0.11161327,\ 0.10138758$ | $-0.01431079,\ 0.01299967$ |

**The common factor.** Every ratio is identical to eight decimals:

$$
\frac{\delta^{(2)}_{\sigma}}{\delta^{(2)}_{\text{lin}}}
= \frac{\partial L/\partial W^{(2)}_1|_\sigma}{\partial L/\partial W^{(2)}_1|_{\text{lin}}}
= \frac{\partial L/\partial W^{(1)}_{11}|_\sigma}{\partial L/\partial W^{(1)}_{11}|_{\text{lin}}}
= 0.12821759
$$

**Where it comes from.** It is not $\sigma'(z^{(2)})$ alone, because the head
also changed the prediction and therefore the loss. Decomposing:

$$
z^{(2)} = 0.05011480, \qquad \sigma(z^{(2)}) = 0.51252608, \qquad \sigma'(z^{(2)}) = 0.24984310
$$

$$
\frac{(\sigma(z^{(2)}) - y)\,\sigma'(z^{(2)})}{z^{(2)} - y}
= \frac{(-0.48747392)(0.24984310)}{-0.94988520} = 0.12821759
$$

Two effects: the sigmoid moved the prediction closer to the target, shrinking
the residual from $-0.94988520$ to $-0.48747392$, and then contributed its own
slope of $0.24984310$. The product of the two is the factor.

**Why the factor is uniform.** By (I.6.2) each adjoint is the one above it times
a local Jacobian, and the new node is above all of them. A common factor at the
sink propagates unchanged through every subsequent multiplication — which is
also why scaling a loss by a constant scales every gradient by that constant,
and why loss scaling in mixed-precision training is sound rather than a
heuristic.

**What it costs.** The slope $0.24984310$ is near the sigmoid's ceiling of
$0.25$, because $z^{(2)}$ happens to be near zero. Had the head been confident —
$z^{(2)} = 6$, say — the slope would be $0.0025$ and every gradient in the
network would be four hundred times smaller. That is the saturation term of
I.6.B04 arriving at the output layer instead of in the middle, and it is the
reason a sigmoid head is paired with cross-entropy rather than squared error:
I.6.X02 shows the pairing that cancels it.
