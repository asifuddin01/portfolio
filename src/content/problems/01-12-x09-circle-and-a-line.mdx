The data are the unit circle in the plane, and the code has width one.

(a) Prove that every continuous encoder gives some pair of antipodal
points the same code.

(b) Deduce that, whatever the decoder, some point of the circle is
reconstructed at distance at least $1$ from itself.

(c) Encoding a point by its angle in $(-\pi,\pi]$ and decoding $z$ to
$(\cos z,\sin z)$ reconstructs every point exactly. Why does this not
contradict (b)? For the inputs at $+175^\circ$ and $-175^\circ$, find the
distance between them, the distance between their codes, and the distance
from either to the decoding of the code halfway between.

(d) How wide a code allows an autoencoder for the circle that is both
continuous and exact?

<Hint>
Write the encoder as a function $F$ of the angle and study
$h(\theta)=F(\theta)-F(\theta+\pi)$.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**(a)** Let $F(\theta)$ be the code of the point at angle $\theta$. It is
continuous, and $F(\theta+2\pi)=F(\theta)$ because the two angles name one
point. Define $h(\theta)=F(\theta)-F(\theta+\pi)$. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
h(\theta+\pi)&=F(\theta+\pi)-F(\theta+2\pi)\\
 &=-h(\theta).
\end{aligned}
$$

If $h(0)=0$ the points at angles $0$ and $\pi$ share a code. Otherwise
$h(0)$ and $h(\pi)$ have opposite signs, and by the intermediate value
theorem $h(\theta^\star)=0$ for some $\theta^\star$ between them. The
points at $\theta^\star$ and $\theta^\star+\pi$ are antipodal and share a
code. $\blacksquare$

**(b)** Call those points $\vec p$ and $-\vec p$. A decoder sees one code,
so it returns one point $\vec a$ for both. By the triangle inequality,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\|\vec a-\vec p\|+\|\vec a+\vec p\|\\
&\;\;\geq\|(\vec a+\vec p)-(\vec a-\vec p)\|\\
&\;\;=2\|\vec p\|=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

The larger of the two distances is at least $1$. $\blacksquare$

**(c)** The angle is not a continuous function on the circle. Crossing
the negative horizontal axis, it jumps from $\pi$ to $-\pi$. Part (a)
needed continuity and does not apply.

The two inputs are $10^\circ$ apart on the circle, at distance
$2\sin5^\circ=0.174311$. Their codes are $\pm3.054326$, which are
$6.108652$ apart. The code halfway between is $0$, which decodes to
$(1,0)$, the point $175^\circ$ around from either input and at distance
$2\sin87.5^\circ=1.998096$ from it.

The exact autoencoder has put two neighbours at opposite ends of its code
and the far side of the circle between them.

**(d)** Width two. Take the encoder and the decoder to be the identity on
the plane. Both are continuous and every point of the circle is
reconstructed exactly.

The circle has one degree of freedom and needs two code numbers. A closed
curve cannot be laid along a line without cutting it somewhere, and (a)
and (b) are that fact stated for encoders. The width a code needs depends
on the shape of the data as well as on its dimension.

As a check, the script takes
$F(\theta)=\cos\theta+0.3\sin2\theta+0.2\sin\theta$, for which
$h(\theta)=2\cos\theta+0.4\sin\theta$, and finds the shared code by
bisection at $\theta^\star=\pi-\arctan5=1.768192$.

</SolutionToggle>
