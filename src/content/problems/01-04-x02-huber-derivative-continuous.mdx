Show that $\mathrm{Huber}_\delta$ is continuous and has a continuous derivative
at $|r| = \delta$. Then show that removing the $-\tfrac12\delta$ term — using
$\delta|r|$ on the outer branch — destroys continuity of the *value* while
leaving the derivative continuous, and say why that is the worse of the two
failures.

<Hint>
Evaluate both branches, and both their derivatives, at $r = \delta$ exactly.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**Continuity of the value.** At $r = \delta$, approaching from inside:

$$\tfrac12 r^2 \big|_{r=\delta} = \tfrac12\delta^2$$

and from outside:

$$\delta\!\left(r - \tfrac12\delta\right)\Big|_{r=\delta} = \delta\!\left(\delta - \tfrac12\delta\right) = \delta \cdot \tfrac12\delta = \tfrac12\delta^2$$

Equal, so the function is continuous. **The $-\tfrac12\delta$ is precisely the
offset that makes the two branches meet.**

**Continuity of the derivative.** Differentiating each branch:

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}\,\tfrac12 r^2 = r
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\delta \text{ at } r = \delta
$$

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}\,\delta\!\left(r - \tfrac12\delta\right) = \delta
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\delta \text{ everywhere on that branch}
$$

Both give $\delta$. So $\mathrm{Huber}_\delta \in C^{1}$: value and slope both
match, and the function has no kink. It is not $C^2$ — the second derivative
jumps from $1$ to $0$ — but $C^1$ is what a first-order optimiser needs.

**Removing the offset.** Define $\tilde{H}(r) = \tfrac12 r^2$ for $|r| \le \delta$
and $\delta|r|$ beyond. Its derivative on the outer branch is still $\delta$, so
the *derivative* is still continuous. But the value jumps:

$$
\lim_{r\to\delta^{-}}\tilde{H} = \tfrac12\delta^{2},
\qquad
\lim_{r\to\delta^{+}}\tilde{H} = \delta^{2}
$$

a discontinuity of size $\tfrac12\delta^2$.

**Why the value discontinuity is worse than a derivative one.** This is the part
worth thinking about, because the naive ranking is the other way round.

A discontinuous *derivative* — a kink, as MAE has at zero — is survivable. The
subgradient convention of I.2.X09 covers it, the set of points where it matters
has measure zero, and every ReLU network already lives with it.

A discontinuous *value* is not survivable, for a reason that has nothing to do
with differentiability. The reported loss becomes uninterpretable: two models
whose residuals differ infinitesimally, one just inside $\delta$ and one just
outside, report losses differing by $\tfrac12\delta^2$. Loss curves acquire jumps
that look like instability and are not. Comparisons between runs with different
$\delta$ are meaningless. And any early-stopping or model-selection rule reading
the loss inherits the artefact.

Worse, gradient descent does not even notice: the gradients are identical to
Huber's, so the optimisation proceeds normally while the number reported about it
is wrong. **A bug that changes the metric but not the training is harder to find
than one that breaks training**, because nothing fails.

**The general form.** Whenever a piecewise loss is defined, check the value and
the derivative at every join, separately. Continuity of one does not imply the
other, and they fail in different ways.
</SolutionToggle>
