In an eigenbasis, the averaged least-squares normal equations have diagonal
curvatures $a=(1,4)$ and right-hand side $b=(2,8)$. Compute the unregularised
solution and the ridge solution at $\lambda=1$. Derive the shrinkage factor.
What coefficient on the penalty preserves the objective's relative weighting
if the data loss is multiplied by the sample count $n$?

<Hint>
Solve one scalar equation per direction. Multiplying the whole objective by
$n$ does not change its minimiser.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

Without a penalty, $w_i=b_i/a_i$, so $w=(2,2)$.
Ridge changes the equations to $(a_i+\lambda)w_i=b_i$, giving

$$
w_{\mathrm{ridge}}=(1,8/5)=(1,1.6).
$$

The ratios relative to the original components are $1/2$ and $4/5$.
In general the factor is $a_i/(a_i+\lambda)$. The smaller-curvature
direction is shrunk more because the penalty contributes more relative
curvature there.

An averaged objective is
$\|Xw-y\|^2/(2n)+\lambda\|w\|^2/2$.
Multiplying the whole objective by $n$ gives
$\|Xw-y\|^2/2+n\lambda\|w\|^2/2$.
Thus the corresponding summed-loss coefficient is $n\lambda$,
not $\lambda/n$.

For positive lambda the solution is unique even if a curvature is zero.
With least squares, a null-space direction also has zero right-hand component;
ridge selects zero in that direction. This says nothing by itself about the
test error of the chosen solution.

</SolutionToggle>
