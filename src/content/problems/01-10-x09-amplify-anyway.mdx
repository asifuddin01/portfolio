A scalar LSTM has constant gates $f=i=1/2$ and $o=3/4$, zero input, and a
candidate $\tilde c_t=\tanh(3h_{t-1})$. The state $c=h=0$ is a fixed point.
Linearise around it, find the factor by which a small perturbation of $c$
grows per step, and estimate a perturbation of $10^{-6}$ after twenty steps.
Does gating remove the need for gradient clipping?

<Hint>
Near zero, $\tanh u\approx u$. Then $h=\tfrac34\tanh c\approx\tfrac34c$.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

Near the fixed point, $h_{t-1}\approx\tfrac34c_{t-1}$ and
$\tilde c_t\approx3h_{t-1}$, so

$$
c_t\approx\tfrac12c_{t-1}+\tfrac12\cdot3\cdot\tfrac34c_{t-1}=1.625\,c_{t-1}.
$$

The carry line contributes $0.5$; the path through $h_{t-1}$ and the candidate
contributes $1.125$. A perturbation of $10^{-6}$ grows to about
$10^{-6}\cdot1.625^{20}=0.016484$ after twenty steps; the script's nonlinear
run gives $0.016480$, slightly less because tanh begins to bend.

The same factor multiplies the backward signal, so the gradient through this
cell grows like $1.625^n$ while the trajectory stays near the origin.
A forget gate below one does not bound the total Jacobian. Clipping (I.9.7)
remains a standard part of training gated networks.

</SolutionToggle>
