Let $A=\operatorname{diag}(2,1/4)$ and
$B=\operatorname{diag}(1/4,2)$. Compute their spectral norms and the norm
of the two-step product $BA$. What happens after $n$ repetitions of this
pair? Explain why the upper bound in (I.9.6) is not an equality.

<Hint>
Each diagonal matrix expands a different coordinate.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

Both factor norms are two, since the spectral norm of a real diagonal
matrix is its largest absolute diagonal entry.
But

$$
BA=\operatorname{diag}(1/2,1/2)=\tfrac12 I.
$$

The actual product norm is $1/2$, while the product of the factor norms
is four. After $n$ pairs, every vector is multiplied by $2^{-n}$.
There is uniform contraction despite every individual factor having
norm above one.

The first factor's most expanded direction is contracted by the second.
Multiplying norms forgets this alignment and gives an upper bound.
A bound that permits growth does not prove growth.

These two diagonal matrices are an abstract Jacobian counterexample.
It is enough to refute an inference made solely from their norms; no claim
is made that every tanh trajectory realises this exact pair.

</SolutionToggle>
