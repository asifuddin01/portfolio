The best width-one linear autoencoder for the four points $(1,2)$,
$(2.2,0.4)$, $(-1,-2)$ and $(-2.2,-0.4)$ projects onto
$\vec u_1=(0.8,0.6)$. Its squared reconstruction error is used as an
anomaly score, and any input scoring above $1.5$ is flagged.

(a) Find the scores of the four training points.

(b) Find the scores of $(8,6)$ and of $(-1.2,1.6)$, and the distance from
each to its nearest training point.

(c) Which of the two is flagged?

(d) What does the score measure, and what would be needed to flag the
other one?

<Hint>
Write each new point in the eigenvectors $\vec u_1=(0.8,0.6)$ and
$\vec u_2=(-0.6,0.8)$. The score is the square of the $\vec u_2$ component.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**(a)** Each training point has a component of $\pm1$ along $\vec u_2$, so
every score is $1$. The threshold of $1.5$ passes all of them.

**(b)** The point $(8,6)$ is $10\,\vec u_1$. It lies on the principal axis
and its score is $0$. Its nearest training points are $(1,2)$ and
$(2.2,0.4)$, each at distance $\sqrt{65}=8.062258$.

The point $(-1.2,1.6)$ is $2\,\vec u_2$. Its score is $4$. Its nearest
training points are $(1,2)$ and $(-2.2,-0.4)$, each at distance
$\sqrt5=2.236068$.

**(c)** Only $(-1.2,1.6)$ is flagged. The point $(8,6)$ passes with the
lowest score of any input seen so far, lower than every training point,
though it is more than three times as far from the data as the point that
was flagged.

**(d)** The score is the squared distance from the reconstruction surface,
here a line. It says nothing about where along the line an input falls.
The line extends without end, and the data occupy a short stretch of it.

To flag $(8,6)$ one must know which codes are usual. Its code is $10$,
where the training codes are $\pm2$. Reconstruction error does not look at
the code, and (I.12.1) defines no distribution over codes against which
$10$ could be judged improbable. A threshold on the size of the code would
catch this case and would be a second detector, added by hand.

The failure grows with the quality of the model. A decoder that
generalises well reconstructs inputs it was never shown, and each of them
scores as normal.

</SolutionToggle>
