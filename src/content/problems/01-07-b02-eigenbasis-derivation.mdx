<Statement>
Derive, from the update rule alone, the exact condition under which gradient
descent converges on a quadratic, the learning rate that converges fastest, and
the rate it achieves. The numbers of I.7.B01 should fall out as one instance.
</Statement>

<Given>
$\mathcal{L}(\vec{\theta}) = \tfrac{1}{2}(\vec{\theta} - \vec{\theta}^*)^{\mathsf{T}}\mat{H}(\vec{\theta} - \vec{\theta}^*)$
with $\mat{H}$ symmetric positive definite, eigenvalues
$0 < \lambda_{\min} = \lambda_1 \le \dots \le \lambda_n = \lambda_{\max}$.
</Given>

<Find>
The error recurrence; the condition on $\eta$ for convergence; the $\eta$
minimising the worst-case per-step contraction; and that contraction in terms of
$\kappa = \lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}$.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Change coordinates so the problem falls apart. $\mat{H}$ is symmetric, so it has
an orthonormal eigenbasis; written in that basis the update decouples into $n$
independent scalar recurrences, and a scalar geometric sequence is something
whose convergence can simply be read off.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**Step 1 — the error recurrence.** The gradient is
$\nabla\mathcal{L} = \mat{H}(\vec{\theta} - \vec{\theta}^*)$. Writing
$\vec{e}_t = \vec{\theta}_t - \vec{\theta}^*$ and substituting into (I.7.1):

$$
\vec{e}_{t+1} = \vec{e}_t - \eta\mat{H}\vec{e}_t = (\mat{I} - \eta\mat{H})\vec{e}_t
$$

so after $t$ steps $\vec{e}_t = (\mat{I} - \eta\mat{H})^t \vec{e}_0$. The whole
trajectory is one matrix raised to a power.

**Step 2 — decouple.** $\mat{H} = \mat{Q}\Lambda\mat{Q}^{\mathsf{T}}$ with
$\mat{Q}$ orthogonal. Since $\mat{I} - \eta\mat{H} = \mat{Q}(\mat{I} - \eta\Lambda)\mat{Q}^{\mathsf{T}}$
and $\mat{Q}^{\mathsf{T}}\mat{Q} = \mat{I}$, powers pass straight through:

$$
(\mat{I} - \eta\mat{H})^t = \mat{Q}(\mat{I} - \eta\Lambda)^t\mat{Q}^{\mathsf{T}}
$$

In the rotated coordinates $\tilde{\vec{e}} = \mat{Q}^{\mathsf{T}}\vec{e}$ the
recurrence is $n$ separate scalar ones:

$$
\tilde{e}_{i,t} = (1 - \eta\lambda_i)^t\,\tilde{e}_{i,0}
$$

The directions do not interact. This is why one badly served direction cannot be
rescued by another going well.

**Step 3 — convergence.** $(1 - \eta\lambda_i)^t \to 0$ exactly when
$|1 - \eta\lambda_i| < 1$, that is $0 < \eta < 2/\lambda_i$. Every direction must
converge, so the smallest of those bounds binds:

$$
0 < \eta < \frac{2}{\lambda_{\max}}
$$

The condition is necessary as well as sufficient — above it the component along
$\vec{q}_{\max}$ grows geometrically, and no other direction can offset that.

**Step 4 — the fastest rate.** Asymptotically the slowest-decaying component
dominates, so the quantity to minimise is

$$
\rho(\eta) = \max_i |1 - \eta\lambda_i|
$$

Each $|1 - \eta\lambda_i|$ is a V-shaped function of $\eta$ with its minimum at
$1/\lambda_i$. The maximum over $i$ is attained at one of the two extreme
eigenvalues: for small $\eta$ by $1 - \eta\lambda_{\min}$, which decreases, and
for large $\eta$ by $\eta\lambda_{\max} - 1$, which increases. The minimum of the
maximum is where they cross:

$$
1 - \eta\lambda_{\min} = \eta\lambda_{\max} - 1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\eta^* = \frac{2}{\lambda_{\max} + \lambda_{\min}}
$$

**Step 5 — the rate.** Substituting back:

$$
\rho(\eta^*) = 1 - \frac{2\lambda_{\min}}{\lambda_{\max} + \lambda_{\min}}
= \frac{\lambda_{\max} - \lambda_{\min}}{\lambda_{\max} + \lambda_{\min}}
= \frac{\kappa - 1}{\kappa + 1}
$$

which is (I.7.4). Dividing through by $\lambda_{\min}$ is what turns two
eigenvalues into one ratio: only the *shape* of the problem matters, not its
scale.

**Step 6 — check against I.7.B01.** With $\lambda = (20, 0.2)$:
$\eta^* = 2/20.2 = 0.0990099$ and $\rho = 99/101 = 0.980198$, matching the
measured values exactly.

</Solution>

<Answer>

$$
\vec{e}_{t} = (\mat{I} - \eta\mat{H})^{t}\vec{e}_0,
\qquad \text{converges} \iff 0 < \eta < \frac{2}{\lambda_{\max}}
$$

$$
\eta^* = \frac{2}{\lambda_{\max} + \lambda_{\min}},
\qquad \rho(\eta^*) = \frac{\kappa - 1}{\kappa + 1}
$$

</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
**$\kappa = 1$ gives $\rho = 0$.** A perfectly conditioned quadratic is solved in
a single step, and the formula says so: $\eta^* = 1/\lambda$ lands exactly on the
optimum. Any correct derivation must reproduce this.

**$\kappa \to \infty$ gives $\rho \to 1$.** Progress per step vanishes as the
problem stiffens, which is the behaviour to expect and the reason the ratio
rather than either eigenvalue is the quantity of interest.

**$\eta^*$ lies strictly inside the stability interval.**
$2/(\lambda_{\max} + \lambda_{\min}) < 2/\lambda_{\max}$ since
$\lambda_{\min} > 0$. The optimum is never on the boundary, which is worth
knowing: a rate tuned to the edge of stability is not the fast one.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
Every step used symmetry and positive definiteness. A real loss Hessian is
symmetric but is *not* positive definite away from a minimum: at a saddle it has
negative eigenvalues, and for $\lambda_i < 0$ the multiplier $1 - \eta\lambda_i$
exceeds one for every positive $\eta$. Gradient descent moves *away* from a
saddle along those directions, which is the behaviour wanted, but it means the
analysis above describes only the neighbourhood of a minimum and says nothing
about the journey there.

The asymptotic framing hides something practical too. $\rho$ governs the
long-run rate, and training runs are not long-run: they are stopped after a
budget. The transient matters, which is why $\eta^*$ was beaten at 100 steps by
a smaller rate in I.7.B01 despite being optimal in the limit.

Momentum breaks the derivation rather than extending it. Its update depends on
two previous iterates, so the recurrence is second-order and the analysis needs
the roots of a quadratic characteristic polynomial rather than a single
multiplier. That is what produces $\sqrt{\kappa}$ in place of $\kappa$ — I.7.T2,
and the subject of I.7.B03.
</Breaks>

<Variation>
Repeat Step 4 for the *transient* rather than the asymptote: which $\eta$
minimises $\|\vec{e}_T\|$ for a fixed budget $T$ and a given $\vec{e}_0$? Show
that it depends on $\vec{e}_0$, and say what that implies about tuning a
learning rate on a short run.
</Variation>
