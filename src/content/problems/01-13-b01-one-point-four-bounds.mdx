<Statement>
Compute the log-evidence of one point under probabilistic PCA, then the
lower bound that four different encoder distributions give it, and confirm
that each gap is a KL divergence.
</Statement>

<Given>
The four points of I.12 have covariance eigenvalues $4$ and $1$ along
$\vec u_1=(0.8,0.6)$ and $\vec u_2=(-0.6,0.8)$. The model has one latent
dimension, $z\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$ and
$\vec x\mid z\sim\mathcal N(\vec wz,\sigma^2\mat I)$, at its maximum
likelihood $\sigma^2=1$ and $\vec w=\sqrt3\,\vec u_1$. Take
$\vec x=(1,2)$. The four candidate encoder distributions are
$\mathcal N(0,1)$, $\mathcal N(0,\tfrac14)$, $\mathcal N(\tfrac{\sqrt3}2,1)$
and $\mathcal N(\tfrac{\sqrt3}2,\tfrac14)$.
</Given>

<Find>
$\log p(\vec x)$; the posterior of $z$; the ELBO for each candidate; the
gaps; and the two terms of the ELBO at the posterior.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Use (I.13.2) for the evidence and the posterior, written in the
eigenbasis. For the ELBO, both terms of (I.13.4) have closed forms for a
Gaussian $q$ with mean $m$ and variance $v$.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**The evidence.** The point has coordinates $\vec u_1^{\mathsf T}\vec x=2$
and $\vec u_2^{\mathsf T}\vec x=1$. The model's covariance
$\vec w\vec w^{\mathsf T}+\mat I$ has eigenvalues $4$ along $\vec u_1$ and
$1$ along $\vec u_2$, so its determinant is $4$ and

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\log p(\vec x)\\
&\;=-\log2\pi-\tfrac12\log4\\
&\;\quad-\tfrac12\Big(\frac{2^2}{4}+\frac{1^2}{1}\Big)\\
&\;=-\log2\pi-\log2-1\\
&\;=-3.531024.
\end{aligned}
$$

**The posterior.** With $\|\vec w\|^2=3$ and
$\vec w^{\mathsf T}\vec x=2\sqrt3$, (I.13.2) gives variance
$\sigma^2/(\|\vec w\|^2+\sigma^2)=1/4$ and mean $2\sqrt3/4=0.866025$.

**The bounds.** For $q=\mathcal N(m,v)$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\E_q\|\vec x-\vec wz\|^2\\
&\;=\|\vec x\|^2-2m\,\vec w^{\mathsf T}\vec x\\
&\;\quad+(m^2+v)\|\vec w\|^2\\
&\;=5-4\sqrt3\,m+3(m^2+v),
\end{aligned}
$$

and the reconstruction term is $-\log2\pi$ minus half of that. The price
of the code is $\tfrac12(m^2+v-1-\log v)$ by (I.13.5). For the prior,
$m=0$ and $v=1$: the squared error averages to $8$, the reconstruction
term is $-\log2\pi-4$, the price is $0$, and

$$
\operatorname{ELBO}=-5.837877.
$$

The other three, computed the same way, are $-5.031024$, $-4.337877$ and
$-3.531024$.

**The gaps.** Subtracting from $\log p(\vec x)$ gives $2.306853$, $1.5$,
$0.806853$ and $0$. By (I.13.4) each must equal the KL divergence from
$\mathcal N(m,v)$ to the posterior $\mathcal N(0.866025,0.25)$. For the
prior,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\tfrac12\Big(\frac{1}{0.25}+\frac{0.75}{0.25}\\
&\qquad-1+\log0.25\Big)\\
&\;\;=\tfrac12(4+3-1-1.386294)\\
&\;\;=2.306853.
\end{aligned}
$$

**At the posterior.** The squared error averages to
$5-6+3=2$, so the reconstruction term is $-\log2\pi-1=-2.837877$. The price
is $\tfrac12(0.75+0.25-1-\log0.25)=\log2=0.693147$. Their difference is
the evidence.

</Solution>

<Answer>
$\log p(\vec x)=-3.531024$; posterior $\mathcal N(0.866025,0.25)$; ELBOs
$-5.837877$, $-5.031024$, $-4.337877$, $-3.531024$; gaps $2.306853$, $1.5$,
$0.806853$, $0$; at the posterior, reconstruction $-2.837877$ and price
$0.693147$.
</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
Every bound is below the evidence and the gaps are non-negative. The
correct width with mean $0$ costs $1.5$, which is
$\tfrac12(0.866025^2)/0.25$: with equal variances, the KL is the squared
distance between the means over twice the variance.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
With a nonlinear decoder neither the evidence nor the posterior has a
closed form, and only the ELBO can be computed. The gaps above can be
seen only because this model is linear.
</Breaks>

<Variation>
Every corner of the rectangle has $|\vec u_1^{\mathsf T}\vec x|=2$ and
$|\vec u_2^{\mathsf T}\vec x|=1$, so all four have the same evidence, and
the posterior means are $\pm0.866025$. The average log-likelihood over the
dataset is therefore also $-3.531024$.
</Variation>
