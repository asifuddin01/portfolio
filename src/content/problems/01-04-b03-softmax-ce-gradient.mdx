<Statement>
Derive $\partial L/\partial \vec{z} = \vec{p} - \vec{y}$ for softmax followed by
cross-entropy. Do not quote the result: obtain the softmax Jacobian from the
quotient rule, apply the chain rule through it in full, and show explicitly
which terms cancel and why. Then instantiate the whole calculation on three
logits.
</Statement>

<Given>
Logits $\vec{z} \in \R^{K}$, predictions and target

$$
p_i = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_{j=1}^{K} e^{z_j}}, \qquad
L = -\sum_{k=1}^{K} y_k \log p_k, \qquad
\vec{y}\ \text{one-hot at class } c
$$

For the numeric part, $\vec{z} = (2, 1, 0)$ and $c = 1$ (the first entry).
</Given>

<Find>
$\partial p_i / \partial z_j$ for all $i, j$; then $\partial L/\partial z_j$;
then both evaluated at the given logits.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Differentiate softmax by the quotient rule, splitting into the $i = j$ and
$i \neq j$ cases because the numerator depends on $z_j$ only in the first. Then
push the loss gradient through the Jacobian and use $\sum_k y_k = 1$ — that one
identity is what collapses a $K \times K$ matrix product to a subtraction.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

### Part 1 — the softmax Jacobian

**Step 1 — name the denominator.** Let

$$S = \sum_{j=1}^{K} e^{z_j}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad p_i = \frac{e^{z_i}}{S}$$

The key observation before any differentiation: **$S$ depends on every logit**.
So $p_i$ depends on $z_j$ even when $i \neq j$ — through the denominator alone.
That is the whole source of the off-diagonal terms.

Differentiating $S$:

$$\frac{\partial S}{\partial z_j} = \frac{\partial}{\partial z_j}\sum_{m} e^{z_m} = e^{z_j}$$

since every term but the $j$-th is constant in $z_j$.

**Step 2 — the diagonal case, $i = j$.** Apply the quotient rule
$\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)' = \frac{u'v - uv'}{v^2}$ with $u = e^{z_i}$ and
$v = S$. Here $u' = e^{z_i}$ and $v' = e^{z_i}$:

$$
\frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_i}
= \frac{e^{z_i}\,S - e^{z_i}\,e^{z_i}}{S^{2}}
$$

Split the fraction into two, so each piece becomes a $p$:

$$
= \frac{e^{z_i}S}{S^{2}} - \frac{e^{z_i}e^{z_i}}{S^{2}}
= \frac{e^{z_i}}{S} - \frac{e^{z_i}}{S}\cdot\frac{e^{z_i}}{S}
= p_i - p_i^{2} = p_i(1 - p_i)
$$

**Step 3 — the off-diagonal case, $i \neq j$.** Now $u = e^{z_i}$ does **not**
depend on $z_j$, so $u' = 0$ and only the denominator contributes:

$$
\frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_j}
= \frac{0\cdot S - e^{z_i}e^{z_j}}{S^{2}}
= -\frac{e^{z_i}}{S}\cdot\frac{e^{z_j}}{S}
= -p_i p_j
$$

**Step 4 — combine.** Using the Kronecker delta
$\delta_{ij} = 1$ if $i=j$ and $0$ otherwise, the two cases are one formula:

$$
\frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_j} = p_i\left(\delta_{ij} - p_j\right)
\tag{0.MC.06}
$$

Check it reproduces both branches: at $i=j$, $p_i(1 - p_i)$ ✓; at $i \ne j$,
$p_i(0 - p_j) = -p_ip_j$ ✓.

### Part 2 — the chain rule through it

**Step 5 — differentiate the loss with respect to the probabilities.**

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial p_i} = \frac{\partial}{\partial p_i}\left(-\sum_k y_k \log p_k\right) = -\frac{y_i}{p_i}$$

only the $k = i$ term surviving.

**Step 6 — assemble.** The chain rule for a vector-to-vector map sums over the
intermediate index (<ApparatusRef ref="0.MC.03" />):

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial z_j}
= \sum_{i=1}^{K} \frac{\partial L}{\partial p_i}\,\frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_j}
= \sum_{i=1}^{K} \left(-\frac{y_i}{p_i}\right) p_i\left(\delta_{ij} - p_j\right)
$$

**Step 7 — the first cancellation.** The factor $p_i$ from the Jacobian meets
the $1/p_i$ from the loss and they cancel exactly:

$$
= -\sum_{i=1}^{K} y_i\left(\delta_{ij} - p_j\right)
$$

This is the step that makes the whole thing work, and it is why softmax and
cross-entropy are paired rather than chosen independently. Had the loss been
anything but a logarithm, the $1/p_i$ would not have appeared and nothing would
cancel — which is exactly what problem I.4.B06 shows happening with MSE.

**Step 8 — expand the bracket and use $\sum_i y_i = 1$.**

$$
= -\sum_{i} y_i \delta_{ij} + \sum_{i} y_i p_j
$$

The first sum has exactly one non-zero term, at $i = j$, giving $y_j$. In the
second, $p_j$ does not depend on $i$, so it factors out:

$$
= -y_j + p_j \underbrace{\sum_{i} y_i}_{=\,1} = p_j - y_j
$$

$$
\boxed{\ \frac{\partial L}{\partial \vec{z}} = \vec{p} - \vec{y}\ }
\tag{I.4.4}
$$

$\blacksquare$

**Where each hypothesis was used.** Step 7 needed the loss to be logarithmic.
Step 8 needed $\vec{y}$ to sum to one — note it did **not** need $\vec{y}$ to be
one-hot, so the result holds for soft targets and therefore for label smoothing
unchanged. That is worth recording, because it is often stated as requiring
one-hot labels and does not.

### Part 3 — the numbers

**Step 9 — softmax at $\vec{z} = (2,1,0)$.** Subtract the maximum first, which
changes nothing and prevents overflow (<ApparatusRef ref="0.NU.02" />):

$$
e^{2-2} = 1.000000, \qquad
e^{1-2} = 0.367879, \qquad
e^{0-2} = 0.135335
$$

$$S = 1.000000 + 0.367879 + 0.135335 = 1.503215$$

$$
p_1 = \frac{1.000000}{1.503215} = 0.6652, \quad
p_2 = \frac{0.367879}{1.503215} = 0.2447, \quad
p_3 = \frac{0.135335}{1.503215} = 0.0900
$$

Sum: $0.6652 + 0.2447 + 0.0900 = 0.9999 \approx 1$ ✓ (the last digit is
rounding).

**Step 10 — the loss.** $\vec{y} = (1,0,0)$, so

$$L = -\log p_1 = -\log 0.6652 = 0.4076 \text{ nats}$$

**Step 11 — the full Jacobian, entry by entry.** Using
$p_i(\delta_{ij} - p_j)$:

$$
\frac{\partial p_1}{\partial z_1} = 0.6652(1 - 0.6652) = 0.6652 \times 0.3348 = 0.2227
$$
$$
\frac{\partial p_1}{\partial z_2} = -0.6652 \times 0.2447 = -0.1628
$$
$$
\frac{\partial p_2}{\partial z_2} = 0.2447(1 - 0.2447) = 0.2447 \times 0.7553 = 0.1848
$$

and so on, giving

$$
\mat{J} =
\begin{bmatrix}
+0.2227 & -0.1628 & -0.0599\\
-0.1628 & +0.1848 & -0.0220\\
-0.0599 & -0.0220 & +0.0819
\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Step 12 — the long route, to confirm the short one.**
$\partial L/\partial \vec{p} = (-1/0.6652,\ 0,\ 0) = (-1.5032,\ 0,\ 0)$.
Multiplying by the Jacobian, only the first row contributes:

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial z_1} = (-1.5032)(0.2227) = -0.3348
$$
$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial z_2} = (-1.5032)(-0.1628) = +0.2447
$$
$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial z_3} = (-1.5032)(-0.0599) = +0.0900
$$

**Step 13 — the short route.**

$$
\vec{p} - \vec{y} = (0.6652 - 1,\ 0.2447 - 0,\ 0.0900 - 0) = (-0.3348,\ +0.2447,\ +0.0900)
$$

Identical to Step 12, entry for entry.

</Solution>

<Answer>
$$
\frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_j} = p_i(\delta_{ij} - p_j),
\qquad
\frac{\partial L}{\partial \vec{z}} = \vec{p} - \vec{y}
$$

At $\vec{z} = (2,1,0)$ with the true class first:
$\vec{p} = (0.6652, 0.2447, 0.0900)$, $L = 0.4076$ nats, and

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial \vec{z}} = (-0.3348,\ +0.2447,\ +0.0900)$$

a dimensionless vector of length $K$, summing to zero.
</Answer>


</CheckNumeric>

<CheckSanity>
**The gradient sums to zero.** $-0.3348 + 0.2447 + 0.0900 = -0.0001 \approx 0$.
This must hold: softmax is invariant to adding a constant $\lambda$ to every
logit, so the directional derivative along $(1,1,\dots,1)$ is zero, which is
exactly $\sum_j \partial L/\partial z_j = 0$. A gradient not summing to zero
means an arithmetic slip.

**The signs are right.** The true class has a negative gradient, so gradient
descent *raises* its logit; every other class has a positive gradient, so their
logits are lowered. That is the behaviour the loss should produce, read directly
off the sign pattern.

**The magnitudes are bounded.** Every entry of $\vec{p} - \vec{y}$ lies in
$[-1, 1]$, since $p_j \in (0,1)$ and $y_j \in \{0,1\}$. **The gradient can never
explode**, whatever the logits. Contrast with the MSE-through-sigmoid gradient of
I.4.B06, which is bounded too — but by a number that shrinks to zero exactly when
it is needed.

**The Jacobian is symmetric with zero row sums.** $J_{12} = J_{21} = -0.1628$,
and $0.2227 - 0.1628 - 0.0599 = 0.0000$. Both properties follow from the formula:
$p_i(\delta_{ij} - p_j)$ is symmetric because $p_ip_j$ is, and rows sum to
$p_i(1 - \sum_j p_j) = p_i(1-1) = 0$.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
The clean result needs softmax and cross-entropy to be **fused**. Computing
$\vec{p}$ first, storing it, and then computing $-\log p_c$ gives the same number
but a worse computation: when $p_c$ underflows to $0$ the logarithm is $-\infty$,
and the $1/p_i$ in Step 5 is a division by zero even though the *final* answer
$\vec{p}-\vec{y}$ is perfectly well behaved.

This is why every framework has a single `cross_entropy(logits, target)` rather
than a softmax followed by a log — the fused version computes
$z_c - \log\sum_j e^{z_j}$ directly via log-sum-exp and never forms the
intermediate that overflows. The mathematics is identical; the arithmetic is not,
and I.4.B04 makes the same point for the binary case in detail.
</Breaks>
