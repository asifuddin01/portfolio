<Statement>
Derive $\sigma'(z) = \sigma(z)\,(1 - \sigma(z))$ and
$\tanh'(z) = 1 - \tanh^2(z)$ from the definitions, then show
$\tanh(z) = 2\sigma(2z) - 1$. Say in each case what the identity buys.
</Statement>

<Given>
$\sigma(z) = (1 + e^{-z})^{-1}$ and
$\tanh(z) = (e^{z} - e^{-z})/(e^{z} + e^{-z})$.
</Given>

<Find>
The two derivative identities and the relation between the functions.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Differentiate by the chain rule, then rewrite the result in terms of the
function itself rather than of $z$ — that rewriting is the entire content of
each identity.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**Step 1 — differentiate sigmoid.** Write $\sigma = u^{-1}$ with
$u = 1 + e^{-z}$, so $u' = -e^{-z}$. By the chain rule
(<ApparatusRef ref="0.MC.03" />):

$$\sigma'(z) = -u^{-2}\,u' = \frac{e^{-z}}{(1+e^{-z})^{2}}$$

**Step 2 — rewrite in terms of $\sigma$.** Split the fraction deliberately:

$$
\frac{e^{-z}}{(1+e^{-z})^{2}}
= \frac{1}{1+e^{-z}} \cdot \frac{e^{-z}}{1+e^{-z}}
= \sigma(z) \cdot \frac{e^{-z}}{1+e^{-z}}
$$

For the second factor, add and subtract $1$ in the numerator:

$$\frac{e^{-z}}{1+e^{-z}} = \frac{(1+e^{-z}) - 1}{1+e^{-z}} = 1 - \sigma(z)$$

$$\boxed{\ \sigma'(z) = \sigma(z)\big(1 - \sigma(z)\big)\ } \tag{I.3.1}$$

*What it buys.* The derivative costs nothing extra at run time. The forward pass
already computed $\sigma(z)$; the backward pass needs only one multiply and one
subtract, and never touches $e^{-z}$ again. That is why frameworks cache
activations rather than pre-activations for these units.

**Step 3 — differentiate tanh.** Quotient rule on
$\tanh = (e^{z}-e^{-z})/(e^{z}+e^{-z})$. Writing $N$ and $D$ for numerator and
denominator, $N' = D$ and $D' = N$, so

$$\tanh'(z) = \frac{N'D - ND'}{D^{2}} = \frac{D^{2} - N^{2}}{D^{2}} = 1 - \left(\frac{N}{D}\right)^{2}$$

$$\boxed{\ \tanh'(z) = 1 - \tanh^{2}(z)\ }$$

*What it buys.* The same as before, and one more thing: at $z = 0$,
$\tanh' = 1 - 0 = 1$. Compared with $\sigma'(0) = 1/4$, tanh passes four times
as much gradient at its best point. That factor of four, compounded over depth,
is the quantitative form of "tanh trains better than sigmoid".

**Step 4 — the relation between them.** Start from the right-hand side:

$$
2\sigma(2z) - 1
= \frac{2}{1+e^{-2z}} - 1
= \frac{2 - (1 + e^{-2z})}{1+e^{-2z}}
= \frac{1 - e^{-2z}}{1+e^{-2z}}
$$

Multiply numerator and denominator by $e^{z}$:

$$= \frac{e^{z} - e^{-z}}{e^{z} + e^{-z}} = \tanh(z)$$

$$\boxed{\ \tanh(z) = 2\sigma(2z) - 1\ } \tag{I.3.2}$$

*What it buys.* Tanh is not a second idea. It is sigmoid, stretched vertically
by $2$, shifted down by $1$, and compressed horizontally by $2$. Every property
of one transfers to the other with those three transformations applied —
including, by the chain rule, the derivative relation
$\tanh'(z) = 4\sigma'(2z)$, which is where the factor of four came from.

</Solution>

<Answer>
$$
\sigma'(z) = \sigma(z)(1-\sigma(z)), \qquad
\tanh'(z) = 1 - \tanh^{2}(z), \qquad
\tanh(z) = 2\sigma(2z) - 1
$$

All three are exact identities on all of $\R$, not approximations.
</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
**Numerically at $z = 0.5$.** $\sigma(0.5) = 0.6225$, so (I.3.1) predicts
$\sigma'(0.5) = 0.6225 \times 0.3775 = 0.2350$. Differencing numerically:
$(\sigma(0.501) - \sigma(0.499))/0.002 = 0.2350$. Agreement to four digits.

**The maxima are where they should be.** $\sigma(1-\sigma)$ is a downward
parabola in $\sigma$, maximised at $\sigma = 1/2$, i.e. $z = 0$, with value
$1/4$. And $1 - \tanh^2$ is maximised where $\tanh = 0$, again $z = 0$, with
value $1$. Both match the shapes plotted from I.3.B01.

**The factor of four is consistent.** Differentiating (I.3.2) gives
$\tanh'(z) = 4\sigma'(2z)$. At $z = 0$: $1 = 4 \times 0.25$. ✓
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
Identity (I.3.1) expresses the derivative in terms of the *output*. That is
convenient and it is also a trap in mixed precision: if $\sigma(z)$ has been
stored in fp16 and rounded to exactly $1.0$, then $\sigma(1-\sigma)$ evaluates
to exactly $0$ and the unit reports no gradient — even though the true
derivative is small but nonzero. Recomputing from $z$ would not help much, but
the failure is silent either way, and it is the reason logits rather than
probabilities are carried through a loss (<ApparatusRef ref="0.NU.02" />).
</Breaks>
