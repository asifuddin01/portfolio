A backward linear encoder obeys
$\overleftarrow h_t=x_t+0.5\overleftarrow h_{t+1}$ with terminal state
$\overleftarrow h_4=0$. Compute its state at position one for
$(1,2,3)$, then for $(1,2,7)$.
Could a representation containing that state be used unchanged to predict
the next token causally after reading only position one?

<Hint>
Evaluate from position three to position one.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

For $(1,2,3)$ the backward states at positions three, two and one are
$3$, $3.5$ and $2.75$.
For $(1,2,7)$ they are $7$, $5.5$ and $3.75$.

Position one's representation changes even though the observed prefix
$(1)$ is identical. It depends on the future.
Using it unchanged for causal next-token prediction leaks information that
would be unavailable at generation time.

A bidirectional encoder is appropriate when the full input is already
observed, such as tagging a completed sentence. The objection is to
using future context for a task whose information boundary forbids it,
not to bidirectional encoding itself.

</SolutionToggle>
