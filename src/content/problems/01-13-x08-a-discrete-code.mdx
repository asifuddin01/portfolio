A one-bit code is $z\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\pi)$, and
$f(z)=(z-0.3)^2$. Find the true derivative of $\E[f(z)]$ with respect to
$\pi$. Write the draw as $z=\mathbf 1[u<\pi]$ with $u$ uniform on
$(0,1)$, and find what differentiating through it returns. Then find the
score-function estimate at $\pi=0.5$, its mean and its variance.

<Hint>
$\E[f(z)]=\pi f(1)+(1-\pi)f(0)$. The function $\mathbf 1[u<\pi]$ is a step
in $\pi$.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**The truth.** $f(1)=0.49$ and $f(0)=0.09$, so the derivative is
$f(1)-f(0)=0.4$.

**Through the draw.** For fixed $u$, $\mathbf 1[u<\pi]$ is constant in
$\pi$ except at $\pi=u$, where it jumps. Its derivative is zero with
probability one, so backpropagating through the draw returns $0$. The
claim broken is that writing a draw as a function of noise always gives a
usable pathwise gradient: the function must be differentiable, and a step
is not.

**Score function.** $\log q(z)=z\log\pi+(1-z)\log(1-\pi)$, so its
derivative is $z/\pi-(1-z)/(1-\pi)$. At $\pi=0.5$ the estimate is
$f(1)/0.5=0.98$ when $z=1$ and $-f(0)/0.5=-0.18$ when $z=0$. Its mean is
$\tfrac12(0.98-0.18)=0.4$, correct, and its variance is

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\tfrac12(0.98-0.4)^2\\
&\;\;+\tfrac12(-0.18-0.4)^2=0.3364.
\end{aligned}
$$

The standard deviation $0.58$ exceeds the gradient itself, which is
typical of the score function. Relaxations such as the Gumbel-softmax
replace the step by a smooth function of the noise, accepting bias for a
pathwise gradient.

</SolutionToggle>
