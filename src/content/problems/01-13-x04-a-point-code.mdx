Keep the encoder's mean at $0.866025$, the posterior mean of the point
$(1,2)$, and shrink its standard deviation $s$. Compute the price of the
code, $\KL(\mathcal N(0.866025,s^2)\,\|\,\mathcal N(0,1))$, at $s=0.1$,
$0.01$ and $0.001$. How fast does it grow, and what does the limit say
about the autoencoder of Chapter I.12?

<Hint>
Only the $-\log s^2$ term of (I.13.5) grows as $s\to0$.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

With $\mu^2=0.75$, (I.13.5) gives $\tfrac12(s^2-0.25-\log s^2)$:

| $s$ | price of the code |
|---|---|
| $0.1$ | $2.182585$ |
| $0.01$ | $4.480220$ |
| $0.001$ | $6.782756$ |

Each tenfold narrowing adds $\log10=2.302585$ nats, apart from the vanishing
$s^2$ term: the price grows like $-\log s$ without limit.

A deterministic encoder is the limit $s\to0$, so its rate is infinite. The
autoencoder of I.12 is a VAE whose code costs nothing because its price is
never charged. Proposition 4 reaches the same corner from the other side:
in the linear model the encoder's variance is $\beta\sigma^2/\lambda_1$,
which goes to zero only as $\beta\to0$, and the rate
$\tfrac12\log(\lambda_1/\beta\sigma^2)$ diverges with it.

The noise in the code is what makes the space between codes trained. A
point code puts the decoder back where it was in I.12.

</SolutionToggle>
