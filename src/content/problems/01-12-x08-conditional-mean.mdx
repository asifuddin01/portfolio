A scalar input is $+1$ or $-1$ with probability one half each. It is
corrupted to $\tilde x=x+\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon$ drawn from
$\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)$.

(a) Find the best denoiser under squared error,
$\E[x\mid\tilde x]$.

(b) Evaluate it at $\tilde x=0.5$ for $\sigma^2=1$ and for
$\sigma^2=0.25$, and at $\tilde x=0$.

(c) Verify (I.12.8) for this distribution.

(d) The output at $\tilde x=0$ is not a possible input. What does that say
about reconstructions trained with squared error?

<Hint>
Use Bayes' rule (<ApparatusRef ref="0.PR.04" />) for the two posterior
probabilities. The Gaussian normalising constants cancel in their ratio.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**(a)** The two likelihoods are proportional to
$e^{-(\tilde x-1)^2/2\sigma^2}$ and $e^{-(\tilde x+1)^2/2\sigma^2}$, and
the priors are equal. Their ratio is $e^{2\tilde x/\sigma^2}$, so the
posterior probabilities are

$$
p_\pm=\frac{e^{\pm\tilde x/\sigma^2}}
 {e^{\tilde x/\sigma^2}+e^{-\tilde x/\sigma^2}}.
$$

The conditional mean is $p_+-p_-$:

$$
\E[x\mid\tilde x]=\tanh\!\big(\tilde x/\sigma^2\big).
$$

**(b)** At $\tilde x=0.5$: $\tanh(0.5)=0.462117$ for $\sigma^2=1$ and
$\tanh(2)=0.964028$ for $\sigma^2=0.25$. At $\tilde x=0$ it is $0$ for any
$\sigma^2$.

With less noise the same observation is stronger evidence, and the
denoiser commits to the nearer input. As $\sigma^2\to0$ it becomes the
sign of $\tilde x$. As $\sigma^2\to\infty$ it flattens to the prior mean,
$0$.

**(c)** The noised density is proportional to the sum of the two
likelihoods. Differentiating its logarithm,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{d}{d\tilde x}\log p_\sigma(\tilde x)\\
&\;\;=\frac{-(\tilde x-1)\,p_+-(\tilde x+1)\,p_-}{\sigma^2}\\
&\;\;=\frac{\tanh(\tilde x/\sigma^2)-\tilde x}{\sigma^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

So $\tilde x+\sigma^2$ times the score is $\tanh(\tilde x/\sigma^2)$, as
(I.12.8) requires. The displacement of the denoiser points towards the
nearer of the two inputs, where the noised density is higher. The script
checks this with a numerical derivative at all three points of (b).

**(d)** At $\tilde x=0$ the two inputs are equally likely. The value that
minimises the expected squared error is their mean, $0$, even though the
input is never $0$. Squared error rewards the average of the plausible
answers and not any one of them.

For images, the average of several sharp reconstructions that differ
slightly is a blurred one. An autoencoder trained with squared error on
inputs it cannot resolve returns that average. More training does not
sharpen it, because the average is the optimum.

</SolutionToggle>
