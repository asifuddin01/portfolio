Consider the fixed diagonal preconditioner
$P=\operatorname{diag}(1,0.01)$, weights $w=(1,1)$, data gradient
$g=(1,100)$, step size $\eta=0.01$, and $\lambda=0.1$.
Compare $w-\eta P(g+\lambda w)$ with $(1-\eta\lambda)w-\eta Pg$.
State what this example proves, and what it leaves out of Adam.

<Hint>
In the first expression the penalty is multiplied by $P$. In the second it
is not.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

The coupled gradient is $(1.1,100.1)$. Preconditioning gives $(1.1,1.001)$,
so the first update is

$$
w_{\mathrm{coupled}}=(0.989,\ 0.98999).
$$

For the decoupled expression, $Pg=(1,1)$ and
$(1-\eta\lambda)w=(0.999,0.999)$, giving

$$
w_{\mathrm{decoupled}}=(0.989,\ 0.989).
$$

The first coordinate agrees because its preconditioner equals one.
The second does not. The coupled penalty displacement there is
$0.00001$, one hundredth of the decoupled displacement $0.001$.

This is an exact counterexample for a fixed preconditioned update.
It isolates one reason the plain-gradient identity (I.8.6) fails.
Actual Adam does more: a penalty included in the gradient also changes the
history-dependent first and second moments, so its denominator is not
a fixed $P$ shared by both algorithms. Do not present this frozen-preconditioner
calculation as a complete Adam trajectory.

</SolutionToggle>
