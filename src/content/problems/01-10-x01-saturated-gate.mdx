A forget gate has bias $10$ and no other input, so $f=\sigma(10)$. Is the carry
line lossless? Compute $f$, $1-f$, the carry-line factor after $10^5$ steps and
the memory horizon.

<Hint>
Use $1-\sigma(b)=\sigma(-b)$, and (I.10.5) for the horizon.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

$f=\sigma(10)=0.9999546$ and $1-f=\sigma(-10)=4.539787\times10^{-5}$.
After $10^5$ steps the factor is $f^{100000}=0.010675$: about one per cent of
the signal is left.

The horizon is $-1/\ln f=22026.97$ steps. By (I.10.5) it lies just below
$1/(1-f)=1+e^{10}=22027.47$.

The carry line is not lossless. A sigmoid gate can be pushed as close to one
as the bias allows, which sets a long but finite horizon. Only the 1997
design, with no forget gate at all, had a factor of exactly one, and
[I.10.X03](/elementa/neural-networks/gating/problems/x03) shows what that cost.

</SolutionToggle>
