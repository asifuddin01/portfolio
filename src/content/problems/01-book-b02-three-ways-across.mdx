<Statement>
Compare the derivative of a quantity $L$ steps downstream with respect to
its start, for a saturating stack, a plain recurrence, a gated carry line
and a residual stack, and find the limit of each as $L$ grows.
</Statement>

<Given>
Four scalar chains, each evaluated where its per-step derivative is
constant:

- a sigmoid stack at its centre, derivative $\sigma'(0)=0.25$ per step (I.3);
- a recurrence $h_t=\tanh(wh_{t-1})$ near $0$ with $w=0.9$ (I.9);
- an LSTM carry line with forget value $f=0.95$ (I.10);
- a residual stack $x_{l+1}=x_l+a\tanh(x_l)$ near $0$, with $a=+0.05$,
  $a=-0.05$ or $a=-1$ (I.11).
</Given>

<Find>
Each per-step factor; the product after $L=20$ and $L=100$ steps; the
limit as $L\to\infty$; what sets each base.
</Find>

<Strategy>
By the chain rule (I.6), the derivative across $L$ steps is the product of
the per-step derivatives. Each chain here has a constant per-step factor,
so each product is a power.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**Per-step factors.** The sigmoid stack multiplies by $0.25$. The
recurrence by $w\tanh'(0)=0.9$. The carry line by $f=0.95$. The residual
stack by $1+a\tanh'(0)=1+a$: $1.05$, $0.95$ or $0$.

**Products.**

| factor | $L=20$ | $L=100$ |
|---|---|---|
| $0.25$ | $9.09495\times10^{-13}$ | $6.22302\times10^{-61}$ |
| $0.9$ | $0.121577$ | $2.65614\times10^{-5}$ |
| $0.95$ | $0.358486$ | $0.00592$ |
| $1.05$ | $2.65330$ | $131.501$ |

The rows are the sigmoid stack, the recurrence, the carry line and the
residual stack with $a=+0.05$.

The residual stack with $a=-0.05$ gives the carry line's numbers, and with
$a=-1$ the product is exactly $0$ after one step.

**Limits.** Every product of a constant base is geometric: it goes to $0$
when the base is below $1$ in size and grows without bound when it is
above. Only a base of exactly $1$ survives every depth. None of the four
mechanisms abolishes the geometry; they differ in what sets the base.

**What sets each base.** In the sigmoid stack it is the activation's
slope, at most $0.25$, so depth alone destroys the signal (I.3.P02). In
the recurrence it is a weight times a slope, and pushing it towards $1$
means asking a weight matrix to have norm near one everywhere (I.9.P02).
On the carry line it is a learned gate, close to $1$ without any weight
matrix being involved (I.10.P02). In the residual stack it is $1$ plus the
branch's slope, near $1$ when the branch is small, and it can be driven to
$0$ by a branch that cancels the identity (I.11.X09).

</Solution>

<Answer>
At $L=20$: $9.09\times10^{-13}$, $0.122$, $0.358$ and $2.65$; at $L=100$:
$6.22\times10^{-61}$, $2.66\times10^{-5}$, $0.00592$ and $132$. All are
geometric in $L$: below one they vanish, above one they explode.
</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
The carry line keeps $36$ per cent after twenty steps where the plain
recurrence keeps $12$ per cent, although its factor differs by only
$0.05$: small differences in the base compound.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
Real chains have factors that vary from step to step, and vectors whose
Jacobians are matrices. Then the products are of matrices, the relevant
quantity is a norm or a spectral radius, and the residual and gated paths
add terms that can reinforce or cancel, as I.10.B02 showed.
</Breaks>

<Variation>
To keep half the signal across $L=100$ steps, the base must be
$0.5^{1/100}=0.993092$. A forget bias of $\ln(0.993092/0.006908)=4.97$
sets that gate value at initialisation (I.10.T2).
</Variation>
