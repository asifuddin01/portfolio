For $\vec{y} = \vec{x}\mat{W}$ with $\vec{x} \in \R^{1 \times n}$ and
$\mat{W} \in \R^{n \times m}$, write down the Jacobian
$\partial \vec{y}/\partial \vec{x}$ explicitly, count its entries, and then show
that the quantity backpropagation actually needs — $\bar{\vec{y}}\mat{J}$ — can
be computed without ever building it. State the cost of each route.

<Hint>
Ask what $\partial y_j / \partial x_i$ is for this particular operation. The
answer does not depend on $\vec{x}$ at all, which is why the product collapses.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**The Jacobian.** $y_j = \sum_i x_i W_{ij}$, so

$$
\frac{\partial y_j}{\partial x_i} = W_{ij}
$$

The Jacobian *is* $\mat{W}$, with $nm$ entries. For a modest layer with
$n = m = 1024$ that is $1\,048\,576$ numbers.

**The product.** By Definition 7 the backward pass needs
$\bar{\vec{x}} = \bar{\vec{y}}\mat{J}^{\mathsf{T}}$, and since $\mat{J} = \mat{W}$:

$$
\bar{\vec{x}} = \bar{\vec{y}}\mat{W}^{\mathsf{T}}
$$

which is one matrix–vector product of exactly the shape the forward pass
already performed. No new object is constructed.

**The costs.** Forming $\mat{J}$ and multiplying costs $O(nm)$ storage and
$O(nm)$ arithmetic. The VJP costs $O(nm)$ arithmetic and $O(n + m)$ storage.
The arithmetic is the same; the storage is not, and storage is what decides
whether the layer fits.

**Why this generalises.** The saving is not special to matrix products. For
every primitive the derivative is known in closed form, and in every case the
closed form is a rule for computing $\bar{\vec{y}}\mat{J}$ rather than a recipe
for $\mat{J}$ — elementwise operations give a Hadamard product, a sum gives a
broadcast, a broadcast gives a sum. A system that materialised Jacobians would
be quadratic in width at every layer and could not train anything.

**Where it stops working.** A second derivative needs the Jacobian of the
Jacobian, and the same trick applies one level up — a Hessian–vector product is
computable without the Hessian, by differentiating a VJP. But asking for the
full Hessian of a model with $N$ parameters really does mean $N^2$ numbers, and
no reformulation avoids it.
