<Statement>
Identify exactly which location changes a pooled representation forgets.
</Statement>
<Given>
Use one-dimensional width-two max pooling with stride two, no padding.
Consider $a=(1,0,0,0)$, $b=(0,1,0,0)$ and $c=(0,0,1,0)$.
Also consider width-two average pooling with the same stride.
</Given>
<Find>
All pooled outputs. Decide whether the operation is invariant under every
one-position right shift and whether its output determines the original input.
</Find>
<Strategy>
Write the two disjoint windows explicitly. Distinguish a permutation inside
one window from movement into another window.
</Strategy>
<Solution>

Max pooling yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{\max}(a)&=(1,0),\\
P_{\max}(b)&=(1,0),\\
P_{\max}(c)&=(0,1).
\end{aligned}
$$

The shift from $a$ to $b$ stays within the first window and leaves its
maximum unchanged. The equally small shift from $b$ to $c$ crosses a
window boundary and changes the output.

Average pooling gives $(1/2,0)$, $(1/2,0)$ and $(0,1/2)$.
The same boundary distinction remains.

Both maps are many-to-one: $a$ and $b$ have different locations but
identical pooled outputs. No deterministic inverse receiving only that
output can reconstruct both correctly. This proves loss of information for
these maps, without claiming that every downsampling transform is lossy.

</Solution>
<Answer>
Pooling is invariant to these within-window rearrangements, not to every
small translation of the whole signal. It cannot recover the lost location
from the pooled values alone.
</Answer>
<CheckSanity>
A two-position shift aligned to the stride moves the pooled response by
one position, provided the signal and boundaries are treated consistently.
That is an equivariance relation, not an unchanged output.
</CheckSanity>
<Breaks>
The argument depends on the window partition. Changing stride, padding or
the reduction changes the equivalence classes of inputs. Extra side
information, such as stored argmax indices, also changes what a decoder receives.
</Breaks>
<Variation>
The vectors $(1,1)$ and $(0,2)$ have the same mean but different maxima.
Max and average pooling discard different information even in one window.
</Variation>
