For a residual block $\vec{y} = \vec{x} + F(\vec{x})$, derive the Jacobian and
then the adjoint recurrence for a stack of $L$ such blocks. Show that the
product cannot vanish for want of a route, and state precisely what the identity
term does and does not guarantee.

<Hint>
Differentiate the sum term by term. One of the two derivatives does not depend
on $F$ at all.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**The Jacobian.** Differentiating $\vec{y} = \vec{x} + F(\vec{x})$ coordinate by
coordinate:

$$
\frac{\partial y_j}{\partial x_i} = \delta_{ij} + \frac{\partial F_j}{\partial x_i},
\qquad \text{so} \qquad \mat{J} = \mat{I} + \mat{J}_F
$$

which is (I.6.5).

**The recurrence.** By (I.6.2) the adjoint entering block $\ell$ from block
$\ell + 1$ is

$$
\bar{\vec{x}}^{(\ell)} = \bar{\vec{x}}^{(\ell+1)}\left(\mat{I} + \mat{J}_{F_\ell}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}
= \bar{\vec{x}}^{(\ell+1)} + \bar{\vec{x}}^{(\ell+1)}\mat{J}_{F_\ell}^{\mathsf{T}}
$$

**Across the stack.** Expanding the product over $L$ blocks:

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial \vec{x}^{(0)}} = \bar{\vec{x}}^{(L)}\prod_{\ell=1}^{L}\left(\mat{I} + \mat{J}_{F_\ell}\right)^{\mathsf{T}}
$$

Multiplying out gives $2^L$ terms, one for each subset of blocks whose $J_F$ is
taken. Exactly one of those terms takes the identity from every block:

$$
\bar{\vec{x}}^{(L)}\mat{I}^L = \bar{\vec{x}}^{(L)}
$$

The adjoint that left the loss arrives at the first block undiminished, whatever
the $F_\ell$ are and however many there are. In the plain stack of I.6.B04 every
term carried a factor below $1/4$, so the whole product decayed; here one term
carries no factor at all.

**What this does not promise.** Three things.

It is not a guarantee that the gradient is *useful*. The identity path
transmits the adjoint of the loss with respect to the block's input, not with
respect to its parameters — the parameter gradients still flow through
$\mat{J}_{F_\ell}$, and a block whose $F$ is saturated still learns nothing.
The signal reaches it; whether it can act on it is separate.

It does not prevent explosion. $\mat{I} + \mat{J}_F$ has eigenvalues
$1 + \lambda_i$, and if $\mat{J}_F$ has eigenvalues near $+1$ the product grows
like $2^L$. Residual stacks are normally paired with normalisation for exactly
this reason, and the pairing is not decoration.

It says nothing about optimisation beyond the gradient's magnitude. A route for
the adjoint is a necessary condition for training a deep stack, not a sufficient
one, and the empirical fact that residual networks train at depths plain stacks
cannot is a result rather than a corollary of this algebra.
