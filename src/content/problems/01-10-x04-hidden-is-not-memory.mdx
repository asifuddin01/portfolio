Two runs of an LSTM coordinate reach $c_t=0.5$ and $c_t=-0.5$. At step $t$ the
output gate is $0.01$. At step $t+1$ the gates are $f=0.99$, $i=0.01$, with
candidate $0$ and output gate $0.99$. How far apart are the two hidden states
at $t$, and at $t+1$? What does this say about reading the memory from $h_t$?

<Hint>
The cell update at $t+1$ writes nothing, so $c_{t+1}=0.99\,c_t$.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

At $t$, $h=0.01\tanh(\pm0.5)$, a gap of
$0.01\cdot2\tanh(0.5)=0.009242$. A readout of $h_t$ sees runs that are almost
identical.

At $t+1$ the cells are $\pm0.495$, and $h=0.99\tanh(\pm0.495)$, a gap of
$0.907188$. The difference was in the cell all along. The output gate hid it
for one step and then exposed it.

So $h_t$ is a view of the cell through a gate, not the memory itself. A probe
or classifier trained on $h_t$ can miss information the network will use one
step later. To ask what an LSTM remembers, look at $\vec c_t$.

</SolutionToggle>
