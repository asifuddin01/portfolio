Let $q=\mathcal N(\mu,1)$ at $\mu=0$ and $f(z)=\sin(10z)$. Find the true
derivative of $\E_q[f(z)]$ with respect to $\mu$, and the variances of the
pathwise estimate $f'(z)$ and the score-function estimate
$f(z)(z-\mu)$.

<Hint>
For $z\sim\mathcal N(\mu,1)$, $\E[\cos(\omega z)]=\cos(\omega\mu)e^{-\omega^2/2}$.
Use $\cos^2t=\tfrac12(1+\cos2t)$.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**The truth.** $\E_q[\sin(10z)]=\sin(10\mu)e^{-50}$, so the derivative at
$\mu=0$ is $10e^{-50}=1.9\times10^{-21}$: zero to any printed precision.

**Pathwise.** The estimate is $10\cos(10z)$. Its mean is the derivative,
and its second moment is
$100\,\E[\cos^2(10z)]=50\big(1+e^{-200}\big)$. Its variance is therefore
$50$ to every printed digit.

**Score function.** The estimate is $\sin(10z)\,z$. Its mean is also the
derivative. Its second moment is
$\E[\sin^2(10z)\,z^2]=\tfrac12\E[z^2]-\tfrac12\E[z^2\cos(20z)]$, and the
second average is $-399\,e^{-200}$, so the variance is $0.5$ to every
printed digit.

So the pathwise estimate is a hundred times noisier here. It reads the
slope of $f$ at the draw, and a function that oscillates ten times per
unit has slopes of size ten everywhere, which cancel only on average. The
score function reads the value of $f$, which never exceeds one. The claim
broken is that reparameterisation always lowers the variance. It does for
the smooth, slowly varying log-likelihoods of a VAE decoder; it does not
in general.

</SolutionToggle>
