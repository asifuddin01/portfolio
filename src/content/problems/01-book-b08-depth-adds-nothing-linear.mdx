<Statement>
Prove that a deep linear autoencoder, of any depth and widths, attains no
lower reconstruction loss than principal components with as many
components as its narrowest layer, and that it attains exactly that loss.
Connect the result to the linear β-VAE of I.13 as β goes to zero.
</Statement>

<Given>
Centred data with covariance eigenvalues
$\lambda_1\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_d$. A linear autoencoder
$\hat{\vec x}=\mat W_L\cdots\mat W_1\vec x$ whose layer widths are
$d=n_0,n_1,\ldots,n_{L-1},n_L=d$, with $k=\min_\ell n_\ell<d$. For the
numbers, the four points of I.12, with widths $2,3,1,3,2$.
</Given>

<Find>
A lower bound on the loss, a choice of weights that attains it, and the
loss for the four points; the link to I.13.T3.
</Find>

<Strategy>
The product of the layers is one matrix whose rank is limited by the
narrowest layer. Then I.12.T1 applies to that one matrix.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**The product is one map.** Without nonlinearities the network computes
$\mat M=\mat W_L\cdots\mat W_1$, a single linear map (I.3.B05). Every vector
that leaves the narrowest layer lies in a space of dimension $k$, so the
image of $\mat M$ has dimension at most $k$: $\operatorname{rank}\mat M\leq k$
(<ApparatusRef ref="0.LA.04" />).

**The bound.** The loss depends on the weights only through $\mat M$, as
$\operatorname{tr}\big((\mat I-\mat M)\mat S(\mat I-\mat M)^{\mathsf T}\big)$
(I.12.2). A matrix of rank at most $k$ factors as $\mat D\mat E$ with
$\mat E$ of shape $k\times d$, so I.12.T1 applies:
$\mathcal L\geq\lambda_{k+1}+\cdots+\lambda_d$. $\blacksquare$

**Attaining it.** Let $\mat U_k$ hold the top $k$ eigenvectors. Map the
input to $\mat U_k^{\mathsf T}\vec x$ in the first layers, carry those $k$
numbers unchanged through every later layer, padding with zeros where a
layer is wider than $k$, and map back with $\mat U_k$ at the end. Every
width is at least $k$, so each step is possible, and the product is
$\mat U_k\mat U_k^{\mathsf T}$, which attains the bound. $\blacksquare$

**The four points.** With widths $2,3,1,3,2$, the narrowest is $1$. The
layers
$\begin{bmatrix}0.8&0.6\\0&0\\1&0\end{bmatrix}$,
$\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\end{bmatrix}$,
$\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\\0\end{bmatrix}$ and
$\begin{bmatrix}0.8&0&0\\0.6&0&0\end{bmatrix}$
multiply to $\begin{bmatrix}0.64&0.48\\0.48&0.36\end{bmatrix}=\vec u_1\vec u_1^{\mathsf T}$,
and the loss is $1=\lambda_2$. The extra rows in the first layer and the
extra width in the third change nothing: depth and width beyond the
bottleneck buy no expressiveness here.

**The link to I.13.** By I.13.T3 the linear β-VAE has distortion
$\lambda_2+\cdots+\lambda_d+\beta\sigma^2$, which tends to the same bound
as $\beta\to0$. A deterministic linear autoencoder of any depth, and a
linear VAE whose code becomes free, end at one point: the principal
subspace.

</Solution>

<Answer>
$\mathcal L\geq\lambda_{k+1}+\cdots+\lambda_d$ with $k$ the narrowest width,
attained by passing the top $k$ principal scores through every layer. For
the four points with widths $2,3,1,3,2$ the loss is $1$, which is also the
linear β-VAE's distortion as $\beta\to0$.
</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
If every width were at least $d$, then $k=d$, the bound would be an empty
sum, and the identity would be reachable: depth cannot make a linear
autoencoder compress unless some layer is narrow.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
The result is about the minimum, not the path to it. Gradient descent on
the deep factorisation meets saddles that the one-layer problem does not
have (I.12.T2), and its trajectory depends on depth, as I.3.B05 noted. With
nonlinear layers the bound no longer applies, which is the point of making
them nonlinear.
</Breaks>

<Variation>
A two-dimensional code, widths $2,3,2,3,2$, makes $k=2=d$: the loss can be
$0$, and the network can copy. The narrowest layer, not the depth, decides
what is lost.
</Variation>
