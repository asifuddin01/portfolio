<Statement>
Evaluate sigmoid, tanh, ReLU, LeakyReLU with slope $0.01$, and GELU at
$z \in \{-2,\ -0.5,\ 0,\ 0.5,\ 2\}$. Then read four structural facts off the
table that no single value shows.
</Statement>

<Given>
$$
\sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1+e^{-z}}, \qquad
\tanh(z) = \frac{e^{z}-e^{-z}}{e^{z}+e^{-z}}, \qquad
\mathrm{ReLU}(z) = \max(0, z)
$$

$$
\mathrm{LeakyReLU}(z) = \begin{cases} z & z > 0\\ 0.01z & z \le 0\end{cases},
\qquad
\mathrm{GELU}(z) = z\,\Phi(z)
$$

with $\Phi$ the standard normal CDF.
</Given>

<Find>
A $5\times 5$ table of values, and four properties visible only across it.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Compute the two exponential families first, since tanh follows from sigmoid;
the two rectifiers need no arithmetic at all; GELU needs $\Phi$.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**Step 1 — sigmoid.** $\sigma(-2) = 1/(1+e^{2}) = 1/8.3891 = 0.1192$. By the
symmetry $\sigma(-z) = 1 - \sigma(z)$, $\sigma(2) = 0.8808$ without further work.
$\sigma(0) = 1/2$ exactly. $\sigma(-0.5) = 1/(1+e^{0.5}) = 1/2.6487 = 0.3775$,
so $\sigma(0.5) = 0.6225$.

**Step 2 — tanh from sigmoid.** Using $\tanh(z) = 2\sigma(2z) - 1$:
$\tanh(0.5) = 2\sigma(1) - 1 = 2(0.7311) - 1 = 0.4621$, and
$\tanh(2) = 2\sigma(4) - 1 = 2(0.9820) - 1 = 0.9640$. Odd symmetry gives the
negatives.

**Step 3 — the rectifiers.** No arithmetic. ReLU zeroes the negatives and copies
the positives; LeakyReLU scales the negatives by $0.01$.

**Step 4 — GELU.** $\Phi(-2) = 0.0228$, so $\mathrm{GELU}(-2) = (-2)(0.0228) = -0.0455$.
$\Phi(0) = 0.5$ so $\mathrm{GELU}(0) = 0$. $\Phi(2) = 0.9772$, giving $1.9545$.

**The table.**

| $z$ | $\sigma$ | $\tanh$ | ReLU | LeakyReLU | GELU |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $-2.0$ | $0.1192$ | $-0.9640$ | $0.0000$ | $-0.0200$ | $-0.0455$ |
| $-0.5$ | $0.3775$ | $-0.4621$ | $0.0000$ | $-0.0050$ | $-0.1543$ |
| $0.0$ | $0.5000$ | $0.0000$ | $0.0000$ | $0.0000$ | $0.0000$ |
| $0.5$ | $0.6225$ | $0.4621$ | $0.5000$ | $0.5000$ | $0.3457$ |
| $2.0$ | $0.8808$ | $0.9640$ | $2.0000$ | $2.0000$ | $1.9545$ |

**Four structural facts.**

*Only sigmoid fails to pass zero to zero.* $\sigma(0) = 0.5$. Every other
column has $0 \mapsto 0$. A layer of sigmoids therefore emits a nonzero mean
even from centred input, and that offset compounds with depth — the historical
reason tanh replaced sigmoid in hidden layers.

*Sigmoid and tanh are bounded; the rectifiers are not.* At $z = 2$ the bounded
pair are already at $0.88$ and $0.96$, close to their ceilings, while ReLU
returns $2$ and would return $200$ at $z = 200$. Boundedness is what causes
saturation and also what prevents blow-up.

*GELU is not monotonic.* From $z = -2$ to $z = -0.5$ the output *falls* from
$-0.0455$ to $-0.1543$. No other column does this. GELU is a soft gate, not a
soft switch, and this dip is where its behaviour genuinely differs from a
smoothed ReLU.

*GELU is close to ReLU where it matters and different where it does not.* At
$z = 2$ the two differ by $0.0455$; at $z = -0.5$ they differ by $0.1543$. The
whole difference lives in a band around the origin, which is exactly where
gradients are decided.

</Solution>

<Answer>
The $5\times5$ table above. The four properties: sigmoid alone has
$\varphi(0) \neq 0$; the bounded pair saturate while the rectifiers do not;
GELU is non-monotonic on $(-\infty, -0.75)$ roughly; and GELU and ReLU agree
away from the origin and differ only near it.
</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
**The symmetries hold.** $\sigma(-2) + \sigma(2) = 0.1192 + 0.8808 = 1.0000$
exactly, as $\sigma(-z) = 1 - \sigma(z)$ requires. And
$\tanh(-2) = -\tanh(2)$ to every printed digit.

**The identity $\tanh(z) = 2\sigma(2z) - 1$ checks out.** At $z = 0.5$:
$2\sigma(1) - 1 = 2(0.7311) - 1 = 0.4622$, against $\tanh(0.5) = 0.4621$. The
last digit differs by rounding of the intermediate, not by error.

**GELU is between $0$ and $z$ for $z > 0$.** $0 < 0.3457 < 0.5$ and
$0 < 1.9545 < 2$. Since $\Phi \in (0,1)$ and $\mathrm{GELU} = z\Phi(z)$, that
must hold — and it is the fastest way to catch a sign or scale slip.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
Every value here assumes the activation is applied to a scalar independently.
That is what "elementwise" means, and it is why one table of five numbers
characterises the function completely. Softmax is not elementwise — its output
at one coordinate depends on all of them — so no such table exists for it, and
it belongs to Chapter I.4 with the losses rather than here with the activations.
</Breaks>
