<Statement>
Derive the two equations every implementation of backpropagation contains — the
recurrence that carries an adjoint from one layer to the previous one, and the
outer product that turns an adjoint into a weight gradient — from the graph
rule (I.6.2) alone. Nothing else may be assumed.
</Statement>

<Given>
One layer of an MLP, written as three nodes so that each has a Jacobian that can
be stated:

$$
\vec{z}^{(\ell)} = \vec{a}^{(\ell-1)}\mat{W}^{(\ell)} + \vec{b}^{(\ell)},
\qquad
\vec{a}^{(\ell)} = \varphi(\vec{z}^{(\ell)})
$$

with $\vec{a}^{(\ell-1)} \in \R^{1 \times d_{\ell-1}}$,
$\mat{W}^{(\ell)} \in \R^{d_{\ell-1} \times d_\ell}$ and $\varphi$ applied
elementwise. A scalar loss $L$ sits at the sink. Write
$\delta^{(\ell)} = \partial L / \partial \vec{z}^{(\ell)}$.
</Given>

<Find>
Expressions for $\partial L/\partial \mat{W}^{(\ell)}$,
$\partial L/\partial \vec{b}^{(\ell)}$ and $\delta^{(\ell-1)}$ in terms of
$\delta^{(\ell)}$ and quantities the forward pass already stored.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Work one node at a time and never write down a full Jacobian. Each step asks
the same question: given the adjoint of this node's output, what is the adjoint
of its input? That is the vector–Jacobian product of Definition 7, and for
these three operations it has a closed form in each case.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**Step 1 — through the nonlinearity.** $\varphi$ acts elementwise, so
$\partial a^{(\ell)}_j / \partial z^{(\ell)}_i$ is zero unless $i = j$. The
Jacobian is diagonal, and multiplying by a diagonal matrix is multiplying
elementwise:

$$
\delta^{(\ell)} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial \vec{z}^{(\ell)}}
= \frac{\partial L}{\partial \vec{a}^{(\ell)}} \odot \varphi'(\vec{z}^{(\ell)})
$$

This is the first place the forward pass is consumed: $\varphi'$ is evaluated at
the stored $\vec{z}^{(\ell)}$, and without it there is nothing to multiply.

**Step 2 — the bias.** In $\vec{z} = \vec{a}\mat{W} + \vec{b}$ the bias enters
with coefficient one, so $\partial z_j / \partial b_i = \delta_{ij}$ and the
local Jacobian is the identity. By (I.6.2) the adjoint passes through unchanged:

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial \vec{b}^{(\ell)}} = \delta^{(\ell)}
$$

**Step 3 — the weights.** Take one entry. Only the $j$-th output involves
$W_{ij}$, and it involves it linearly with coefficient $a^{(\ell-1)}_i$:

$$
\frac{\partial z^{(\ell)}_k}{\partial W^{(\ell)}_{ij}}
= \begin{cases} a^{(\ell-1)}_i & k = j \\ 0 & k \neq j \end{cases}
$$

So the sum over consumers in (I.6.2) collapses to a single term:

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial W^{(\ell)}_{ij}}
= \sum_k \delta^{(\ell)}_k \frac{\partial z^{(\ell)}_k}{\partial W^{(\ell)}_{ij}}
= a^{(\ell-1)}_i \delta^{(\ell)}_j
$$

Every entry is a product of one input coordinate and one output adjoint, which
is exactly an outer product:

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial \mat{W}^{(\ell)}}
= (\vec{a}^{(\ell-1)})^{\mathsf{T}} \delta^{(\ell)}
$$

The shapes confirm it: $(d_{\ell-1} \times 1)(1 \times d_\ell)$ gives
$d_{\ell-1} \times d_\ell$, the shape of $\mat{W}^{(\ell)}$. A gradient always
has the shape of the thing it differentiates.

**Step 4 — back to the previous layer.** Now differentiate with respect to the
*input* rather than the parameters. Coordinate $a^{(\ell-1)}_i$ feeds every
output $z^{(\ell)}_j$, so this time the sum does not collapse:

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial a^{(\ell-1)}_i}
= \sum_j \delta^{(\ell)}_j \frac{\partial z^{(\ell)}_j}{\partial a^{(\ell-1)}_i}
= \sum_j \delta^{(\ell)}_j W^{(\ell)}_{ij}
$$

which is the $i$-th entry of $\delta^{(\ell)}(\mat{W}^{(\ell)})^{\mathsf{T}}$:

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial \vec{a}^{(\ell-1)}} = \delta^{(\ell)}(\mat{W}^{(\ell)})^{\mathsf{T}}
$$

The transpose is not a convention. Forwards, $\mat{W}$ maps $d_{\ell-1}$
coordinates to $d_\ell$; backwards, the adjoint must travel from $d_\ell$ back
to $d_{\ell-1}$, and the only way to do that with the same matrix is to use it
the other way round.

**Step 5 — combine.** Applying Step 1 at layer $\ell - 1$ to the result of Step
4 gives the recurrence:

$$
\delta^{(\ell-1)} = \left(\delta^{(\ell)}(\mat{W}^{(\ell)})^{\mathsf{T}}\right)
\odot \varphi'(\vec{z}^{(\ell-1)})
$$

which is (I.6.3), and Steps 2 and 3 are (I.6.4). Both came from (I.6.2) and
three local Jacobians, and no Jacobian was ever written out in full.

</Solution>

<Answer>

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial \mat{W}^{(\ell)}} = (\vec{a}^{(\ell-1)})^{\mathsf{T}} \delta^{(\ell)},
\qquad
\frac{\partial L}{\partial \vec{b}^{(\ell)}} = \delta^{(\ell)}
$$

$$
\delta^{(\ell-1)} = \left(\delta^{(\ell)}(\mat{W}^{(\ell)})^{\mathsf{T}}\right) \odot \varphi'(\vec{z}^{(\ell-1)})
$$

</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
**Every gradient has the shape of its parameter.**
$(d_{\ell-1} \times 1)(1 \times d_\ell) = d_{\ell-1} \times d_\ell$ for the
weights, and $\delta^{(\ell)}$ is $1 \times d_\ell$ for the bias. This is the
cheapest check there is and it catches a transposed derivation immediately.

**The recurrence terminates.** $\delta^{(L)}$ comes from the loss, and each
application of the recurrence produces $\delta$ one layer earlier, so $L$ steps
reach the input. Nothing in the derivation refers to a layer that has not been
reached, which is what makes reverse topological order the right traversal.

**Only stored quantities appear.** $\vec{a}^{(\ell-1)}$, $\vec{z}^{(\ell-1)}$
and $\mat{W}^{(\ell)}$ — all present after the forward pass. No re-evaluation
of $\varphi$ or of any matrix product is required, which is why the backward
pass costs about what the forward pass cost rather than twice it.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
The derivation assumes $\varphi$ is elementwise, which is what makes its
Jacobian diagonal and its VJP a Hadamard product. Softmax is not elementwise:
its Jacobian is $\mathrm{diag}(\vec{p}) - \vec{p}^{\mathsf{T}}\vec{p}$, dense
and of size $d \times d$, and Step 1 does not apply to it. Every layer that
mixes coordinates — softmax, normalisation over a feature dimension, attention
— needs its own VJP derived separately, and treating one of them as elementwise
produces a gradient that is wrong in a way no shape check detects, because the
shapes are unchanged.

It also assumes each of $\vec{a}^{(\ell-1)}$, $\mat{W}^{(\ell)}$ and
$\vec{b}^{(\ell)}$ has exactly one consumer. Tie a weight across two layers and
Step 3 acquires a second term, by (I.6.2); I.6.X04 is that case, and the
failure of forgetting it is not subtle.
</Breaks>
