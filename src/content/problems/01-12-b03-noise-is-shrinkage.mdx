<Statement>
Find the linear map that best removes noise of variance $\sigma^2$ from four
points, with and without a bottleneck, and follow it as the noise goes to
zero and to infinity.
</Statement>

<Given>
The four points of
[I.12.B01](/elementa/neural-networks/autoencoders/problems/b01), with
eigenvalues $\lambda=(4,1)$ and eigenvectors $\vec u_1=(0.8,0.6)$,
$\vec u_2=(-0.6,0.8)$. The input is corrupted as
$\tilde{\vec x}=\vec x+\vec\varepsilon$, where $\vec\varepsilon$ is
independent of $\vec x$ with mean zero and covariance $\sigma^2\mat I$. The
loss is $\E\|\mat M\tilde{\vec x}-\vec x\|^2$.
</Given>

<Find>
(a) The optimal gain on each principal direction and the loss it leaves.
(b) The optimal map and its loss at $\sigma^2=1$.
(c) The same when $\mat M$ must have rank one.
(d) The limits $\sigma^2\to0$ and $\sigma^2\to\infty$, with values at
$\sigma^2=0.01$ and $100$.
(e) A check of (I.12.8) for Gaussian data.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Use the decomposition in (I.12.7). The optimal map shares the eigenvectors
of $\mat S$, so the problem separates into one scalar problem per direction.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**(a)** By (I.12.7) the loss is the clean reconstruction loss of
$\mat M$ plus $\sigma^2\|\mat M\|_F^2$, and its minimiser
$\mat S(\mat S+\sigma^2\mat I)^{-1}$ has the eigenvectors of $\mat S$. Write
$\mat M=\sum_ig_i\vec u_i\vec u_i^{\mathsf T}$. The loss becomes
$\sum_i\big[(1-g_i)^2\lambda_i+\sigma^2g_i^2\big]$. Each term is a parabola
in its own $g_i$, with derivative $-2(1-g_i)\lambda_i+2\sigma^2g_i$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_i&=\frac{\lambda_i}{\lambda_i+\sigma^2},\\
\text{loss}_i&=\frac{\lambda_i\,\sigma^2}{\lambda_i+\sigma^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

A direction is kept at exactly one half when the noise variance equals its
own.

**(b)** At $\sigma^2=1$ the gains are $4/5$ and $1/2$. The two
projections are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec u_1\vec u_1^{\mathsf T}&=\begin{bmatrix}0.64&0.48\\0.48&0.36\end{bmatrix},\\[2pt]
\vec u_2\vec u_2^{\mathsf T}&=\begin{bmatrix}0.36&-0.48\\-0.48&0.64\end{bmatrix},
\end{aligned}
$$

so

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mat M^\star&=0.8\,\vec u_1\vec u_1^{\mathsf T}+0.5\,\vec u_2\vec u_2^{\mathsf T}\\[2pt]
 &=\begin{bmatrix}0.692&0.144\\0.144&0.608\end{bmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The loss is $4/5+1/2=1.3$.

**(c)** A rank-one map keeps one direction. Keeping direction $i$ costs
$\lambda_i\sigma^2/(\lambda_i+\sigma^2)$ there and the full $\lambda_j$ on
the other. Keeping $\vec u_1$ costs $0.8+1=1.8$; keeping $\vec u_2$ costs
$0.5+4=4.5$. So the best rank-one map is
$0.8\,\vec u_1\vec u_1^{\mathsf T}$, with loss $1.8$.

The bottleneck chooses the direction, as in I.12.T1. The noise then shrinks
what is kept.

**(d)** As $\sigma^2\to0$ each gain tends to $1$, the map tends to the
identity and the loss to $0$. Without noise, nothing stops the copy. As
$\sigma^2\to\infty$ each gain behaves like $\lambda_i/\sigma^2$ and tends
to $0$; the map outputs the mean, and the loss tends to
$\operatorname{tr}\mat S=5$.

| $\sigma^2$ | $0.01$ | $1$ | $100$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $g_1$ | $0.997506$ | $0.8$ | $0.038462$ |
| $g_2$ | $0.990099$ | $0.5$ | $0.009901$ |
| loss | $0.019876$ | $1.3$ | $4.836253$ |

**(e)** If $\vec x$ is Gaussian with mean zero and covariance $\mat S$, then
$\tilde{\vec x}$ is Gaussian with covariance $\mat S+\sigma^2\mat I$ and
score $-(\mat S+\sigma^2\mat I)^{-1}\tilde{\vec x}$. Then (I.12.8) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\tilde{\vec x}-\sigma^2(\mat S+\sigma^2\mat I)^{-1}\tilde{\vec x}\\
&\;\;=\mat S(\mat S+\sigma^2\mat I)^{-1}\tilde{\vec x},
\end{aligned}
$$

which is the map of (a). For Gaussian data the best denoiser is linear, and
the two results are one.

</Solution>

<Answer>
Gains $\lambda_i/(\lambda_i+\sigma^2)$ with loss
$\sum_i\lambda_i\sigma^2/(\lambda_i+\sigma^2)$. At $\sigma^2=1$:
$\mat M^\star=\begin{bmatrix}0.692&0.144\\0.144&0.608\end{bmatrix}$ with
loss $1.3$, or $0.8\,\vec u_1\vec u_1^{\mathsf T}$ with loss $1.8$ at rank
one. The map tends to the identity as $\sigma^2\to0$ and to zero as
$\sigma^2\to\infty$.
</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
At $\sigma^2=1$, copying the corrupted input costs
$\sigma^2\|\mat I\|_F^2=2$ and outputting the mean costs
$\operatorname{tr}\mat S=5$. The optimum, $1.3$, is below both. Every gain
lies strictly between $0$ and $1$.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
Noise with a general covariance $\mat N$ gives
$\mat S(\mat S+\mat N)^{-1}$, which shares eigenvectors with $\mat S$ only
if the two matrices commute. And for data that are not Gaussian the best
denoiser is not linear at all:
[I.12.X08](/elementa/neural-networks/autoencoders/problems/x08) finds a
$\tanh$.
</Breaks>

<Variation>
At $\sigma^2=4$ the gains are $(0.5,\;0.2)$ and the loss is $2+0.8=2.8$.
The script also confirms the decomposition of (I.12.7) exactly, by
averaging over the four points and four noise vectors
$(\pm\sigma\sqrt2,0)$, $(0,\pm\sigma\sqrt2)$, whose covariance is
$\sigma^2\mat I$.
</Variation>
