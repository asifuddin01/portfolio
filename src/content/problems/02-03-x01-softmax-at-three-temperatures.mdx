Compute $\softmax(\vec{s}/\tau)$ for $\vec{s} = (2, 1, 0)$ at
$\tau = 1$, $\tau = 0.5$ and $\tau = 2$. State what each result says about
the temperature's role, and name the two limits.

<Hint>
Divide before exponentiating, and subtract the largest entry of the scaled
vector first. The three denominators are all you need.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**τ = 1.** Scaled scores $(2,1,0)$. Subtract the maximum: $(0,-1,-2)$, giving
exponentials $1$, $0.3679$, $0.1353$, and a denominator of $1.5032$.

$$\vec{a} = (0.6652,\; 0.2447,\; 0.0900)$$

**τ = 0.5.** Scaled scores $(4,2,0)$. Shifted: $(0,-2,-4)$, exponentials
$1$, $0.1353$, $0.0183$, denominator $1.1536$.

$$\vec{a} = (0.8668,\; 0.1173,\; 0.0159)$$

**τ = 2.** Scaled scores $(1, 0.5, 0)$. Shifted: $(0,-0.5,-1)$, exponentials
$1$, $0.6065$, $0.3679$, denominator $1.9744$.

$$\vec{a} = (0.5065,\; 0.3072,\; 0.1863)$$

Each row sums to $1.0000$.

**What it says.** Lowering $\tau$ multiplies every score gap by $1/\tau$ before
exponentiating, so the distribution concentrates on the largest entry; raising
$\tau$ shrinks the gaps toward zero and flattens it. The mass on the top token
moves from $0.5065$ to $0.8668$ as $\tau$ falls from 2 to 0.5, on unchanged
scores.

**The two limits.** As $\tau \to 0^{+}$ the distribution approaches
$\text{one-hot}(\arg\max \vec{s})$; as $\tau \to \infty$ it approaches the
uniform distribution over the three entries, $(1/3, 1/3, 1/3)$. Neither limit
is reached at any finite $\tau$.

</SolutionToggle>
