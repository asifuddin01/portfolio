<Statement>
Find the eigenvalues and the limit of the graph convolution's averaging
on the paw graph exactly, using the graph's symmetry, and bound the
distance to the limit after $k$ layers.
</Statement>

<Given>
The paw graph of [I.14.B01](/elementa/neural-networks/gnn/problems/b01),
with degrees $\tilde{\vec d}=(3,3,4,2)$ counted with self-loops,
$\hat{\mat A}=\tilde{\mat D}^{-1/2}(\mat A+\mat I)\tilde{\mat D}^{-1/2}$ and
$\vec x=(1,2,3,4)$.
</Given>

<Find>
(a) An eigenvector with eigenvalue $1$, and one with eigenvalue $0$.
(b) The other two eigenvalues, exactly. (c) The limit of
$\hat{\mat A}^k\vec x$. (d) A bound on the distance after $k$ layers, and its
value at $k=8$.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Guess two eigenvectors from the structure: square roots of degrees, and
the difference of the two nodes that swap under the graph's symmetry. Get
the other two from the trace of $\hat{\mat A}$ and of $\hat{\mat A}^2$.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**(a)** Let $\vec s=(\sqrt3,\sqrt3,2,\sqrt2)$, the square roots of the
degrees. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hat{\mat A}\vec s
 &=\tilde{\mat D}^{-1/2}(\mat A+\mat I)\mathbf 1\\
 &=\tilde{\mat D}^{-1/2}\tilde{\vec d}=\vec s,
\end{aligned}
$$

since each row of $\mat A+\mat I$ sums to the degree with self-loop. So
$\vec s$ has eigenvalue $1$. Nodes $1$ and $2$ have identical rows of
$\hat{\mat A}$, so $\hat{\mat A}(1,-1,0,0)^{\mathsf T}=\vec 0$: eigenvalue $0$.

**(b)** The trace of $\hat{\mat A}$ is the sum of its diagonal,
$\sum_v1/\tilde d_v=\tfrac13+\tfrac13+\tfrac14+\tfrac12=\tfrac{17}{12}$. The
other two eigenvalues therefore add to $\tfrac{17}{12}-1-0=\tfrac5{12}$. The
trace of $\hat{\mat A}^2$ is the sum of squared entries. The diagonal gives
$\tfrac19+\tfrac19+\tfrac1{16}+\tfrac14=\tfrac{77}{144}$, and the four edges,
each appearing twice, give
$2\big(\tfrac19+\tfrac1{12}+\tfrac1{12}+\tfrac18\big)=\tfrac{116}{144}$, for a
total of $\tfrac{193}{144}$. So the squares of the two add to $\tfrac{193}{144}-1=\tfrac{49}{144}$, and
their product is
$\tfrac12\big((\tfrac5{12})^2-\tfrac{49}{144}\big)=-\tfrac1{12}$. They solve
$12\lambda^2-5\lambda-1=0$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda&=\frac{5\pm\sqrt{73}}{24}\\
 &=0.564333,\;-0.147667.
\end{aligned}
$$

**(c)** All other eigenvalues have absolute value below $1$, so only the
component along $\vec u_1=\vec s/\|\vec s\|$ survives, and $\|\vec s\|^2=12$.
With $\vec s^{\mathsf T}\vec x=3\sqrt3+6+4\sqrt2=16.853006$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\vec u_1\vec u_1^{\mathsf T}\vec x=\frac{16.853006}{12}\,\vec s\\
&\;=1.404417\,\vec s\\
&\;=(2.432522,\;2.432522,\\
&\;\qquad2.808834,\;1.986146).
\end{aligned}
$$

**(d)** Write $\vec x$ minus its limit in the remaining orthonormal
eigenvectors. Each component is multiplied by its eigenvalue at every
layer, and none exceeds $0.564333$ in absolute value, so

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\big\|\hat{\mat A}^k\vec x-\vec u_1\vec u_1^{\mathsf T}\vec x\big\|\\
&\;\;\leq0.564333^k\cdot2.516217,
\end{aligned}
$$

where $2.516217$ is the starting distance. At $k=8$ the bound is
$0.025884$ and the actual distance $0.024757$: almost all of the remainder
lies along the second eigenvector.

</Solution>

<Answer>
Eigenvalues $1$, $(5+\sqrt{73})/24=0.564333$, $0$ and
$(5-\sqrt{73})/24=-0.147667$; limit $1.404417\,\vec s=(2.432522,\,2.432522,$ $2.808834,\,1.986146)$;
distance at most $0.564333^k\cdot2.516217$, which is $0.025884$ at
$k=8$, against an actual $0.024757$.
</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
The four eigenvalues add to $\tfrac{17}{12}$, the trace. Divided by
$\sqrt{\tilde d_v}$, every entry of the limit is $1.404417$: the nodes are
indistinguishable except through their degrees.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
Without self-loops the graph's normalised adjacency would have eigenvalue
$-1$ if the graph were bipartite; the paw is not, because it contains a
triangle. Learned weights between layers change the rate, and the bound
then needs their singular values as well.
</Breaks>

<Variation>
The zero eigenvalue means one layer removes the difference between nodes
$1$ and $2$ completely, which is why
[I.14.B01](/elementa/neural-networks/gnn/problems/b01) found them equal.
Any graph with two nodes sharing a closed neighbourhood and a degree has
such an eigenvector.
</Variation>
