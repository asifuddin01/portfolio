Use the model of [I.13.B01](/elementa/neural-networks/vae/problems/b01):
$\vec w=\sqrt3\,\vec u_1$, $\sigma^2=1$, $\vec x=(1,2)$, so
$\vec w^{\mathsf T}\vec x=2\sqrt3$ and $\|\vec w\|^2=3$. The encoder returns
$m=0.5$ and $s=0.5$, and the draw is $\varepsilon=0.6$. Compute the
one-draw gradient of the ELBO with respect to $m$ and $s$, then the exact
gradient with respect to $m$.

<Hint>
The reconstruction term is $-\|\vec x-\vec wz\|^2/2$ plus a constant, with
$z=m+s\varepsilon$. The KL term is $\tfrac12(m^2+s^2-1-\log s^2)$.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

The code is $z=0.5+0.5\cdot0.6=0.8$. The derivative of the reconstruction
term with respect to $z$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec w^{\mathsf T}(\vec x-\vec wz)
 &=2\sqrt3-3\cdot0.8\\
 &=1.064102.
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $\partial z/\partial m=1$ and $\partial z/\partial s=\varepsilon$,
and the KL has derivatives $m$ and $s-1/s$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial}{\partial m}&=1.064102-0.5=0.564102,\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial s}&=1.064102\cdot0.6-(0.5-2)\\
 &=2.138461.
\end{aligned}
$$

The exact gradient with respect to $m$ averages over $\varepsilon$. The
reconstruction derivative is linear in $z$, so its average is its value at
$z=m$: $2\sqrt3-1.5=1.964102$. Subtracting the KL's $0.5$ gives
$1.464102$. The single draw underestimated it by $0.9$, because
$\varepsilon=0.6$ moved the code above its mean; an $\varepsilon$ of
$-0.6$ would have overestimated it by the same amount.

Both one-draw derivatives point uphill. An unbiased estimate need not be
close; it needs only to be right on average, which is all stochastic
gradient ascent requires.

</SolutionToggle>
