For the path $1$–$2$–$3$, write $\hat{\mat A}$ of (I.14.5) and apply it to
the signal $(1,0,0)$, a unit at one end. Where has the signal gone after one
layer?

<Hint>
With self-loops the degrees are $2$, $3$ and $2$, and
$\hat A_{vu}=1/\sqrt{\tilde d_v\tilde d_u}$ for neighbours and for $u=v$.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

The matrix is

$$
\hat{\mat A}=\begin{bmatrix}
\tfrac12&\tfrac1{\sqrt6}&0\\[2pt]
\tfrac1{\sqrt6}&\tfrac13&\tfrac1{\sqrt6}\\[2pt]
0&\tfrac1{\sqrt6}&\tfrac12
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Its first column is the output for the input $(1,0,0)$:
$(0.5,\;0.408248,\;0)$. Half of the unit stays at node $1$, part moves to its
neighbour, and node $3$, two hops away, receives nothing. A second layer
would reach it, as I.14.T2 says.

The output's entries add to $0.908248$, not $1$: the symmetric
normalisation does not preserve sums. It preserves the vector of square
roots of degrees, $(\sqrt2,\sqrt3,\sqrt2)$, which it maps to itself.

</SolutionToggle>
