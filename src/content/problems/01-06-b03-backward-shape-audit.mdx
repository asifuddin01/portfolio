<Statement>
Tabulate the shape of every tensor in both directions through a batched MLP,
and say where the batch dimension goes. One of the two gradients in (I.6.4)
sums over the batch and the other does not; the shapes say which, before any
calculus is done.
</Statement>

<Given>
The network of I.5.B03 — widths $(784, 512, 256, 128, 10)$, four affine layers,
ReLU after the first three, no activation on the head — now run on a batch of
$B = 32$ examples, so the input is
$\mat{A}^{(0)} \in \R^{32 \times 784}$. The loss is a scalar: a mean over the
batch.
</Given>

<Find>
For every layer $\ell$: the shapes of $\mat{A}^{(\ell)}$, $\Delta^{(\ell)}$,
$\partial L / \partial \mat{W}^{(\ell)}$ and $\partial L / \partial \vec{b}^{(\ell)}$.
Then the total activation memory kept for the backward pass, in fp32.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Two rules settle every row. A gradient has the shape of the thing it
differentiates, so parameter gradients cannot carry a batch dimension — the
parameters do not have one. An adjoint has the shape of the value it belongs
to, so activation adjoints must carry it. Where those two rules disagree, a
sum appears, and that is the only place a sum can appear.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**Step 1 — forward shapes.** The batch dimension is untouched by every layer:

| $\ell$ | $\mat{W}^{(\ell)}$ | $\mat{Z}^{(\ell)}$ | $\mat{A}^{(\ell)}$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $784 \times 512$ | $32 \times 512$ | $32 \times 512$ |
| 2 | $512 \times 256$ | $32 \times 256$ | $32 \times 256$ |
| 3 | $256 \times 128$ | $32 \times 128$ | $32 \times 128$ |
| 4 | $128 \times 10$ | $32 \times 10$ | $32 \times 10$ |

**Step 2 — adjoints.** $\Delta^{(\ell)} = \partial L / \partial \mat{Z}^{(\ell)}$
has the shape of $\mat{Z}^{(\ell)}$, so it carries the batch:
$32 \times 512$, $32 \times 256$, $32 \times 128$, $32 \times 10$.

**Step 3 — the weight gradient absorbs the batch.** Per example the gradient is
the outer product of (I.6.4). Over a batch it is the sum of those outer
products, which is a single matrix product:

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial \mat{W}^{(\ell)}}
= (\mat{A}^{(\ell-1)})^{\mathsf{T}} \Delta^{(\ell)}
$$

with shapes $(d_{\ell-1} \times 32)(32 \times d_\ell) \to d_{\ell-1} \times d_\ell$.
The 32 is contracted away — it is the inner dimension of the product. The
summation over the batch is not an extra step anybody writes; it is what the
matrix product already does.

| $\ell$ | $\partial L / \partial \mat{W}^{(\ell)}$ | $\partial L / \partial \vec{b}^{(\ell)}$ |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $784 \times 512$ | $1 \times 512$ |
| 2 | $512 \times 256$ | $1 \times 256$ |
| 3 | $256 \times 128$ | $1 \times 128$ |
| 4 | $128 \times 10$ | $1 \times 10$ |

**Step 4 — the bias gradient needs an explicit sum.** Per example
$\partial L / \partial \vec{b}^{(\ell)} = \delta^{(\ell)}$, of shape
$1 \times d_\ell$. Over a batch there is no matrix product to hide the
contraction in, so it is written out:

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial \vec{b}^{(\ell)}} = \sum_{n=1}^{B} \Delta^{(\ell)}_{n,:}
$$

This is the asymmetry the Statement promised. Both gradients sum over the
batch; the weight gradient's sum is the inner dimension of a matrix product and
the bias gradient's is a reduction along an axis. Forgetting the second is a
common implementation error and it is caught immediately by a shape check —
$32 \times 512$ where $1 \times 512$ was required.

**Step 5 — what must be kept.** The backward pass consumes
$\mat{A}^{(\ell-1)}$ for (I.6.4) and $\mat{Z}^{(\ell)}$ for $\varphi'$. For
ReLU the mask $\mathbb{1}[\mat{Z} > 0]$ suffices, but counting stored floats:

$$
32 \times (784 + 512 + 256 + 128 + 10) = 32 \times 1690 = 54\,080
$$

At 4 bytes each that is $216\,320$ bytes, about $0.22$ MB.

**Step 6 — compare with the parameters.** I.5.B03 counted $567\,434$
parameters, $2.27$ MB in fp32. At $B = 32$ the activations are a tenth of the
parameters. At $B = 1024$ they are $1\,730\,560$ floats, $6.92$ MB — three
times the parameters. Activation memory is linear in the batch and parameter
memory is constant in it, so which term dominates is a property of the batch
size and not of the model.

</Solution>

<Answer>

Adjoints carry the batch: $32 \times d_\ell$ at every layer. Parameter gradients
do not: $\partial L/\partial \mat{W}^{(\ell)}$ is $d_{\ell-1} \times d_\ell$ and
$\partial L/\partial \vec{b}^{(\ell)}$ is $1 \times d_\ell$. The weight
gradient's batch sum is the inner dimension of
$(\mat{A}^{(\ell-1)})^{\mathsf{T}} \Delta^{(\ell)}$; the bias gradient's is an
explicit reduction over rows.

Stored activations at $B = 32$: $54\,080$ floats, $216\,320$ bytes, $0.22$ MB —
against $2.27$ MB of parameters. At $B = 1024$: $6.92$ MB, three times the
parameters.

</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
**No parameter gradient carries a batch dimension.** It cannot: the parameter
does not have one, and a gradient has the shape of what it differentiates. Any
implementation returning a $32 \times 784 \times 512$ weight gradient has
computed per-example gradients and not yet reduced them — sometimes deliberately,
which is what per-sample gradient methods want, but never by accident.

**Every adjoint does carry it.** Different examples have different activations
and therefore different adjoints, and they stay separate until a parameter is
reached. The batch dimension survives exactly as far as the parameters and no
further.

**The two contractions are the same contraction.** Both gradients sum $B$ terms;
only the notation differs. Writing the bias as a row of ones times
$\Delta^{(\ell)}$ makes them identical in form, which is a good way to remember
that the missing sum is missing rather than unnecessary.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
The audit counts the activations this architecture stores, and that count is a
floor rather than an estimate. A real framework also holds the gradient buffer
for every parameter, the optimiser state — two more copies for Adam — and
workspace for the matrix products. It may also keep both $\mat{Z}^{(\ell)}$ and
$\mat{A}^{(\ell)}$ where one would do, since whether $\varphi$ can be inverted
from its output is a per-operation question the memory planner may not answer.

The larger omission is that the numbers here are for an MLP, where activations
are $B \times d$. For a convolutional network they are $B \times C \times H
\times W$ and for a transformer the attention matrices are $B \times h \times
T \times T$, quadratic in sequence length. The shape of the bill changes
completely with the architecture even though the rule producing it — keep what
the backward pass will read — does not.
</Breaks>

<Variation>
Run the same audit with the batch dimension last, so activations are
$d_\ell \times B$. Every shape transposes and every product reverses. Which of
the two batch contractions is now the explicit one, and which is hidden inside
a matrix product?
</Variation>
