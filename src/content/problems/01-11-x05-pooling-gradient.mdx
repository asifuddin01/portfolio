A four-entry pooling window is $(1,3,2,0)$ and its upstream derivative is
four. Find max- and average-pooling input gradients.
What changes if the window is $(1,3,3,0)$?

<Hint>
The first maximum is unique; the second is tied.
</Hint>
<SolutionToggle>

The unique max routes the upstream derivative to the second entry:
$(0,4,0,0)$. Average pooling sends $4/4=1$ to every entry,
giving $(1,1,1,1)$.

At the tie, max pooling has no unique ordinary derivative. A valid
subgradient has the form $(0,4\alpha,4(1-\alpha),0)$ for
$0\leq\alpha\leq1$. An implementation storing one argmax can choose one
endpoint. A central difference at the tie need not reproduce that choice.

All these subgradients sum to four: increasing every window entry by the
same amount increases its maximum by that amount. The reproduction check
uses finite differences only at the unique maximum.

</SolutionToggle>
