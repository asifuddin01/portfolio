A node has $100$ neighbours whose features have mean $\mu$ and variance
$\sigma^2=1$ around it. A sampled layer averages $s$ of them, drawn without
replacement. Show that the sampled mean is unbiased, find its variance,
and evaluate it for $s=10$, $25$ and $100$.

<Hint>
Each neighbour is included with probability $s/100$. For sampling without
replacement from a finite population of size $N$, the variance of the
sample mean is $(\sigma^2/s)(N-s)/(N-1)$.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

Write the sampled mean as $\tfrac1s\sum_uI_ux_u$, where $I_u$ is $1$ if
neighbour $u$ is drawn. Each $I_u$ has mean $s/N$, so the expectation is
$\tfrac1s\cdot\tfrac sN\sum_ux_u=\mu$: unbiased.

The draws are not independent, since choosing one neighbour makes the
others slightly less likely. The covariance of two indicators is
$-\tfrac sN\cdot\tfrac{N-s}{N(N-1)}$, and summing gives the finite-population
variance

$$
\operatorname{Var}=\frac{\sigma^2}{s}\cdot\frac{N-s}{N-1}.
$$

With $N=100$ and $\sigma^2=1$: $0.090909$ at $s=10$, $0.030303$ at $s=25$,
and $0$ at $s=100$, where every neighbour is used and the mean is exact.
Independent draws would have given $0.1$ and $0.04$; the factor
$(N-s)/(N-1)$ is what sampling without replacement saves.

The layer stays unbiased in expectation, but a nonlinearity applied after
it does not average out the noise exactly, and stacking sampled layers
compounds it. Sampling buys a fixed cost per node at the price of a noisy,
slightly biased deep network, a trade Hamilton, Ying and Leskovec (2017)
found worth making on large graphs.

</SolutionToggle>
