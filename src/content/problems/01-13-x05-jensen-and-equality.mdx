Derive the ELBO from Jensen's inequality, and state exactly when the
inequality is an equality. Then check the condition for the model of
[I.13.B01](/elementa/neural-networks/vae/problems/b01): show that
$\log p(\vec x,z)-\log p(z\mid\vec x)$ takes the same value at every $z$,
and find it.

<Hint>
The logarithm is strictly concave, so $\log\E[Y]=\E[\log Y]$ only when $Y$
is constant.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

Write $Y=p(\vec x,\vec z)/q(\vec z\mid\vec x)$ with $\vec z\sim q$. Its mean
is $\int p(\vec x,\vec z)\,d\vec z=p(\vec x)$, so by Jensen's inequality
(<ApparatusRef ref="0.IT.05" />)

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log p(\vec x)&=\log\E_q[Y]\\
 &\geq\E_q[\log Y]=\operatorname{ELBO}(q).
\end{aligned}
$$

Because $\log$ is strictly concave, equality holds exactly when $Y$ is the
same number for every $\vec z$ that $q$ can draw. Then
$q(\vec z\mid\vec x)\propto p(\vec x,\vec z)$, and normalising gives
$q=p(\vec x,\vec z)/p(\vec x)$, the posterior. The constant is $p(\vec x)$.

For the linear model, with $q$ the posterior, $Y$ is
$p(\vec x,z)/p(z\mid\vec x)$, which by Bayes' rule is $p(\vec x)$ at every
$z$. The script evaluates
$\log p(z)+\log p(\vec x\mid z)-\log\mathcal N(z;0.866025,0.25)$ at
$z=-1$, $0$, $0.5$ and $2$ and gets $-3.531024$ each time, the
log-evidence.

For any other $q$ the ratio varies with $z$, and the spread of
$\log Y$ is what Jensen's inequality charges. The first derivation in
proposition 2 shows that the charge is exactly
$\KL(q\,\|\,p(\vec z\mid\vec x))$.

</SolutionToggle>
