Prove that a GRU in the form (I.10.7) keeps every coordinate of $\vec h_t$ in
$[-1,1]$ for all $t$ if $\vec h_0$ starts there. Then show the premise
matters: start a coordinate at $h_0=3$ with $z=0.5$ and candidate $0$.

<Hint>
A convex combination of two numbers lies between them.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

Fix a coordinate and write $h_t=z\,h_{t-1}+(1-z)n$ with $z\in(0,1)$ and
$n=\tanh(\cdot)\in(-1,1)$. By the triangle inequality,

$$
|h_t|\leq z|h_{t-1}|+(1-z)|n|\leq\max(|h_{t-1}|,|n|).
$$

If $|h_{t-1}|\leq1$, the right-hand side is at most one, so $|h_t|\leq1$.
Induction on $t$ from $|h_0|\leq1$ completes the proof. In fact $|h_t|<1$ for
$t\geq1$, since $1-z>0$ and $|n|<1$.

The argument uses the starting value. With $h_0=3$, $z=0.5$ and $n=0$ the
states are $3$, $1.5$, $0.75$, $0.375$: outside the box for one step, then
inside. The same inequality shows $|h_t|\leq\max(|h_0|,1)$ in general.

An LSTM has no such bound on $\vec c_t$, which is why I.10.T3 needs
forget values below one for the cell.

</SolutionToggle>
