$\mathrm{ReLU}(z) = \max(0, z)$ is not differentiable at $z = 0$. Compute the
left and right limits of the difference quotient there, say what a framework
returns instead, and explain why a chapter built on the chain rule can proceed
anyway.

<Hint>
The chain rule requires differentiability at the point evaluated. Ask how often
a floating-point pre-activation is exactly zero, and what happens when it is.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**The limits disagree.**

$$
\lim_{h \to 0^-} \frac{\max(0, h) - 0}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0^-} \frac{0}{h} = 0,
\qquad
\lim_{h \to 0^+} \frac{h}{h} = 1
$$

No derivative exists at $0$. Every value in $[0, 1]$ is a subgradient, and the
subdifferential is that whole interval.

**What is returned.** PyTorch, TensorFlow and JAX all return $0$. That is a
choice among the valid subgradients, not a derivation, and the frameworks differ
in principle even where they agree in practice — nothing in the mathematics
selects $0$ over $1$ or $1/2$.

**Why it does not matter much.** The set of inputs producing exactly $z = 0$ has
measure zero in the parameter space, and in floating point it is reached only by
exact cancellation. Training touches it approximately never, and when it does
the choice moves one coordinate of one gradient by at most the adjoint arriving
there.

**Why it is not nothing.** Three cases make it visible. A network initialised
with all-zero weights has every pre-activation exactly zero, so the convention
decides whether anything moves at all — with $0$ returned, nothing does, and the
network never leaves its initialisation. A ReLU unit driven far negative has
slope exactly $0$ everywhere it is evaluated, not merely at one point, and stops
receiving gradient permanently: the dead-unit failure, which is a statement
about the whole negative half-line rather than about the kink. And when a
learning rate is large enough to jump across the kink each step, the loss can
oscillate in a way that looks like instability but is the function being
genuinely non-smooth.

**The honest framing.** Backpropagation as derived in I.6.B02 assumes
differentiability at the evaluated point, and ReLU violates it on a set of
measure zero. What is computed is a subgradient, descent methods for convex
problems have a theory for subgradients, and this problem is not convex — so
the practice is better supported by its results than by its theorems. Saying so
is more useful than pretending the kink is not there.
