<Statement>
Compare full backpropagation with a one-step truncation that detaches the state
after the second input.
</Statement>

<Given>
Use I.9.B01's linear scalar model:
$(u,w,b,v,c)=(1,1/2,0,2,0)$, inputs $(1,2,0)$, initial state zero,
and final target one. The full forward states are $(1,5/2,5/4)$.
There is only a final output loss.
</Given>

<Find>
The prediction and loss in both computations, and their derivatives with
respect to $w$ and $u$. Explain the correct finite-difference check for each.
</Find>

<Strategy>
After detaching $h_2$, replace its computation by the constant $5/2$.
Differentiate the remaining one-step function and compare it with I.9.B02.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

The full graph has $h_3=u(w^2+2w)$ at $b=0$.
At the stated parameters its gradients are
$\partial_w\mathcal L=9$ and $\partial_u\mathcal L=15/4$.

The detached graph instead evaluates

$$
\begin{aligned}
h_3&=u\cdot0+w\cdot(5/2)+b,\\
z_3&=vh_3+c.
\end{aligned}
$$

At the original parameters this gives the same $h_3=5/4$, $z_3=5/2$
and loss $9/8$. Its last-state derivative is still $\delta_3=3$, but
there is no earlier parameter path:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_w\mathcal L_{\mathrm{trunc}}&=3(5/2)=15/2,\\
\partial_u\mathcal L_{\mathrm{trunc}}&=3\cdot0=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The derivative with respect to the boundary value itself would be
$w\delta_3=3/2$. Detachment stops it from propagating into the calculation
that produced that value.

To finite-difference the full graph, perturb $w$ or $u$ and recompute every
state. To finite-difference the truncated graph, first compute the boundary
state at the original parameters, freeze it at $5/2$, then perturb the
parameters only in the final chunk.

Recomputing the boundary during the truncated check reattaches the dependency
you intended to remove. Its result would be the full gradient, not a failure
of the truncated backward implementation.

</Solution>

<Answer>
Both predictions are $2.5$ and both losses are $1.125$.
Full $(\partial_w,\partial_u)=(9,3.75)$;
truncated $(\partial_w,\partial_u)=(7.5,0)$.
</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
Resetting instead of detaching would set the boundary to zero.
Because $x_3=b=c=0$, that would give prediction zero and loss $1/2$,
a different forward computation. Detach preserves the value; reset replaces it.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
This comparison uses one fixed parameter setting and one final loss.
Chunk-wise updates may also change parameters between chunks; they are
not identical to a single full-sequence update even if boundary derivatives
happen to be small.
</Breaks>

<Variation>
Keep the final two time steps after detaching $h_1$. The $w$ gradient happens
to remain nine because the removed first-step contribution is zero, but the
$u$ gradient drops from $3.75$ to $3$. Matching one parameter's derivative
does not establish that the whole backward graph matches.
</Variation>
