Derive the gradients of a loss $\mathcal L$ with respect to $\mat W$ and $\mat H$
for the linear layer $\mat Y=\hat{\mat A}\mat H\mat W$, given
$\mat G=\partial\mathcal L/\partial\mat Y$. Then evaluate them for the paw
graph with scalar features $\vec x=(1,2,3,4)$, weight $w=0.5$, target
$\vec t=(0,0,1,1)$ and $\mathcal L=\tfrac12\|\vec y-\vec t\|^2$.

<Hint>
Treat $\hat{\mat A}\mat H$ as one matrix and use the chain rule for matrix
products (<ApparatusRef ref="0.MC.07" />). $\hat{\mat A}$ is symmetric.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

With $\mat Z=\hat{\mat A}\mat H$, $\mat Y=\mat Z\mat W$, so
$\partial\mathcal L/\partial\mat W=\mat Z^{\mathsf T}\mat G=(\hat{\mat A}\mat H)^{\mathsf T}\mat G$
and $\partial\mathcal L/\partial\mat Z=\mat G\mat W^{\mathsf T}$. Then
$\partial\mathcal L/\partial\mat H=\hat{\mat A}^{\mathsf T}\mat G\mat W^{\mathsf T}=\hat{\mat A}\mat G\mat W^{\mathsf T}$.
The backward pass through a graph convolution is another graph
convolution, applied to the gradients: each node receives a degree-weighted
average of its neighbours' errors.

For the numbers, $\hat{\mat A}\vec x=(1.866025,\,1.866025,$
$3.030239,\,3.060660)$ from I.14.B01, so

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec y&=(0.933013,\;0.933013,\\
 &\qquad1.515119,\;1.530330),
\end{aligned}
$$

the residual is $\vec y-\vec t=(0.933013,\,0.933013,$
$0.515119,\,0.530330)$, and
$\mathcal L=1.143812$. The weight's gradient is the dot product of
$\hat{\mat A}\vec x$ with the residual, $6.666146$. The input's gradient is
$0.5\,\hat{\mat A}(\vec y-\vec t)=(0.385355,\,0.385355,$
$0.427478,\,0.223644)$.

Nodes $1$ and $2$ receive the same gradient, as they produced the same
output: one layer cannot tell them apart in either direction. The script
confirms all five derivatives by central finite differences.

</SolutionToggle>
