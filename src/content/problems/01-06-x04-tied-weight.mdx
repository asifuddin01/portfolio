A scalar weight $w$ is used twice in the same graph:

$$
u = wx, \qquad v = wu, \qquad L = \tfrac{1}{2}v^2
$$

Compute $\partial L/\partial w$ correctly. Then compute what a backward pass
would produce if each use *assigned* into the gradient slot instead of adding to
it, and say why the resulting number is not merely inaccurate.

<Hint>
$w$ has two consumers. (I.6.2) says its adjoint is a sum over them, and an
assignment keeps whichever term arrives last.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**Correct.** Substituting, $v = w^2x$ and $L = \tfrac{1}{2}w^4x^2$, so

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial w} = 2w^3x^2
$$

**By the graph.** $\bar{v} = v = w^2x$. The node $v = wu$ has two producers:

$$
\bar{w}\big|_{v} = \bar{v}u = w^2x \cdot wx = w^3x^2, \qquad \bar{u} = \bar{v}w = w^3x
$$

The node $u = wx$ then contributes

$$
\bar{w}\big|_{u} = \bar{u}x = w^3x^2
$$

By (I.6.2) the adjoint of $w$ is the sum of the contributions from its
consumers:

$$
\bar{w} = w^3x^2 + w^3x^2 = 2w^3x^2
$$

which matches the direct computation.

**With assignment instead of accumulation.** Whichever contribution is written
last survives, and each is exactly $w^3x^2$ — half the truth. The gradient is
not noisy, not biased in a complicated way, and not sometimes right: it is
consistently a factor of two short.

**Why that is worse than it sounds.** A gradient scaled by a constant is still
a descent direction, so training does not diverge or produce obvious garbage.
It converges, more slowly, and looks like a learning-rate problem. Now make the
two uses asymmetric — an embedding matrix that is also the output projection, a
convolutional filter applied at 4096 positions, a recurrent matrix at 512
timesteps — and the surviving term is one of many, in a proportion that varies
per example. That is not a rescaled gradient; it is the gradient of a different
function, and it descends confidently to the wrong place.

**Which is why frameworks accumulate.** `.grad` is added into, never assigned,
and the mirror-image bug follows immediately: forget to zero it between steps
and every step's gradient is the sum of all steps so far. Both errors come from
the same line of (I.6.2), read in opposite directions.

</SolutionToggle>
