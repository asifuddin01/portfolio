<Statement>
A language model emits logits of shape $(B, T, |V|)$. Trace every shape on the
path from that tensor to the single number the optimiser differentiates. Compute
the memory the logits occupy, and determine what masking does to the reduction —
including the size of the error if it is done wrongly.
</Statement>

<Given>
Batch $B = 4$, sequence length $T = 512$, vocabulary $|V| = 32{,}000$. Targets
are token indices, shape $(B, T)$. Fifteen per cent of positions are padding and
must be excluded. Cross-entropy is applied per position and then reduced to one
scalar.
</Given>

<Find>
The shape after each stage; the element count and memory of the logits in fp32
and bf16; and the two possible reductions under masking, with the discrepancy
between them.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Follow the rank down. The logits are rank $3$; the loss is rank $0$. Two things
remove a rank — indexing away the vocabulary axis, and reducing away the
position axes — and the whole trace is deciding where each happens.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**Step 1 — the incoming pair.**

$$
\text{logits} : (B, T, |V|) = (4, 512, 32000),
\qquad
\text{targets} : (B, T) = (4, 512)
$$

The shapes do not match, and they should not. The targets are **indices**, not
one-hot vectors: integers in $[0, |V|)$, one per position. A one-hot target of
shape $(4, 512, 32000)$ would hold the same information in
$65{,}536{,}000$ numbers instead of $2{,}048$, of which all but $2{,}048$ are
zero. Frameworks index rather than multiply for exactly this reason.

**Step 2 — the element count.**

$$B \times T \times |V| = 4 \times 512 \times 32{,}000 = 65{,}536{,}000$$

**Step 3 — flatten the position axes.** Cross-entropy treats every position
independently, so the batch and time axes carry no meaning for it and can be
merged:

$$
(4, 512, 32000) \longrightarrow (2048,\ 32000),
\qquad
(4, 512) \longrightarrow (2048,)
$$

$2048 = 4 \times 512$, and the element count is unchanged — a reshape moves no
data. Every framework's cross-entropy expects exactly this two-dimensional form,
which is why calling it on rank-3 logits requires an explicit reshape.

**Step 4 — the per-position loss.** For each of the $2{,}048$ rows, take the
row's $32{,}000$ logits and its one target index and produce one number:

$$(2048, 32000) \times (2048,) \longrightarrow (2048,)$$

The vocabulary axis is gone. This is where $-z_c + \log\sum_j e^{z_j}$ is
evaluated per row — never by forming probabilities first, for the reasons of
I.4.B04.

**Step 5 — the reduction.** $(2048,) \longrightarrow ()$, rank $0$. This is the
one number $\nabla_\theta$ is taken of, and Step 7 shows it is the step most
often got wrong.

**The complete trace.**

| Stage | Shape | Values |
|---|---|---|
| logits | $(4, 512, 32000)$ | $65{,}536{,}000$ |
| targets | $(4, 512)$ | $2{,}048$ |
| flattened logits | $(2048, 32000)$ | $65{,}536{,}000$ |
| flattened targets | $(2048,)$ | $2{,}048$ |
| per-token loss | $(2048,)$ | $2{,}048$ |
| reduced loss | $()$ | $1$ |

**Step 6 — memory.**

$$
\text{fp32:}\quad 65{,}536{,}000 \times 4\ \text{bytes} = 262.1\ \text{MB}
$$
$$
\text{bf16:}\quad 65{,}536{,}000 \times 2\ \text{bytes} = 131.1\ \text{MB}
$$

And if softmax probabilities are materialised as a separate tensor of the same
shape — which the naive route of I.4.B04 requires — that doubles to
$524.3$ MB in fp32 and $262.1$ MB in bf16.

**This single tensor is often the largest in the model.** At $B = 4$ it already
rivals the activations of an entire transformer block, and it grows linearly in
both $B$ and $|V|$. It is why the loss is computed in chunks over the batch for
large vocabularies, and why $|V|$ appears in memory budgets as prominently as
depth does.

**Step 7 — masking, and the reduction it changes.** With $15\%$ padding:

$$
\text{total positions} = 2{,}048,
\qquad
\text{valid positions} = \text{round}(2048 \times 0.85) = 1{,}741
$$

Padded positions contribute a loss of $0$, having been masked. But there are two
different things one can then divide by.

*Mean over all positions.* $\displaystyle \frac{1}{2048}\sum_i \ell_i$ — divides
by the number of slots.

*Mean over valid positions.* $\displaystyle \frac{1}{1741}\sum_i \ell_i$ —
divides by the number of real tokens.

The ratio is

$$\frac{2048}{1741} = 1.1763$$

so **the first understates the loss by $17.6\%$** relative to the second.

**Why this matters more than it looks.** The reported loss is wrong by a fixed
factor, which is merely embarrassing. The *gradient* is wrong by the same factor,
which is an unintended $17.6\%$ reduction in effective learning rate. And the
factor is not fixed across batches: it depends on how much padding each batch
happens to contain, so the effective learning rate fluctuates from step to step
with the batch's sequence-length distribution. Sorting examples by length into
buckets — done for speed — changes the padding fraction systematically, and so
silently changes the learning rate schedule.

Dividing by the valid count is the correct choice, and it is not the default in
every framework.

</Solution>

<Answer>
$$
(4, 512, 32000) \to (2048, 32000) \to (2048,) \to ()
$$

Logits hold $65{,}536{,}000$ values: $262.1$ MB in fp32, $131.1$ MB in bf16, and
double that if probabilities are materialised too.

With $15\%$ padding, $1{,}741$ of $2{,}048$ positions are valid. Reducing over
all positions rather than valid ones understates the loss and the gradient by a
factor of $2048/1741 = 1.1763$, or $\mathbf{17.6\%}$.
</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
**The reshape conserves elements.** $4 \times 512 \times 32000 = 65{,}536{,}000$
and $2048 \times 32000 = 65{,}536{,}000$. A reshape that changed the count would
be a copy or a truncation, not a reshape.

**Rank falls exactly twice.** Rank $3 \to 2$ by the flatten, $2 \to 1$ by
indexing away the vocabulary, $1 \to 0$ by the reduction. Three drops, three
identifiable operations, no rank lost silently.

**bf16 is exactly half of fp32.** $262.1 / 131.1 = 2.0$, as two bytes against
four requires.

**The masking ratio bounds correctly.** $1 \le 2048/1741 \le 1/0.85 = 1.1765$.
The computed $1.1763$ sits just inside, the difference being the rounding of
$1741.$ At zero padding the ratio would be exactly $1$ and the two reductions
would agree — which is why this bug is invisible on fixed-length data.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
The trace assumes every position has exactly one target. Two common cases break
it, and both are worth recognising by their shapes.

*Soft targets.* With label smoothing or distillation the target is a full
distribution, shape $(2048, 32000)$ rather than $(2048,)$. The indexing step of
Step 4 becomes a contraction over the vocabulary axis, the targets now cost the
same memory as the logits, and the total doubles again.

*Multi-label.* When a position may carry several correct classes, softmax is
wrong outright — it forces the outputs to sum to one, and $K$ independent
sigmoids with binary cross-entropy is the right structure instead. The shape
$(2048, 32000)$ survives but the reduction is over $|V|$ as well as over
positions, and the loss is a sum of $32{,}000$ binary terms per position rather
than one categorical term.
</Breaks>
