<Statement>
Show that applying the reset gate before or after the recurrent matrix gives
different GRUs, and find exactly when the two agree.
</Statement>

<Given>
A two-unit GRU step with zero input contribution and zero candidate biases.
The recurrent candidate matrix swaps coordinates,
$\mat W_n=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}$. The preceding state is
$\vec h_{t-1}=(0.5,-0.5)$. The reset pre-activations are $(\ln3,-\ln3)$ and the
update pre-activations are zero. Use PyTorch's update,
$\vec h_t=(1-\vec z_t)\odot\vec n_t+\vec z_t\odot\vec h_{t-1}$, for both.
</Given>

<Find>
The gates, both candidates and both new states; then a condition on
$\mat W_n$ and $\vec r_t$ under which the two placements agree for every state.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Compute $\mat W(\vec r\odot\vec h)$ and $\vec r\odot(\mat W\vec h)$ separately.
For the general condition, write both as products with $\operatorname{diag}(\vec r)$
and compare entries.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

The gates are $\vec r=(\sigma(\ln3),\sigma(-\ln3))=(0.75,0.25)$ and
$\vec z=(0.5,0.5)$. The update gate is the same in both models, so any
difference comes from the candidate.

**Reset before the matrix,** as in Cho and colleagues:
$\vec r\odot\vec h=(0.375,-0.125)$, and the swap gives $(-0.125,0.375)$. Hence

$$
\vec n^{\text{before}}=(\tanh(-0.125),\tanh(0.375))=(-0.124353,0.358357).
$$

**Reset after the matrix,** as in PyTorch: $\mat W\vec h=(-0.5,0.5)$, then
$\vec r\odot\mat W\vec h=(-0.375,0.125)$, and

$$
\vec n^{\text{after}}=(-0.358357,0.124353).
$$

With $z=1/2$, the new states are the averages of candidate and old state:
$(0.187823,-0.070821)$ before and $(0.070821,-0.187823)$ after. Same weights,
same state, same gates, and every coordinate differs by more than $0.1$.

**When they agree.** The candidates' recurrent parts are
$\mat W\operatorname{diag}(\vec r)\vec h$ and $\operatorname{diag}(\vec r)\mat W\vec h$.
They agree for every $\vec h$ exactly when the matrices are equal. Their
$(j,k)$ entries are $W_{jk}r_k$ and $r_jW_{jk}$, so the condition is

$$
W_{jk}(r_k-r_j)=0\quad\text{for all }j,k.
$$

A weight joining two coordinates may be nonzero only if their reset values
are equal. A uniform reset, $\vec r=\rho\vec 1$, satisfies it for every
$\mat W$; a diagonal $\mat W$ satisfies it for every $\vec r$. The swap matrix
with unequal resets satisfies neither, which is why this example separates them.

Biases add a second difference. In the after form the recurrent bias
$\vec b_{hn}$ is inside the reset, $\vec r\odot(\mat W\vec h+\vec b_{hn})$,
so the reset scales it. In the before form a bias added outside is not scaled.
Even scalar models differ when that bias is nonzero.

</Solution>

<Answer>
$\vec r=(0.75,0.25)$, $\vec z=(0.5,0.5)$. Before: candidate $(-0.124353,0.358357)$,
state $(0.187823,-0.070821)$. After: candidate $(-0.358357,0.124353)$, state
$(0.070821,-0.187823)$. They agree for all states exactly when
$W_{jk}(r_k-r_j)=0$ for all $j,k$.
</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
The swap matrix moves each coordinate's reset to the other side. Reset before:
coordinate one's value, scaled by $0.75$, lands in coordinate two. Reset after:
coordinate two's value lands in coordinate one and is scaled there by
$0.75$. The mirror-image answers are what that bookkeeping predicts.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
The commutation test is for one step with fixed gates. Over a sequence the
two models also produce different gates, because the gates read states that
have already diverged. Load a checkpoint into the placement it was trained
with. The update-gate convention can be converted by negating weights; this
example gives no such conversion for the reset placement.
</Breaks>

<Variation>
Make the reset uniform at $(0.5,0.5)$ and keep the swap matrix. Both forms now
give the candidate $(\tanh(-0.25),\tanh(0.25))=(-0.244919,0.244919)$.
</Variation>
