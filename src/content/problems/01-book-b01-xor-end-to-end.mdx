<Statement>
Show that no perceptron computes XOR, that a two-unit ReLU network does,
and then backpropagate through that network after one of its weights has
been disturbed and take a gradient step.
</Statement>

<Given>
The four XOR examples: $(0,0)\mapsto0$, $(0,1)\mapsto1$, $(1,0)\mapsto1$,
$(1,1)\mapsto0$. The network, with $s=x_1+x_2$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
h_1&=\operatorname{relu}(s),\\
h_2&=\operatorname{relu}(s-1),\\
y&=h_1+v_2h_2,\\
v_2&=-2.
\end{aligned}
$$

The loss is the mean squared error over the four examples. For the second
part, $v_2=-1.8$ and the step size is $1$.
</Given>

<Find>
Why no threshold unit $\mathbf 1[w_1x_1+w_2x_2+b>0]$ fits the data; the
network's outputs; after disturbing $v_2$, the loss and the gradients with
respect to $v_2$, the bias of $h_2$ and the bias of $h_1$; the new $v_2$.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Write the perceptron's four conditions as inequalities (I.2). For the
network, only the example $(1,1)$ has an error after the disturbance, so
the backward pass of I.6 runs through one example.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**No perceptron.** The examples require $b\leq0$ for $(0,0)$,
$w_2+b>0$ and $w_1+b>0$ for the two positives, and $w_1+w_2+b\leq0$ for
$(1,1)$. Adding the middle two gives $w_1+w_2+2b>0$, so
$w_1+w_2+b>-b\geq0$, contradicting the last. The positives and negatives
are not linearly separable (I.2.P04), and no learning rule changes that.

**The network.** At $s=0,1,1,2$ the hidden units are $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$,
$(1,0)$ and $(2,1)$, so

$$
\begin{aligned}
y&=(0,\;1,\;1,\;2-2)\\
 &=(0,1,1,0).
\end{aligned}
$$

The ReLU's kink at $s=1$ is what a linear map cannot supply (I.3.P01):
the network bends the line $s$ once and folds the fourth example back to
zero.

**Disturbed.** With $v_2=-1.8$ the last output is $2-1.8=0.2$ and the
others are unchanged. The loss is $0.2^2/4=0.01$. The output error is
$\delta=2(0.2-0)/4=0.1$ at $(1,1)$ and zero elsewhere, so

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial v_2}&=\delta\,h_2=0.1,\\
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial b_2}&=\delta\,v_2\operatorname{relu}'(1)\\
 &=-0.18,\\
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial b_1}&=\delta\operatorname{relu}'(2)=0.1.
\end{aligned}
$$

**One step.** Gradient descent moves $v_2$ to $-1.8-1\cdot0.1=-1.9$, back
towards $-2$. The gradient supplied the direction and the step size of $1$
was a choice (I.7.P01). Repeating the step halves the remaining error each
time: $-1.95$, then $-1.975$, approaching $-2$ without reaching it.

</Solution>

<Answer>
The four perceptron inequalities contradict each other. The network
outputs $(0,1,1,0)$. Disturbed: loss $0.01$, gradients $0.1$, $-0.18$ and
$0.1$; after one step $v_2=-1.9$.
</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
The gradient with respect to $v_2$ is positive because raising $v_2$ makes
the wrong output $0.2$ larger. The script confirms it with a central finite
difference.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
The network computes a function of $s$ alone, so it cannot represent the
other three Boolean functions of two inputs that need $x_1$ and $x_2$
separately. Two hidden units suffice for XOR because XOR is symmetric.
</Breaks>

<Variation>
With $v_2=-2$ every error is zero and so is every gradient: XOR is a global
minimum. Disturbing the bias of $h_2$ instead moves the kink, and then
several examples err at once.
</Variation>
