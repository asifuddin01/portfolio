Let the training set have $N$ examples, chosen with equal probability
$q(n)=1/N$, and let the encoder give $q(\vec z\mid n)$. Prove (I.13.9):
the average of $\KL(q(\vec z\mid n)\,\|\,p(\vec z))$ equals
$I_q(n;\vec z)+\KL(q(\vec z)\,\|\,p(\vec z))$, with
$q(\vec z)=\frac1N\sum_nq(\vec z\mid n)$. Deduce that a zero average KL
means a code that carries no information, and that the rate bounds the
information from above.

<Hint>
Insert $q(\vec z)$ into the log-ratio:
$\log\frac{q(\vec z\mid n)}{p(\vec z)}
=\log\frac{q(\vec z\mid n)}{q(\vec z)}+\log\frac{q(\vec z)}{p(\vec z)}$.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**The identity.** Average the split in the hint over $q(n,\vec z)=q(n)\,q(\vec z\mid n)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\frac1N\sum_n\KL\big(q(\vec z\mid n)\,\|\,p(\vec z)\big)\\
&\;=\E_{q(n,\vec z)}\Big[\log\frac{q(\vec z\mid n)}{q(\vec z)}\Big]\\
&\;\quad+\E_{q(n,\vec z)}\Big[\log\frac{q(\vec z)}{p(\vec z)}\Big].
\end{aligned}
$$

In the first term,
$q(\vec z\mid n)/q(\vec z)=q(n,\vec z)/\big(q(n)\,q(\vec z)\big)$, so it is
$\KL(q(n,\vec z)\,\|\,q(n)q(\vec z))=I_q(n;\vec z)$
(<ApparatusRef ref="0.IT.04" />). The second term depends on $\vec z$
alone, and the marginal of $q(n,\vec z)$ over $\vec z$ is $q(\vec z)$, so
it is $\KL(q(\vec z)\,\|\,p(\vec z))$. $\blacksquare$

**Zero rate means no information.** Both terms on the right are
non-negative. If the left side is zero, both are zero. Then
$I_q(n;\vec z)=0$: the code is independent of the example, so it tells the
decoder nothing about which input it came from. Equivalently, each
$\KL(q(\vec z\mid n)\,\|\,p)$ is non-negative and their average is zero, so
each is zero and every $q(\vec z\mid n)$ is the prior. That is posterior
collapse.

**The rate bounds the information.** Dropping the non-negative second term
gives $I_q(n;\vec z)\leq\frac1N\sum_n\KL(q(\vec z\mid n)\,\|\,p)$. The rate
is an upper bound on what the code says about the example. Since $n$ takes
$N$ values, the information is also at most $\log N$; any rate above that
is spent on the mismatch.

The identity holds for every encoder, not only at an optimum. The script
checks it for three encoders on the four points, including the collapsed
one, where both sides are zero.

</SolutionToggle>
