<Statement>
Count the copies of the parameter vector each optimiser holds, price them for
three model sizes, and identify the size at which the choice of optimiser stops
being a convergence question and becomes a hardware one.
</Statement>

<Given>
A model with $N$ parameters in fp32. Every method holds the weights and the
gradient; they differ in what else persists between steps. Sizes: 124M, 1.5B
and 7B.
</Given>

<Find>
State per parameter and total copies for SGD, SGD with momentum, RMSProp and
Adam; total memory at each size; the optimiser state as a share of the total;
and what swapping Adam for SGD with momentum saves at 1.5B.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Count in copies of $\vec{\theta}$ rather than in bytes, because the ratio is
what transfers between precisions and model sizes. Weights and gradients are
common to all four, so the whole difference is the third term.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**Step 1 — the state each method keeps.**

| method | state | copies | bytes/param |
|---|---|---|---|
| SGD | 0 | 2 | 8 |
| SGD + momentum | 1 | 3 | 12 |
| RMSProp | 1 | 3 | 12 |
| Adam / AdamW | 2 | 4 | 16 |
| Adam, fp32 master | 4 | 6 | 24 |

Adam keeps both moments of (I.7.6): $m$ for direction and $v$ for scale. That is
two extra copies, and with mixed precision an fp32 master copy of fp16 weights
pushes it to six.

**Step 2 — priced.**

| method | 124M | 1.5B | 7B |
|---|---|---|---|
| SGD | $0.99$ GB | $12.00$ GB | $56.00$ GB |
| SGD + momentum | $1.49$ GB | $18.00$ GB | $84.00$ GB |
| RMSProp | $1.49$ GB | $18.00$ GB | $84.00$ GB |
| Adam / AdamW | $1.98$ GB | $24.00$ GB | $112.00$ GB |

**Step 3 — where it stops being a preference.** At 7B, Adam's four copies come
to $112$ GB. The largest single accelerator commonly available holds $80$ GB, so
the model cannot be trained on one device with Adam *before a single activation
is stored* — and activations are the larger term at any useful batch, by I.6.B05.
SGD with momentum brings it to $84$ GB, which is still over. The decision has
moved from "which converges better" to "which fits", and no amount of gradient
checkpointing addresses it, because checkpointing trades activation memory and
this is not activation memory.

**Step 4 — the share.**

$$
\text{SGD } 0\%, \qquad \text{momentum } 33.3\%, \qquad \text{Adam } 50.0\%
$$

Half of Adam's four-way footprint is state that exists only to choose a step
size per coordinate.

**Step 5 — the swap.** At 1.5B:

$$
24.00\ \text{GB} \longrightarrow 18.00\ \text{GB}, \qquad 6.00\ \text{GB saved},\ 1.33\times
$$

A third of the optimiser footprint, for one moment estimate. Whether that is a
good trade depends on whether the per-coordinate scaling is buying convergence
on the model in question — which is an empirical matter, and is exactly the
question the framing of Adam as a default discourages anyone from asking.

</Solution>

<Answer>

Copies: SGD 2, momentum 3, RMSProp 3, Adam 4, Adam with an fp32 master 6.

At 1.5B: SGD $12.00$ GB, momentum $18.00$ GB, Adam $24.00$ GB. At 7B, Adam needs
$112.00$ GB — over a single 80 GB device before any activation.

Adam's state is $50\%$ of its own footprint. Swapping to SGD with momentum at
1.5B saves $6.00$ GB, a factor of $1.33$.

</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
**The ratios are precision-independent.** Counting in copies means fp16 halves
every number in the table and changes none of the ratios. That is why the copy
count is the quantity to remember and the GB figures are an illustration.

**Adam is exactly twice SGD.** Four copies against two, at every size. A useful
sanity check on any memory estimate: if switching from SGD to Adam does not
roughly double the parameter-side footprint, something else is being counted.

**The 124M row is unremarkable and that is the point.** $1.98$ GB against
$0.99$ GB — a gigabyte, on hardware with tens. The choice genuinely does not
matter at that scale, which is why the habit of reaching for Adam forms on small
models and then goes unexamined on large ones.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
This counts the optimiser and nothing else. Activations are usually the larger
term — I.6.B05 measured $3.22$ GB of them for a 48-layer model at batch
$32 \times 512$, against $0.20$ GB of parameters — and they scale with batch
while this table does not. A memory plan built from this table alone will be
wrong in the direction that matters.

The fp32-master row also understates modern practice. A production mixed-precision
setup may hold fp16 weights, fp16 gradients, an fp32 master copy, and fp32
moments, and sharded training distributes those copies across devices so that
the per-device figure is smaller than any row here while the total is larger.
ZeRO-style sharding exists precisely because this table is the binding
constraint at scale.

Finally, the memory saved by dropping to SGD is not free. Adam's per-coordinate
scaling is what makes one learning rate serviceable across parameters whose
gradients differ by orders of magnitude, and removing it usually means tuning a
schedule that Adam absorbed. The trade is real in both directions.
</Breaks>

<Variation>
Recompute the 7B row in bf16 for weights and gradients with fp32 moments — the
common mixed-precision arrangement. Does Adam fit on an 80 GB device then? State
what you had to assume about the master copy to get your answer.
</Variation>
