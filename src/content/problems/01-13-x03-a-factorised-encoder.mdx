A two-dimensional posterior is $\mathcal N(\vec 0,\mat\Sigma)$ with unit
variances and correlation $\rho=0.9$. The encoder's family is factorised:
$q=\mathcal N(\vec m,\operatorname{diag}(v_1,v_2))$. Find the member of the
family that minimises $\KL(q\,\|\,p)$, the variance it reports for each
coordinate, and the gap that remains.

<Hint>
For Gaussians in $d$ dimensions,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\KL(q\,\|\,p)=\tfrac12\big(\operatorname{tr}(\mat\Sigma^{-1}\mat\Sigma_q)\\
&\quad+\vec m^{\mathsf T}\mat\Sigma^{-1}\vec m-d\\
&\quad+\log\det\mat\Sigma-\log\det\mat\Sigma_q\big),
\end{aligned}
$$

and the diagonal of $\mat\Sigma^{-1}$ is $1/(1-\rho^2)$.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

The mean term is minimised at $\vec m=\vec 0$. With $\mat\Sigma_q$
diagonal, only the diagonal of $\mat\Sigma^{-1}$ enters the trace:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\KL=\tfrac12\Big(\frac{v_1+v_2}{1-\rho^2}-2\\
&\quad+\log(1-\rho^2)\\
&\quad-\log v_1-\log v_2\Big).
\end{aligned}
$$

Each $v_i$ appears in its own term. Setting
$1/(1-\rho^2)-1/v_i=0$ gives $v_i=1-\rho^2=0.19$. Substituting, the trace term becomes $2$ and cancels
the $-2$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\KL&=\tfrac12\big(\log0.19-2\log0.19\big)\\
 &=-\tfrac12\log0.19\\
 &=0.830366.
\end{aligned}
$$

So the best factorised encoder reports a variance of $0.19$ for each
coordinate, whose true posterior variance is $1$. It matches the
conditional variance of one coordinate given the other, not the marginal
one, and so understates the uncertainty more than fivefold.

The claim this breaks is that a trained encoder's variance measures how
uncertain the code is. The gap of $0.830366$ nats is the
approximation gap: it does not shrink with training, because no member of
the family is closer. Only a richer family, one with correlations, can
remove it.

</SolutionToggle>
