The second assumption of this chapter is that the activation is non-polynomial —
the exact hypothesis of I.3.T2. Show that a polynomial activation fails
universal approximation, by identifying precisely what such a network can
compute. Then state the smallest change that restores universality.

What is a polynomial of a polynomial?

<SolutionToggle>

**The claim.** Let $\varphi$ be a polynomial of degree $k$. Then a network of
depth $L$ with activation $\varphi$ computes a polynomial of degree at most
$k^{L}$ in its inputs — and *only* polynomials of that bounded degree.

**Proof.** Induct on depth. A linear layer applied to a polynomial of degree $m$
gives a polynomial of degree $m$, since it is a linear combination. Applying
$\varphi$, of degree $k$, to a polynomial of degree $m$ gives degree $km$.
Starting from degree $1$ at the input, after $L$ layers the degree is at most
$k^{L}$. $\blacksquare$

**Why that defeats universality.** The set of polynomials of degree $\le k^{L}$
is a **finite-dimensional vector space** — for $d$ inputs its dimension is
$\binom{k^L + d}{d}$, a finite number. But the continuous functions on a compact
set form an infinite-dimensional space. A finite-dimensional subspace is closed
and nowhere dense in it, so there are continuous functions at a *bounded positive
distance* from everything the network can compute, no matter how many units it
has.

**Concretely.** With $\varphi(z) = z^2$ and $L = 2$, the network computes only
polynomials of degree $\le 4$. Ask it to approximate $\sin(10x)$ on $[0, 2\pi]$
to within $0.1$: impossible, because the best degree-4 polynomial approximation
to $\sin(10x)$ on that interval has error close to $1$. Adding a million hidden
units does not help — width increases the *number* of degree-4 polynomials
available, not the degree.

**Contrast with a non-polynomial.** ReLU is piecewise linear, and by I.3.X03 a
ReLU network is piecewise linear with a number of pieces growing exponentially
in depth. Piecewise-linear functions are dense in the continuous functions on a
compact set, so the obstruction disappears. Sigmoid, tanh and GELU are all
non-polynomial too — indeed all three are transcendental.

**The smallest change that restores universality.** Add a single non-polynomial
element. Even one layer of ReLU somewhere in an otherwise-polynomial network
breaks the degree argument, because the induction requires *every* layer to be
polynomial. This is the same structural point as I.3.B05's *Where this breaks*:
these collapse theorems are chains, and one non-conforming link is enough.

**Why the hypothesis is stated the way it is.** Hornik's 1991 result is often
quoted as needing a "sigmoidal" activation, which is what Cybenko proved in
1989. The sharper statement — non-polynomial is necessary *and sufficient* — is
Leshno, Lin, Pinkus and Schocken (1993). It is worth knowing the sharp version,
because it explains why ReLU works despite being neither bounded nor sigmoidal.
