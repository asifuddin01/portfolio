Adding $\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\|\theta\|^2$ to the loss and subtracting
$\eta\lambda\theta$ from each update are the same operation under plain gradient
descent. Show they are the same, then show they are *not* the same under Adam,
and say which coordinates the difference falls on.

<Hint>
Under Adam the penalty's gradient does not go straight into the update — it
passes through the same division by $\sqrt{\hat{v}}$ as everything else.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**Under plain descent they coincide.** The penalty contributes
$\nabla(\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\|\theta\|^2) = \lambda\theta$, so

$$
\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta(g + \lambda\theta) = \theta - \eta g - \eta\lambda\theta
$$

which is the update with a separate $-\eta\lambda\theta$ term. The two
descriptions are one algorithm, which is why the names are used
interchangeably.

**Under Adam they diverge.** L2 regularisation puts $\lambda\theta$ into $g$
*before* the moments are formed, so it is divided by $\sqrt{\hat{v}}$ along with
everything else:

$$
\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta\,\frac{\widehat{m[g + \lambda\theta]}}{\sqrt{\widehat{v[g + \lambda\theta]}} + \varepsilon}
$$

Decoupled decay (AdamW) applies it outside:

$$
\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta\,\frac{\hat{m}[g]}{\sqrt{\hat{v}[g]} + \varepsilon} - \eta\lambda\theta
$$

**Which coordinates differ.** Consider a parameter whose gradient is
persistently large, so $\sqrt{\hat{v}}$ is large. Under L2 its decay term is
divided by that large number and is therefore *weak*. A parameter with a small
gradient has small $\sqrt{\hat{v}}$ and its decay is *strong*.

So L2-under-Adam regularises inversely to gradient magnitude: the parameters
doing the most work are penalised least, and the ones already doing little are
pushed towards zero hardest. That is the opposite of what a practitioner adding
weight decay intends, and it is not a small effect — $\sqrt{\hat{v}}$ can differ
by orders of magnitude across a network.

Decoupled decay applies $\eta\lambda\theta$ uniformly, which is what the name
describes.

**Why this counts as a counterexample.** The claim being refuted is the
reasonable one that weight decay and L2 are two names for one thing. They are,
under any optimiser whose update is proportional to the gradient — SGD,
momentum, Nesterov. They are not, under any optimiser that rescales the gradient
per coordinate, and Adam is the one everybody uses. The identity fails exactly
where it is most relied on.

**The practical residue.** Loshchilov and Hutter's AdamW is the decoupled form,
and it is now the default in most training code. A configuration ported from an
SGD recipe by copying $\lambda$ across will not reproduce its regularisation
under Adam, and the discrepancy is largest for the parameters that matter most.
