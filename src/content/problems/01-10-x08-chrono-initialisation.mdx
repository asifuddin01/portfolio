Show that the forget bias $b_f=\ln(m-1)$, with all forget weights zero, gives
$1/(1-f)=m$ exactly. Show that $b_i=-b_f$ then makes each cell coordinate an
exponential moving average of its candidates. Give $b_f$ for $m=2$, $10$ and
$100$, and say what goes wrong at $m=1$.

<Hint>
$\sigma(\ln a)=a/(1+a)$ and $\sigma(-a)=1-\sigma(a)$.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

With $b_f=\ln(m-1)$, $f=\sigma(\ln(m-1))=(m-1)/m$, so $1-f=1/m$ and
$1/(1-f)=m$. Equivalently, (I.10.5) gives $1+e^{\ln(m-1)}=m$.

With $b_i=-b_f$, $i=\sigma(-b_f)=1-f$, so

$$
c_t=f\,c_{t-1}+(1-f)\,\tilde c_t,
$$

a weighted average whose weights sum to one: an exponential moving average
of the candidates, with horizon set by $m$.

The biases are $\ln1=0$, $\ln9=2.197225$ and $\ln99=4.595120$. At $m=1$ the
formula needs $\ln0$: a horizon of one step would require $f=0$, which no
finite bias produces.

Tallec and Ollivier draw $b_f=\ln u$ with $u$ uniform between $1$ and
$T_{\max}-1$, so the initial horizons spread from two steps up to the longest
dependency the task is expected to need.

</SolutionToggle>
