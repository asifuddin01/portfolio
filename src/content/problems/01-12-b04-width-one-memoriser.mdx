<Statement>
Show that a narrow code does not, by itself, stop an autoencoder from
memorising its training set.
</Statement>

<Given>
The training set is the four points $(1,2)$, $(2.2,0.4)$, $(-1,-2)$ and
$(-2.2,-0.4)$, the corners of a rectangle. The encoder keeps the first
coordinate, $z=x_1$. The decoder returns $g(z)=(z,\,g_2(z))$. Its second
output is piecewise linear, with $\operatorname{relu}(a)=\max(a,0)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_2(z)=&-\tfrac43z-\tfrac{10}3\\
 &+\tfrac{10}3\operatorname{relu}(z+1)\\
 &-\tfrac{10}3\operatorname{relu}(z-1).
\end{aligned}
$$

For comparison, the principal-axis autoencoder of
[I.12.B02](/elementa/neural-networks/autoencoders/problems/b02) projects
onto $\vec u_1=(0.8,0.6)$. The held-out set is the midpoints of the
rectangle's four sides: $(-0.6,0.8)$, $(0.6,-0.8)$, $(1.6,1.2)$ and
$(-1.6,-1.2)$.
</Given>

<Find>
The training loss of both autoencoders, their mean error on the held-out
points, the decoding of the code $z=5$, and what went wrong.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Write the decoder's second coordinate on each of its three linear pieces.
Then evaluate both models point by point.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**The decoder, piece by piece.** The two ReLU terms switch on at $z=-1$
and $z=1$, so the second output is

$$
\begin{aligned}
z\leq-1&:\quad g_2=-\tfrac43z-\tfrac{10}3,\\[2pt]
|z|\leq1&:\quad g_2=2z,\\[2pt]
z\geq1&:\quad g_2=-\tfrac43z+\tfrac{10}3.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Training points.** The codes are $1$, $2.2$, $-1$ and $-2.2$, and
$g_2$ returns $2$, $0.4$, $-2$ and $-0.4$. Every training point is
reconstructed exactly, so the training loss is $0$. The principal axis
gives each corner an error of $1$, so its training loss is $1$.

**Held-out points.** At $(-0.6,0.8)$ the code is $-0.6$ and the decoder
returns $(-0.6,-1.2)$: squared error $(0.8+1.2)^2=4$. At $(0.6,-0.8)$ it
returns $(0.6,1.2)$: error $4$ again. At $(1.6,1.2)$ and $(-1.6,-1.2)$ it
is exact. The mean is $2$.

The principal axis gives the first two points the code $0$ and the
reconstruction $(0,0)$, with error $1$ each. The other two lie on the axis,
with error $0$. The mean is $0.5$.

| | training loss | held-out mean |
|---|---|---|
| width-one code, two ReLU kinks | $0$ | $2$ |
| principal axis | $1$ | $0.5$ |

**Away from the data.** The code $5$ decodes to
$(5,\,-\tfrac{20}3+\tfrac{10}3)=(5,\,-3.333333)$. The training codes lie
between $-2.2$ and $2.2$; nothing constrained the decoder beyond them.

**What went wrong.** The reconstruction surface is a zigzag through the
corners in order of their first coordinate:
$(-2.2,-0.4)\to(-1,-2)\to(1,2)\to(2.2,0.4)$. Its middle piece crosses the
rectangle from one corner to the opposite one. The code is one number, as
narrow as a code can be, and the surface is one-dimensional as it must be.
But a curve with two kinks has three straight pieces, enough to meet four
points. The bottleneck bounded the dimension of the surface and left its
shape free.

</Solution>

<Answer>
Training loss $0$ against $1$. Held-out mean error $2$ against $0.5$, with
$4$ against $1$ at $(-0.6,0.8)$. The code $5$ decodes to $(5,-3.333333)$.
The decoder had enough freedom to pass through every training point.
</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
The decoder is continuous: at $z=-1$ both neighbouring pieces give $-2$,
and at $z=1$ both give $2$. Its first output is the code itself, so only
the second coordinate can be wrong.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
The construction needs the training points to have distinct codes. Two
inputs that share a code share a reconstruction, whatever the decoder, so
they cannot both be memorised. A curve with $n-2$ kinks passes through $n$
points with distinct codes; with more training points than that, the same
decoder can no longer fit them all.
</Breaks>

<Variation>
Add the four midpoints to the training set. The zigzag now has a loss of
$1$ on the eight points, since it errs by $4$ on two of them, while the
principal axis has $0.75$. More data repaired what a narrower code could
not.
</Variation>
