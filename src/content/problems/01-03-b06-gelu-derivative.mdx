<Statement>
Derive $\mathrm{GELU}'$, evaluate it at seven points, and compare its behaviour
at the origin with ReLU's. Then state the optimisation consequence of the
difference and find where the derivative is most negative.
</Statement>

<Given>
$\mathrm{GELU}(z) = z\,\Phi(z)$ with $\Phi$ the standard normal CDF and
$\Phi' = \phi$, the standard normal density
$\phi(z) = e^{-z^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}$.
</Given>

<Find>
A closed form for $\mathrm{GELU}'$; its values at
$z \in \{-3,-1,-0.5,0,0.5,1,3\}$; the value at $z=0$ against ReLU's; and
$\min_z \mathrm{GELU}'(z)$.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Product rule, then evaluate. The interesting content is entirely in what the
second term does for negative $z$.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**Step 1 — differentiate.** By the product rule on $z \cdot \Phi(z)$:

$$\mathrm{GELU}'(z) = \Phi(z) + z\,\phi(z) \tag{I.3.4}$$

Two terms with different characters. $\Phi(z) \in (0,1)$ is a soft gate rising
from $0$ to $1$. The correction $z\phi(z)$ is positive for $z>0$, negative for
$z<0$, and decays to zero in both tails because the Gaussian density does.

**Step 2 — evaluate.**

| $z$ | GELU | $\mathrm{GELU}'$ | $\mathrm{ReLU}'$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $-3.0$ | $-0.0040$ | $-0.0119$ | $0$ |
| $-1.0$ | $-0.1587$ | $-0.0833$ | $0$ |
| $-0.5$ | $-0.1543$ | $+0.1325$ | $0$ |
| $0.0$ | $0.0000$ | $+0.5000$ | undefined |
| $0.5$ | $0.3457$ | $+0.8675$ | $1$ |
| $1.0$ | $0.8413$ | $+1.0833$ | $1$ |
| $3.0$ | $2.9960$ | $+1.0119$ | $1$ |

**Step 3 — the origin.** $\mathrm{GELU}'(0) = \Phi(0) + 0 = 0.5$, and it is a
genuine two-sided derivative: GELU is $C^\infty$ everywhere, being a product of
smooth functions.

ReLU has no derivative at $0$ at all. Its left slope is $0$, its right slope is
$1$, and every framework silently picks a subgradient from $[0,1]$ — usually
$0$, sometimes $0.5$, sometimes $1$. GELU's $0.5$ is not a convention: it is the
value.

**Step 4 — the minimum.** Setting $\mathrm{GELU}'' = 0$ gives
$2\phi(z) + z\phi'(z) = \phi(z)(2 - z^2) = 0$, so $z = -\sqrt2 = -1.4142$
(taking the negative root). There

$$\mathrm{GELU}'(-\sqrt2) = \Phi(-1.4142) + (-1.4142)\phi(-1.4142) = 0.0786 - 0.2075 = -0.1289$$

**The derivative goes negative.** No activation studied so far does this.
Between roughly $z = -\infty$ and $z \approx -0.75$, increasing the
pre-activation *decreases* the output.

**Step 5 — the optimisation consequences.** Three, in order of how often they
are stated correctly.

*No dead region.* $\mathrm{GELU}'(-10) = \Phi(-10) + (-10)\phi(-10)$, which is
about $-7.6\times10^{-23}$ — vanishingly small but nonzero. So the dead-unit
failure of I.3.B04 cannot occur exactly, though at $10^{-23}$ the distinction is
academic and fp16 will round it to zero regardless.

*No kink, so second-order methods are well defined.* Anything relying on
curvature — natural gradient, K-FAC, or simply a smooth loss landscape — is
better behaved when the activation is twice differentiable. ReLU's Hessian is a
sum of Dirac deltas at the kinks.

*The negative lobe is a real functional difference.* It lets a unit express "this
input is moderately against my feature" with a small negative output rather than
with silence. Whether that helps is empirical, and the honest answer is that
GELU's advantage over ReLU in transformers is consistent but small, and no
convincing theoretical account of it exists.

</Solution>

<Answer>
$$\mathrm{GELU}'(z) = \Phi(z) + z\,\phi(z)$$

$\mathrm{GELU}'(0) = 0.5$ exactly, where $\mathrm{ReLU}'(0)$ does not exist.
The minimum is $\mathrm{GELU}'(-\sqrt2) = -0.1289$, so the derivative is
negative on roughly $(-\infty, -0.75)$.
</Answer>

<CheckNumeric snippet="i-3-b06-gelu-derivative.py" verified>
```python
def dgelu(z): return Phi(z) + z * phi(z)
lo = min(dgelu(z / 1000.0) for z in range(-3000, 1))
```
Prints the table, `most negative dGELU -0.1289`, and `dGELU(0) 0.5000`.
</CheckNumeric>

<CheckSanity>
**The tails match ReLU.** At $z = 3$, $\mathrm{GELU}' = 1.0119 \approx 1$; at
$z=-3$, $-0.0119 \approx 0$. GELU is asymptotically ReLU in both directions, as
its construction intends.

**The derivative integrates back to the function.** Between $z=0$ and $z=0.5$
the mean of $\mathrm{GELU}'$ is roughly $(0.5+0.8675)/2 = 0.684$, so the
predicted rise is $0.684 \times 0.5 = 0.342$ against the actual
$0.3457 - 0 = 0.3457$. Agreement to two digits from a two-point trapezoid, which
is as much as that method deserves.

**The minimum is where the second derivative says.** $2 - z^2 = 0$ at
$z = \pm\sqrt2$, and the numerical scan over $3{,}001$ points found $-0.1289$,
consistent with the closed-form evaluation at $-\sqrt2$.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
Everything above is for the exact GELU. The tanh approximation
$0.5z(1 + \tanh(\sqrt{2/\pi}(z + 0.044715z^3)))$, which most implementations
actually use, has a slightly different derivative — the minimum is near
$-0.1288$ rather than $-0.1289$, and the two functions differ by up to about
$10^{-3}$ around $|z| \approx 2$. Small, but the two are not the same function,
and a paper reporting "GELU" may mean either.
</Breaks>
