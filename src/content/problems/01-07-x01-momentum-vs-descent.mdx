On the quadratic of I.7.B01 — $\mat{H} = \mathrm{diag}(20, 0.2)$, $\kappa = 100$
— compare plain descent with momentum at $\beta \in \{0.6694, 0.9, 0.99\}$.
Predict the ordering before looking, then explain the one that surprises you.

<Hint>
By I.7.X03 the buffer settles at $g/(1-\beta)$ for a steady gradient. What does
that do to the effective step size, and what does an effective step size that is
too large do?
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**The measurements.** Steps to reach an error norm of $10^{-3}$:

| method | $\eta$ | $\beta$ | steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| plain descent | $0.099010$ | $0$ | $363$ |
| momentum, tuned | $0.165289$ | $0.6694$ | $\mathbf{58}$ |
| momentum, default | $0.165289$ | $0.9000$ | $132$ |
| momentum, default $\eta$ | $0.099010$ | $0.9000$ | $127$ |
| momentum, $\beta = 0.99$ | $0.165289$ | $0.9900$ | $\mathbf{1\,214}$ |

**The tuned case behaves as advertised.** $58$ steps against $363$, a factor of
$6.3$, against the asymptotic prediction $\sqrt{\kappa} = 10$. The shortfall is
the transient: momentum needs several steps before its average carries
information, and 58 steps is not many multiples of that.

**The surprise is the last row.** At $\beta = 0.99$ momentum takes $1\,214$
steps — **3.3 times worse than using no momentum at all.** More memory made it
worse, decisively.

**Why.** By I.7.X03 a steady gradient drives the buffer to $g/(1-\beta)$, so the
effective step is $\eta/(1-\beta)$. At $\beta = 0.99$ that is a hundredfold
amplification: an $\eta$ of $0.165$ behaves like $16.5$, far past the stability
threshold of $0.1$. The iteration does not diverge only because the oscillation
it induces is itself damped by the averaging — so instead of blowing up, it
lurches around the valley, and the lurching is slower than walking.

**The optimum is not near the default.** For $\kappa = 100$ the tuned $\beta$ is
$0.6694$, well below the $0.9$ everyone writes. The default is optimal for
$\kappa \approx 361$, and the further a problem is from that, the worse it
serves — in both directions.

**What to take from this.** $\beta$ and $\eta$ are not independent knobs, and
raising $\beta$ is a learning-rate increase wearing a different name. A
configuration search that varies them separately is searching a space where the
interesting direction is diagonal. It also explains a common experience: adding
momentum to a run tuned without it makes things worse, and the fix is to lower
$\eta$ at the same time rather than to conclude that momentum does not help.
