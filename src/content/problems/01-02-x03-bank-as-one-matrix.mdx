Write a one-vs-rest bank of $K$ perceptrons over $d$ inputs as a single matrix
operation on a batch of $B$ examples. Give every shape, and state what the bank
can and cannot express that $K$ separately-stored perceptrons can.

<Hint>
Stack the $K$ weight vectors as columns, not rows. Then check the shapes meet
under the row-major convention.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**The stacking.** Place the $k$-th perceptron's weight vector in the $k$-th
*column*:

$$\mat{W} = \begin{bmatrix} \vec{w}_1 & \vec{w}_2 & \cdots & \vec{w}_K \end{bmatrix} \in \R^{d\times K}, \qquad \vec{b} \in \R^{1\times K}$$

**The forward pass.** With a batch $\mat{X} \in \R^{B\times d}$, one row per
example:

$$\mat{S} = \mat{X}\mat{W} + \vec{b} \in \R^{B\times K}$$

Shapes: $(B\times d)(d\times K) \to (B\times K)$, then the bias broadcasts along
the batch axis. Entry $S_{ik}$ is the score of example $i$ under perceptron $k$,
so one product computes $BK$ scores.

**Every shape.**

| Object | Shape |
|---|---|
| $\mat{X}$ | $B \times d$ |
| $\mat{W}$ | $d \times K$ |
| $\vec{b}$ | $1 \times K$ (broadcast to $B \times K$) |
| $\mat{S}$ | $B \times K$ |
| prediction | $B \times 1$, by $\arg\max$ along the $K$ axis |

**What is identical.** The arithmetic. $K$ separate perceptrons compute exactly
these numbers; the matrix form only arranges them so one call does the work of
$K$. The parameter count $K(d+1)$ from I.2.B06 is unchanged.

**What the matrix form adds.** Nothing expressive — and that is the point worth
taking. It is the same hypothesis class. What it adds is:

*A single decision rule.* Separate perceptrons each answer yes or no, and can
answer yes twice or never. Taking $\arg\max$ over the score row forces exactly
one answer, which the $K$ independent models do not.

*The object of Chapter I.5.* $\mat{X}\mat{W} + \vec{b}$ is precisely a linear
layer. A bank of perceptrons and the first layer of an MLP are the same
computation; only the training rule and what sits after it differ. Arriving at
the linear layer from the perceptron rather than from the definition is worth
doing once, because it makes clear that depth — not the layer — is the new idea.

**What neither can express.** Any function requiring a non-linear boundary,
XOR included (I.2.B04). Stacking $K$ hyperplanes side by side gives $K$
hyperplanes, not a curve.
