This chapter assumes a scalar loss. Price what happens when that assumption is
dropped: give the cost of a backward pass for a vector output of width $m$, for
per-example gradients across a batch, and for a full Jacobian — then say why the
assumption is not the restriction it appears to be.

<Hint>
Reverse mode fills one row of the Jacobian per traversal. How many rows does
each of these three objects have?
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**The model.** $L = 48$ layers of width $1024$ at batch $32$, as in I.6.B05.
One forward pass is $3.22$ GFLOP and one backward traversal is twice that.

| sink | traversals | GFLOP | vs scalar |
|---|---|---|---|
| a scalar loss | 1 | $9.66$ | $1.0\times$ |
| a 10-vector output | 10 | $67.65$ | $7.0\times$ |
| per-example gradients, batch 32 | 32 | $209.38$ | $21.7\times$ |
| full Jacobian of 1024 outputs | 1024 | $6600.29$ | $683.0\times$ |

**The pattern.** Cost is $F + 2tF$ for $t$ traversals, so it is linear in $t$
and the ratios approach $t$ from below — the single shared forward pass is
amortised across them. At $t = 1024$ the ratio is $683$ rather than $1024$ for
exactly that reason.

**Per-example gradients are the same problem.** A batch of 32 losses is a
32-vector sink, so 32 traversals. This is why the memory answer in I.6.X05 has a
matching arithmetic answer: per-sample methods cost $B\times$ in both resources,
and neither cost is avoidable by clever bookkeeping — the object genuinely has
$B$ times as many numbers in it.

**Why the assumption is not a restriction.** A loss is a *reduction*. Any
vector-valued objective is summed or averaged before differentiation, and that
reduction is one more node in the graph — the sink where $\bar{L} = 1$ starts.
Summing 32 per-example losses costs 31 additions, which against a $3.22$ GFLOP
forward pass is a share of $9.6 \times 10^{-9}$: free, to nine decimal places.

So the scalar assumption costs nothing whenever a single number is what is
wanted, and training is that case by construction. It bites only when the
individual outputs are wanted *separately* — and then what is being asked for is
a Jacobian, and the price above is the honest price of one.

**The one to remember.** Multi-task learning with $k$ losses does *not* cost $k$
traversals, as long as the tasks are combined into one weighted sum before the
backward pass. Wanting the $k$ gradients separately — to inspect their conflict,
to normalise their scales — is what costs $k$ traversals, and the difference
between those two situations is a modelling decision with a large and easily
overlooked price attached.
