Add the penalty $\gamma(\|\mat E\|_F^2+\|\mat D\|_F^2)$, with $\gamma>0$, to
the loss of (I.12.2).

(a) Write the two stationarity conditions and prove that
$\mat D^{\mathsf T}\mat D=\mat E\mat E^{\mathsf T}$ at every stationary
point.

(b) Let $\mat D=\mat U_k\mat C$ and $\mat E=\mat D^{\mathsf T}$ with
$\mat C=\operatorname{diag}(c_1,\ldots,c_k)$. Show that this pair is
stationary when $c_i^2=1-\gamma/\lambda_i$, and find the end-to-end map.

(c) For the four points, with $k=2$ and $\gamma=0.5$, find the gains, the
decoder and the value of the objective. Compare it with the identity.

(d) Explain why the principal axes can be read from this decoder and not
from an unpenalised one.

(e) What happens to a direction with $\lambda_i\leq\gamma$?

<Hint>
For (b), use $\mat S\mat U_k=\mat U_k\mat\Lambda_k$ and
$\mat U_k^{\mathsf T}\mat U_k=\mat I$, where
$\mat\Lambda_k=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k)$.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**(a)** The penalty adds $2\gamma\mat D$ and $2\gamma\mat E$ to the
gradients of (I.12.2). Setting both to zero:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\mat I-\mat D\mat E)\,\mat S\,\mat E^{\mathsf T}&=\gamma\mat D,\\
\mat D^{\mathsf T}(\mat I-\mat D\mat E)\,\mat S&=\gamma\mat E.
\end{aligned}
$$

Multiply the first on the left by $\mat D^{\mathsf T}$ and the second on
the right by $\mat E^{\mathsf T}$. Both left-hand sides become
$\mat D^{\mathsf T}(\mat I-\mat D\mat E)\mat S\mat E^{\mathsf T}$, so
$\gamma\mat D^{\mathsf T}\mat D=\gamma\mat E\mat E^{\mathsf T}$. Divide by
$\gamma$.

**(b)** Here $\mat D\mat E=\mat U_k\mat C^2\mat U_k^{\mathsf T}$. Using the
hint,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&(\mat I-\mat D\mat E)\,\mat S\,\mat E^{\mathsf T}\\
&\;\;=(\mat I-\mat U_k\mat C^2\mat U_k^{\mathsf T})\,\mat U_k\mat\Lambda_k\mat C\\
&\;\;=\mat U_k(\mat I-\mat C^2)\mat\Lambda_k\mat C.
\end{aligned}
$$

This equals $\gamma\mat D=\gamma\mat U_k\mat C$ when
$(1-c_i^2)\lambda_i=\gamma$ for every $i$, that is,
$c_i^2=1-\gamma/\lambda_i$. Since $\mat D\mat E$ and $\mat S$ are both
symmetric and share eigenvectors, they commute, and the second condition
is the transpose of the first. The end-to-end map is

$$
\mat D\mat E=\mat U_k\operatorname{diag}(1-\gamma/\lambda_i)\,
 \mat U_k^{\mathsf T}.
$$

Replacing $\mat D$ by $\mat D\mat O^{\mathsf T}$ and $\mat E$ by
$\mat O\mat E$ for an orthogonal $\mat O$ changes neither condition nor
either norm. That gives the family (I.12.6).

**(c)** The gains are $1-0.5/4=0.875$ and $1-0.5/1=0.5$, so
$c=(0.935414,\;0.707107)$. The decoder's columns are

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.935414\begin{bmatrix}0.8\\0.6\end{bmatrix}
 &=\begin{bmatrix}0.748331\\0.561249\end{bmatrix},\\[2pt]
0.707107\begin{bmatrix}-0.6\\0.8\end{bmatrix}
 &=\begin{bmatrix}-0.424264\\0.565685\end{bmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The reconstruction term is
$\sum_i(1-g_i)^2\lambda_i=0.0625+0.25=0.3125$. The penalty is
$\gamma\cdot2\sum_ic_i^2=0.5\cdot2\cdot1.375=1.375$. The objective is
$1.6875$. The identity, $\mat E=\mat D=\mat I$, reconstructs exactly and
pays $0.5\cdot4=2$. With weight decay, copying is no longer the best
answer even at full width.

**(d)** The expression $\mat D=\mat U_k\mat C\mat O^{\mathsf T}$ is a
singular value decomposition of the decoder: left singular vectors
$\vec u_i$, singular values $c_i$. When the $\lambda_i$ are distinct, so
are the $c_i$, and the left singular vectors are determined up to sign and
come out in order of $\lambda_i$. The unknown rotation $\mat O$ sits on the
right and does not disturb them.

Without the penalty the decoder is $\mat U_k\mat G$ for an arbitrary
invertible $\mat G$. Its left singular vectors are $\mat U_k$ times those
of $\mat G$, which can be any orthonormal basis of the subspace.

**(e)** In this family the contribution of one direction to the objective
is $(1-c^2)^2\lambda+2\gamma c^2$. Its derivative with respect to $c^2$ is
$-2(1-c^2)\lambda+2\gamma$, which is non-negative for all $c^2\geq0$ when
$\lambda\leq\gamma$. The best choice is then $c=0$: the direction is not
learned at all. Weight decay sets a floor on the variance a direction
needs before it is worth representing.

For the four points with $\gamma=1$, the second direction has
$\lambda_2=\gamma$ and is dropped. The first has gain $0.75$, the decoder
is $\sqrt{0.75}\,\vec u_1=(0.692820,\;0.519615)$, and the objective is
$0.25+1+1.5=2.75$.

The script checks the stationarity of (c) to machine precision, confirms
that sixteen small perturbations all raise the objective, and confirms
that rotating the code leaves it unchanged.
