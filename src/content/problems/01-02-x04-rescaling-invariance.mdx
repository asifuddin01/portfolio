Let $c > 0$ and replace $(\vec{w}, b)$ by $(c\vec{w}, cb)$. Determine what
happens to the decision boundary, the predicted labels, the functional margin,
and the geometric margin. Then say which of the two margins can appear in a
theorem and why.

<Hint>
Write each quantity out with the $c$ in place and see whether it cancels.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**The boundary.** The set $\{\vec{x} : c\langle\vec{w},\vec{x}\rangle + cb = 0\}$
equals $\{\vec{x} : c(\langle\vec{w},\vec{x}\rangle + b) = 0\}$, and since
$c \neq 0$ this is the same set as before. **Unchanged.**

**The labels.** $\mathrm{sign}(c\,s) = \mathrm{sign}(s)$ for $c > 0$.
**Unchanged.**

**The functional margin.** $m = y(\langle\vec{w},\vec{x}\rangle + b)$ becomes

$$y\big(c\langle\vec{w},\vec{x}\rangle + cb\big) = c\,m$$

**Scaled by $c$.** It can be made as large as one likes by taking $c$ large,
without moving the boundary an inch.

**The geometric margin.** Both numerator and denominator scale:

$$\frac{y(c\langle\vec{w},\vec{x}\rangle + cb)}{\lVert c\vec{w}\rVert} = \frac{c\,m}{c\lVert\vec{w}\rVert} = \frac{m}{\lVert\vec{w}\rVert}$$

**Unchanged.** The $c$ cancels exactly.

**Which one can appear in a theorem.** Only the geometric margin. A theorem
whose hypothesis was "the functional margin is at least $\gamma$" would be
vacuous: given *any* separating hyperplane, multiply its parameters by
$c = \gamma / m_{\min}$ and the hypothesis is satisfied, with no change to the
classifier at all. The statement would constrain nothing.

This is why I.2.T1 is stated with a **unit** vector $\vec{u}$, and why the proof
in I.2.B03 uses $\lVert\vec{u}\rVert = 1$ in its Cauchy–Schwarz step. Normalising
is not tidiness; it is what makes the quantity a property of the data rather
than of an arbitrary scaling.

**A consequence worth carrying forward.** Any quantity you plan to threshold,
compare across models, or put in a bound must be checked for this kind of
spurious freedom first. The same question recurs for attention scores in II.3
— where the answer is that $\sqrt{d_k}$ removes a scale that would otherwise
grow with width — and for logits in IV.8, where a temperature does exactly what
$c$ does here.

**One asymmetry.** For $c < 0$ the boundary is still unchanged but every label
flips, since $\mathrm{sign}(cs) = -\mathrm{sign}(s)$. So the invariance is to
*positive* rescaling only, and the sign of $\vec{w}$ carries the orientation —
which side is which.
</SolutionToggle>
