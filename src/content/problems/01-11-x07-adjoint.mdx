A one-dimensional cross-correlation uses kernel $(1,2,3)$, stride two,
padding one and input length four. Write its matrix $A$. For
$x=(1,2,3,4)^{\mathsf T}$ and $g=(1,-1)^{\mathsf T}$,
verify $\langle Ax,g\rangle=\langle x,A^{\mathsf T}g\rangle$.
Choose output padding to return length four with (I.11.5).
Is $A^{\mathsf T}$ an inverse?

<Hint>
The two padded windows are $(0,x_0,x_1)$ and $(x_1,x_2,x_3)$.
</Hint>
<SolutionToggle>

The matrix and products are

$$
A=\begin{bmatrix}2&3&0&0\\0&1&2&3\end{bmatrix},
\qquad Ax=\begin{bmatrix}8\\20\end{bmatrix},
$$

$$
A^{\mathsf T}g=\begin{bmatrix}2\\2\\-2\\-3\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Both dot products equal $-12$:
$8-20=-12$ and $2+4-6-12=-12$.
That is the adjoint identity behind the backward scatter-add.

The transposed output length is
$(2-1)2-2+2+o+1=3+o$.
Choose $o=1$ to return length four.
The original matrix has rank at most two, so it cannot distinguish all
four-dimensional inputs. Its transpose cannot be a two-sided inverse.
Matching the original length is not evidence of inversion.

</SolutionToggle>
