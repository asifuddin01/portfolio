Unroll the momentum recurrence $v_t = \beta v_{t-1} + g_t$ into a sum over past
gradients, identify the weights, and derive the effective window length. Then
say what $\beta = 0.9$ and $\beta = 0.99$ each mean in steps, and what the
buffer's magnitude is for a constant gradient.

<Hint>
Substitute the recurrence into itself a few times. The coefficients form a
sequence you can recognise and sum.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**Unrolling.** The recurrence is the first half of (I.7.5). With $v_0 = 0$:

$$
v_t = g_t + \beta g_{t-1} + \beta^2 g_{t-2} + \dots = \sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\beta^k g_{t-k}
$$

The buffer is a weighted sum of every gradient so far, with weights decaying
geometrically. Nothing is forgotten exactly; contributions become negligible.

**The window.** The natural measure is the mean age of a contribution, weighted
by its coefficient:

$$
\bar{k} = \frac{\sum_k k\beta^k}{\sum_k \beta^k}
= \frac{\beta/(1-\beta)^2}{1/(1-\beta)} = \frac{\beta}{1-\beta}
$$

so the buffer averages over roughly $1/(1-\beta)$ recent steps — the usual
statement, differing from $\bar{k}$ by one.

| $\beta$ | $1/(1-\beta)$ | $\bar{k}$ |
|---|---|---|
| $0.9$ | $10$ | $9$ |
| $0.99$ | $100$ | $99$ |
| $0.999$ | $1000$ | $999$ |

Moving from $0.9$ to $0.99$ is not a small adjustment: it multiplies the memory
by ten.

**Magnitude for a constant gradient.** If $g_t = g$ for every step, the sum is
geometric:

$$
v_t = g\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\beta^k = g\,\frac{1 - \beta^t}{1 - \beta} \xrightarrow{t \to \infty} \frac{g}{1-\beta}
$$

The buffer settles at $g/(1-\beta)$, which is $10g$ at $\beta = 0.9$. The step is
$\eta v$, so **momentum multiplies the effective step size by $1/(1-\beta)$ in
any direction where the gradient is consistent.** Raising $\beta$ without
lowering $\eta$ is a learning-rate increase in disguise, and it is a common way
to make a stable configuration diverge while believing only the smoothing
changed.

**Why the asymmetry helps.** In a direction where the gradient oscillates in
sign, consecutive terms cancel and $v$ stays small. In a direction where it is
consistent, they accumulate to $g/(1-\beta)$. Momentum damps the first and
amplifies the second, which is exactly the discrimination the ill-conditioned
problem of I.7.B03 requires — and it is why the improvement there was a factor
of fifty rather than a modest smoothing.

**The variant worth knowing.** Some formulations use
$v_t = \beta v_{t-1} + (1-\beta)g_t$, which normalises the weights to sum to one
and removes the $1/(1-\beta)$ gain. PyTorch's SGD uses the first form and its
Adam uses the second, so the same $\beta$ means a different effective step in
the two — a fact worth checking before porting a configuration between them.
