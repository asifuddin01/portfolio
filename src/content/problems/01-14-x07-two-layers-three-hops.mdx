Give every node the feature $1$ on a path of four nodes and on a path of
five. Apply the graph convolution's $\hat{\mat A}$ one, two and three times,
and compare the value at node $1$, an end of each path. When do the two
first differ, and why then?

<Hint>
After $k$ applications node $1$ depends on the degrees and features within
$k$ hops. In both paths nodes $1$, $2$ and $3$ have the same degrees; node
$4$ does not.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

| layers | path of four | path of five |
|---|---|---|
| $1$ | $0.908248$ | $0.908248$ |
| $2$ | $0.892956$ | $0.892956$ |
| $3$ | $0.890408$ | $0.880213$ |

The values agree for two layers and differ at the third. Node $1$'s value
after $k$ layers uses the entries of $\hat{\mat A}$ along walks of length at
most $k$ from it. Those entries depend on the degrees at both ends of each
edge walked. Within two hops of node $1$ the walks touch only nodes $1$, $2$
and $3$, whose degrees are $1$, $2$ and $2$ in both paths. The third layer
walks the edge from node $3$ to node $4$, and node $4$ has degree $1$ in the
shorter path and $2$ in the longer.

So a two-layer network cannot tell whether node $1$ sits on a path of four
or five, whatever its width or weights. That is I.14.T2 at work: a task
that depends on something three hops away needs three layers, and the
averaging of proposition 3 makes each extra layer cost something.

</SolutionToggle>
