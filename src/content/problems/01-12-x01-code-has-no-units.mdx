A width-one linear autoencoder has $\mat E=(0.8,\,0.6)$ and
$\mat D=(0.8,\,0.6)^{\mathsf T}$. Encode and reconstruct the new input
$(2,-1)$, and find its squared error. Repeat with $\mat E'=(4,\,3)$ and
$\mat D'=(0.16,\,0.12)^{\mathsf T}$. What do the two codes have in common,
and what do they not?

<Hint>
Compute $\mat D\mat E$ and $\mat D'\mat E'$ before computing anything else.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

The code is $0.8\cdot2+0.6\cdot(-1)=1$. The reconstruction is
$(0.8,\,0.6)$ and the residual is $(1.2,\,-1.6)$, with squared length
$1.44+2.56=4$.

With the second pair the code is $4\cdot2+3\cdot(-1)=5$, and the
reconstruction is $(0.16\cdot5,\;0.12\cdot5)=(0.8,\,0.6)$ again. Both
products equal

$$
\begin{bmatrix}0.64&0.48\\0.48&0.36\end{bmatrix},
$$

so the two autoencoders are the same function of the input and have the
same loss on every dataset. They are the cases $\mat G=1$ and
$\mat G=1/5$ of (I.12.5).

The codes share their ordering and their ratios: one is five times the
other for every input. They do not share a scale. A code's magnitude, and
with more than one code unit its axes and the angles between them, are
set by the factorisation that training happened to reach and not by the
data.

The input $(2,-1)$ is $\vec u_1-2\vec u_2$ in the eigenvectors
$\vec u_1=(0.8,0.6)$ and $\vec u_2=(-0.6,0.8)$. The error $4$ is the square
of its discarded component.
