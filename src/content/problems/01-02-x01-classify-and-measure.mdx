For $\vec{w} = (1, 3)$ and $b = -6$, write the boundary equation and give the
class and signed distance of $P=(3,2)$, $Q=(0,0)$, $R=(6,1)$, $S=(1,0)$. Then
say which point the boundary would reach first if $b$ were increased.

<Hint>
Increasing $b$ raises every score by the same amount, so it slides the boundary
without rotating it. The first point to flip is the one with the smallest
positive score.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**Boundary.** $x_1 + 3x_2 - 6 = 0$, i.e. $x_2 = 2 - x_1/3$.

**Norm.** $\lVert\vec{w}\rVert = \sqrt{1 + 9} = \sqrt{10} = 3.1623$.

| Point | Score | Distance | Class |
|---|---|---|---|
| $P=(3,2)$ | $3 + 6 - 6 = +3$ | $+0.9487$ | $+1$ |
| $Q=(0,0)$ | $0 + 0 - 6 = -6$ | $-1.8974$ | $-1$ |
| $R=(6,1)$ | $6 + 3 - 6 = +3$ | $+0.9487$ | $+1$ |
| $S=(1,0)$ | $1 + 0 - 6 = -5$ | $-1.5811$ | $-1$ |

**Which flips first.** Decreasing $b$ lowers every score equally. $P$ and $R$
tie at $+3$, so both flip together at $b = -9$. Going the other way, increasing
$b$ raises scores: $S$ at $-5$ flips before $Q$ at $-6$, at $b = -1$.

**What that shows.** The bias is a translation, not a rotation. It moves the
boundary along its own normal $\vec{w}$ and cannot change which side of the
*plane through the origin* a point sits on. Any question that requires
re-orienting the boundary needs $\vec{w}$ to change, and the bias cannot help.

The tie between $P$ and $R$ is worth noting: two points at different locations
can be equidistant from a boundary, because distance to a hyperplane collapses
$d$ coordinates into one number.
</SolutionToggle>
