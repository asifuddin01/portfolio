<Statement>
Derive the backward pass of an LSTM step, identify the carry line in it, and
measure how much of a real derivative the carry line accounts for.
</Statement>

<Given>
The cell of (I.10.1) and (I.10.2), with a loss that may read any $\vec h_t$.
For the numerical part, use the scalar cell of
[I.10.B01](/elementa/neural-networks/gating/problems/b01) with one change:
the candidate also reads the hidden state, $\tilde c_t=\tanh(x_t\ln3+2h_{t-1})$.
Inputs are $(1,0,0)$ from zero states.
</Given>

<Find>
The recursions for $\bar{\vec g}_{c,t}$ and the four pre-activation
derivatives; the derivatives sent to $\vec h_{t-1}$ and $\vec c_{t-1}$; the
parameter gradients; and, in the scalar case, $\partial c_3/\partial c_1$
compared with its carry-line term.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Draw the arrows leaving $\vec c_t$ and $\vec h_t$, add the returns, and only
then differentiate the activations. For the scalar part, notice that a
constant output gate makes $h_t$ a function of $c_t$.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**Arrows out of the cell.** The cell state $\vec c_t$ is used twice: by
$\vec h_t=\vec o_t\odot\tanh(\vec c_t)$, and by the next cell update, where it
is multiplied by $\vec f_{t+1}$. Reverse mode adds the two returns:

$$
\bar{\vec g}_{c,t}=\bar{\vec g}_{h,t}\odot\vec o_t\odot(1-\tanh^2\vec c_t)
 +\bar{\vec g}_{c,t+1}\odot\vec f_{t+1},
\qquad\bar{\vec g}_{c,T+1}=0.
$$

Here $\bar{\vec g}_{h,t}$ is already the total for $\vec h_t$: its readout term
plus everything step $t+1$ returned through its gates.

**Pre-activations.** Write $\vec a_{k,t}$ for the argument of the gate or
candidate $k$. Differentiating
$\vec h_t=\vec o_t\odot\tanh\vec c_t$ with respect to $\vec a_{o,t}$, and
$\vec c_t=\vec f_t\odot\vec c_{t-1}+\vec i_t\odot\tilde{\vec c}_t$ with
respect to the other three,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec\delta_{o,t}&=\bar{\vec g}_{h,t}\odot\tanh(\vec c_t)\odot\vec o_t\odot(1-\vec o_t),\\
\vec\delta_{f,t}&=\bar{\vec g}_{c,t}\odot\vec c_{t-1}\odot\vec f_t\odot(1-\vec f_t),\\
\vec\delta_{i,t}&=\bar{\vec g}_{c,t}\odot\tilde{\vec c}_t\odot\vec i_t\odot(1-\vec i_t),\\
\vec\delta_{c,t}&=\bar{\vec g}_{c,t}\odot\vec i_t\odot(1-\tilde{\vec c}_t^2).
\end{aligned}
$$

This is (I.10.6). The output gate is the only one that reads
$\bar{\vec g}_{h,t}$ directly; the other three see the cell's total.

**What goes back one step.** Every pre-activation contains
$\mat W_k\vec h_{t-1}$, so the recurrent return to $\vec h_{t-1}$ is
$\sum_{k\in\{i,f,o,c\}}\mat W_k^{\mathsf T}\vec\delta_{k,t}$. The cell one step
back receives $\bar{\vec g}_{c,t}\odot\vec f_t$, the carry line, which is the
second term of the next $\bar{\vec g}_{c,t-1}$. At $t=1$ these two quantities
are the derivatives with respect to $\vec h_0$ and $\vec c_0$.

**Parameters.** Each block is used at every step, so its gradients are sums,
as in (I.9.4):

$$
\nabla_{\mat W_k}\mathcal L=\sum_t\vec\delta_{k,t}\vec h_{t-1}^{\mathsf T},\quad
\nabla_{\mat U_k}\mathcal L=\sum_t\vec\delta_{k,t}\vec x_t^{\mathsf T},\quad
\nabla_{\vec b_k}\mathcal L=\sum_t\vec\delta_{k,t}.
$$

**The scalar case.** With constant gates $i=1/2$, $f=o=3/4$, the hidden state
$h_t=\tfrac34\tanh c_t$ is a function of $c_t$, so the state is $c$ alone and
the one-step derivative is

$$
k_t=\frac{\partial c_t}{\partial c_{t-1}}
=\underbrace{f}_{\text{carry line}}
 +\underbrace{i\,(1-\tilde c_t^2)\cdot2\cdot o\,(1-\tanh^2c_{t-1})}_{\text{through }h_{t-1}}.
$$

The forward pass gives $c_1=0.4$, $h_1=0.284962$, then
$\tilde c_2=\tanh(0.569924)$ and onwards. Evaluating along that trajectory,
$k_2=1.221326$ and $k_3=1.078827$, so

$$
\frac{\partial c_3}{\partial c_1}=k_3k_2=1.317600,
\qquad\text{carry line alone: }f^2=0.5625.
$$

Expanding $k_3k_2$ gives four terms. Only $f\cdot f$ avoids the recurrent
weight; the other three each contain it at least once, as I.10.T1 says.
Here they contribute $0.755100$, more than the carry line.

The reproduction script perturbs $c_1$ by $10^{-6}$, reruns steps two and three,
and matches $1.317600$ by central differences. It then checks the vector
recursions above on a two-unit cell with non-symmetric recurrent weights,
losses at three of four steps and nonzero initial states. All twelve parameter
blocks, $\vec c_0$ and $\vec h_0$ agree with finite differences.

</Solution>

<Answer>
$\bar{\vec g}_{c,t}=\bar{\vec g}_{h,t}\odot\vec o_t\odot(1-\tanh^2\vec c_t)+\bar{\vec g}_{c,t+1}\odot\vec f_{t+1}$,
with the four $\vec\delta$ above, a return of $\sum_k\mat W_k^{\mathsf T}\vec\delta_{k,t}$
to $\vec h_{t-1}$ and $\bar{\vec g}_{c,t}\odot\vec f_t$ to $\vec c_{t-1}$.
In the scalar case $\partial c_3/\partial c_1=1.317600$, of which the carry
line accounts for $0.5625$.
</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
Set every recurrent weight to zero. Then $\mat M_t=0$, the hidden-state return
vanishes, and the derivative between cell states is exactly the product of
forget values. In the scalar case $k_t=f$ and $\partial c_3/\partial c_1=0.5625$.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
Treating $\vec o_t$ as a function of $\vec c_t$ would add a false term:
the output gate reads $\vec h_{t-1}$ and $\vec x_t$, not the cell. A peephole
LSTM, which does let gates read the cell, adds genuine terms of that kind and
needs its own derivation.
</Breaks>

<Variation>
Replace the candidate weight two with zero. Both factors become $3/4$ and the
derivative falls to $0.5625$. The weight did not change the carry line; it
changed how much signal reached the cell by other routes.
</Variation>
