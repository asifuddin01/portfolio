Find the eigenvalues of the Laplacian $\mat L=\mat D-\mat A$ for two disjoint
triangles and for a hexagon. Show that they differ, explain which
eigenvalue counts components, and say what goes wrong if the eigenvectors
are fed to a network as node positions.

<Hint>
A cycle of $n$ nodes has Laplacian eigenvalues $2-2\cos(2\pi j/n)$. The
spectrum of a disjoint union is the union of the spectra.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

For the hexagon, $2-2\cos(2\pi j/6)$ for $j=0,\ldots,5$ gives
$0,1,3,4,3,1$, that is $\{0,1,1,3,3,4\}$. For one triangle it gives
$0,3,3$; for two, $\{0,0,3,3,3,3\}$. The spectra differ, so a network given
spectral information can separate the pair that defeated colour
refinement in [I.14.B03](/elementa/neural-networks/gnn/problems/b03).

The eigenvalue $0$ counts connected components. Since
$\vec x^{\mathsf T}\mat L\vec x=\sum_{(u,v)\in E}(x_u-x_v)^2$ by (I.14.4),
$\mat L\vec x=\vec 0$ exactly when $\vec x$ is constant across every edge,
that is, constant on each component. There is one independent such vector
per component: one for the hexagon, two for the triangles.

As positions the eigenvectors have two ambiguities. Each is defined only up
to sign, so a network must give the same answer for $\vec v$ and $-\vec v$,
or it depends on an arbitrary choice made by the eigensolver. And a
repeated eigenvalue has no preferred basis: the two triangles' eigenvalue
$3$ has a four-dimensional eigenspace, and any rotation within it is as
valid as any other. Positional features from the Laplacian therefore need
architectures invariant to these choices, or they reintroduce exactly the
dependence on arbitrary labels that equivariance was built to remove.

</SolutionToggle>
