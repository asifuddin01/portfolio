<Statement>
Solve the linear β-VAE exactly, find its rate and distortion, and follow
them as β goes to zero and as β grows past the point where the code is
abandoned.
</Statement>

<Given>
Centred data whose covariance has eigenvalues
$\lambda_1>\lambda_2\geq\cdots$, the top one along $\vec u_1$. One latent
dimension with prior $\mathcal N(0,1)$, a decoder
$\vec x\mid z\sim\mathcal N(\vec wz,\sigma^2\mat I)$ with $\sigma^2$ fixed, and
an encoder $\mathcal N(m,v)$ chosen freely for each input. The objective is
the average of $\E_q\log p(\vec x\mid z)-\beta\,\KL(q\,\|\,\mathcal N(0,1))$.
For numbers use the four points of I.12: $\lambda_1=4$, $\lambda_2=1$,
$\sigma^2=1$.
</Given>

<Find>
(a) The best encoder for a given $\vec w$. (b) The best $\|\vec w\|^2$.
(c) The rate and the mean squared distortion there. (d) The limits
$\beta\to0$ and $\beta\sigma^2\geq\lambda_1$. (e) A table for the four
points at $\beta=0.25$, $1$, $2$ and $4$.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Write $a=\vec w^{\mathsf T}\vec x$, $r=\|\vec w\|^2$ and $c=r+\beta\sigma^2$.
Optimise the encoder first; what is left depends on $\vec w$ only through
$r$ and its direction.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**(a)** For one input, up to terms that do not involve $m$ or $v$, the
objective is

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{2am-(m^2+v)r}{2\sigma^2}\\
&\quad-\frac\beta2\big(m^2+v-1-\log v\big).
\end{aligned}
$$

Setting the derivatives in $m$ and $v$ to zero,

$$
m=\frac{a}{c},\qquad v=\frac{\beta\sigma^2}{c}.
$$

**(b)** Substituting, the terms in $a^2$ combine to $a^2/(2\sigma^2c)$ and
the terms in $v$ to $\tfrac\beta2\log(\beta\sigma^2/c)$, up to constants.
Averaged over the data, $\E[a^2]=\vec w^{\mathsf T}\mat S\vec w$, which for
a given $r$ is largest, $r\lambda_1$, when $\vec w$ lies along $\vec u_1$.
What remains is

$$
\begin{aligned}
J(r)&=\frac{r\lambda_1}{2\sigma^2c}-\frac\beta2\log c\\
 &\quad+\text{const},
\end{aligned}
$$

with derivative

$$
J'(r)=\frac{\beta}{2c}\Big(\frac{\lambda_1}{c}-1\Big).
$$

It is positive while $c<\lambda_1$ and negative after, so

$$
r=\max(\lambda_1-\beta\sigma^2,\;0).
$$

**(c)** While the code is in use, $c=\lambda_1$. Then $\E[m^2]=r/\lambda_1$
and $v=\beta\sigma^2/\lambda_1$, and the average KL is

$$
\begin{aligned}
R&=\tfrac12\Big(\frac{r+\beta\sigma^2}{\lambda_1}-1\\
 &\qquad-\log\frac{\beta\sigma^2}{\lambda_1}\Big)\\
 &=\tfrac12\log\frac{\lambda_1}{\beta\sigma^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The squared error averages to
$\operatorname{tr}\mat S-2r+(\E[m^2]+v)\,r=\operatorname{tr}\mat S-r$, so

$$
D=\sum_{i>1}\lambda_i+\beta\sigma^2.
$$

**(d)** As $\beta\to0$, $r\to\lambda_1$, the encoder's variance
$\beta\sigma^2/\lambda_1$ goes to zero, the rate grows like
$-\tfrac12\log\beta$ without bound, and the distortion falls to
$\sum_{i>1}\lambda_i$, the loss of the principal-axis autoencoder of
I.12.T1. The code tends to $\vec u_1^{\mathsf T}\vec x/\sqrt{\lambda_1}$:
the principal score, scaled to the prior's unit variance.

As $\beta\sigma^2$ rises to $\lambda_1$, $r$ and $R$ fall to zero and $D$
rises to $\operatorname{tr}\mat S$. For any larger $\beta$, $J'(r)<0$ for
all $r\geq0$: the optimum is $\vec w=\vec 0$, the encoder returns
$\mathcal N(0,1)$ for every input, and the code is ignored.

**(e)** With $\lambda_1=4$, $\lambda_2=1$ and $\sigma^2=1$, $R=\tfrac12\log(4/\beta)$
and $D=1+\beta$:

| $\beta$ | $r$ | $v$ | $R$ | $D$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $0.25$ | $3.75$ | $0.0625$ | $1.386294$ | $1.25$ |
| $1$ | $3$ | $0.25$ | $0.693147$ | $2$ |
| $2$ | $2$ | $0.5$ | $0.346574$ | $3$ |
| $4$ | $0$ | $1$ | $0$ | $5$ |

</Solution>

<Answer>
$m=a/(r+\beta\sigma^2)$ and $v=\beta\sigma^2/(r+\beta\sigma^2)$;
$\|\vec w\|^2=\max(\lambda_1-\beta\sigma^2,0)$ along $\vec u_1$; while in use,
$R=\tfrac12\log(\lambda_1/\beta\sigma^2)$ and
$D=\sum_{i>1}\lambda_i+\beta\sigma^2$. As $\beta\to0$, $D\to\sum_{i>1}\lambda_i$
and $R\to\infty$; from $\beta\sigma^2=\lambda_1$ on, the code is unused, with
$R=0$ and $D=\operatorname{tr}\mat S$.
</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
At $\beta=1$ the encoder is the exact posterior of (I.13.2), $r=3$ is
probabilistic PCA's $\|\vec w\|^2$, and the objective equals the
log-evidence $-3.531024$ of every corner. The curve is continuous at the
threshold: $1+4=5=\operatorname{tr}\mat S$.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
The solution needs $\sigma^2$ fixed. If it is learned, at $\beta=1$ it
settles at the average discarded eigenvalue, and a direction collapses
when its eigenvalue does not exceed that. With several latent dimensions
the same threshold applies to each eigen-direction separately.
</Breaks>

<Variation>
At $\beta=8$ the code is unused, as at $\beta=4$. At $\beta=0.5$ the rate
is $\tfrac12\log8=1.039721$ and the distortion $1.5$. Halving $\beta$
always adds $\tfrac12\log2=0.346574$ nats to the rate.
</Variation>
