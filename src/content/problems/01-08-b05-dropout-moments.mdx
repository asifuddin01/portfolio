<Statement>
Compute dropout's distribution exactly, rather than estimating it by sampling.
Use the result to test the claim that deterministic inference is the exact
average of the masked networks.
</Statement>

<Given>
A fixed activation $h=2$, independent keep probability $q=1/2$, inverted
dropout $\widetilde h=Mh/q$, and a downstream function $f(u)=u^2$.
</Given>

<Find>
The mean and variance of the masked activation, the expected squared output,
and the deterministic inference prediction. Then derive the general formulas
for fixed $h$ and $0<q\leq1$.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Enumerate the two mask outcomes. Keep $\mathbb E[f(\widetilde h)]$ separate
from $f(\mathbb E[\widetilde h])$.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

| Mask $M$ | Probability | $\widetilde h$ | $f(\widetilde h)$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $0$ | $1/2$ | $0$ | $0$ |
| $1$ | $1/2$ | $4$ | $16$ |

The first moment is $(0+4)/2=2$. The second moment is $(0+16)/2=8$.
Subtracting the square of the first moment gives variance $8-2^2=4$.

At inference inverted dropout leaves $h$ unchanged, so the squared output
is $f(2)=4$. But averaging the masked predictions gives $8$.
The discrepancy is the activation variance, not a sampling error.

For arbitrary fixed $h$, the outcomes are zero and $h/q$:

$$
\mathbb E[\widetilde h]=q\frac hq=h,\qquad
\mathbb E[\widetilde h^2]=q\frac{h^2}{q^2}=\frac{h^2}{q}.
$$

Therefore $\operatorname{Var}(\widetilde h)=h^2(1-q)/q$, as in (I.8.8).
For an affine downstream map $au+b$, expectation does commute with the map.
The square gives a concrete case where it does not.

For independent masks on several fixed activations, the dropout-induced
off-diagonal covariances are zero. This is conditional on the activations.
It does not claim that the activations themselves are independent across data.

</Solution>

<Answer>
Mean $2$, variance $4$, expected masked squared output $8$, and deterministic
squared output $4$. Inverted dropout preserves the activation mean and
does not, in general, preserve the expected downstream prediction.
</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
At $q=1$ both paths coincide and the variance is zero. For fixed nonzero $h$,
the variance grows without bound as $q$ tends to zero. The operation is not
defined at $q=0$ by the formula used here.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
This arithmetic is not a claim about the test accuracy of dropout. It shows
exactly what the scaling guarantees and what it does not. Correlated masks,
such as dropping a whole channel together, produce a different covariance
structure even when the marginal mean formula still holds.
</Breaks>

<Variation>
Let $h=0$ or replace the square with an affine function. Explain why the
counterexample disappears without establishing the general equality.
</Variation>
