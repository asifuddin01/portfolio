A batch of $N=32$ sequences of length $T=1000$ trains a layer of width
$H=512$ in float32. Under the storage scheme of
[I.10.B05](/elementa/neural-networks/gating/problems/b05), how many bytes of
activations does a plain tanh layer keep for the backward pass, and how many
does an LSTM layer keep? How do both scale with $T$, and what does inference
need instead?

<Hint>
The plain cell stores one $H$-vector per step and sequence; the LSTM stores six.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

The plain layer stores $4NTH=65{,}536{,}000$ bytes, $62.5$ MiB.
The LSTM stores six times as much, $393{,}216{,}000$ bytes, $375$ MiB.
Both grow linearly in $T$: doubling the sequence doubles the storage.

Streaming inference keeps only the current state: $\vec h_t$ for the plain
cell, $\vec h_t$ and $\vec c_t$ for the LSTM, about $2NH$ numbers, independent
of $T$. The distinction of I.9 holds for gated cells too: constant inference
memory says nothing about training memory. Truncated backpropagation or
checkpointing reduces the training figure at the costs I.9 described.

</SolutionToggle>
