A CNN returns 64 feature channels on a $7$ by $7$ grid.
Compare a flattened affine head to ten classes with global average pooling
followed by an affine ten-class head. Include biases.
What distinction can the flattened head express that the pooled head cannot?

<Hint>
Flattening preserves 49 coordinates per channel; averaging retains one.
</Hint>
<SolutionToggle>

Flattening gives $64\cdot7\cdot7=3136$ inputs.
The affine head has $3136\cdot10+10=31370$ parameters.
After global averaging it has $64\cdot10+10=650$.

A flattened head can weight a feature differently at two positions.
A pooled head receives the same channel average after any permutation
within that channel and cannot distinguish those permutations from that
representation alone.

The parameter saving does not prove that the smaller head is better.
Location may be nuisance variation for one task and essential evidence
for another. The upstream feature extractor can also encode some positional
information before pooling, so the limitation is stated at the feature-map
interface, not as a universal claim about all CNN classifiers.

</SolutionToggle>
