<Statement>
A perceptron's parameters are given. Write the equation of its decision
boundary, classify four points, and compute each point's signed distance from
the boundary.
</Statement>

<Given>
$$\vec{w} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad b = -1$$

and the four points

$$A = (2, 1), \quad B = (0, 1), \quad C = (1, -1), \quad D = (0, 3)$$
</Given>

<Find>
The boundary as an equation in $x_1, x_2$; the predicted label of each point;
and each signed distance, a scalar in the same units as the coordinates.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Compute the raw score $s = \langle \vec{w}, \vec{x}\rangle + b$ once per point.
The sign of $s$ gives the class and $s/\lVert\vec{w}\rVert$ gives the distance,
so one quantity answers both questions.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**Step 1 — the boundary.** By Definition 2 the boundary is the set where the
score is zero:

$$2x_1 - x_2 - 1 = 0 \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad x_2 = 2x_1 - 1$$

A line of slope $2$ through $(0.5, 0)$. Note that $\vec{w} = (2, -1)$ is
perpendicular to it: the direction along the line is $(1, 2)$, and
$\langle (2,-1),(1,2)\rangle = 2 - 2 = 0$. **The weight vector is always the
normal to the boundary.** That single fact makes every later step geometric
rather than algebraic.

**Step 2 — the norm.** Needed once, for all four points:

$$\lVert \vec{w}\rVert = \sqrt{2^2 + (-1)^2} = \sqrt{5} = 2.2361$$

**Step 3 — the scores.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
s_A &= (2)(2) + (-1)(1) - 1 = 4 - 1 - 1 = +2 \\
s_B &= (2)(0) + (-1)(1) - 1 = 0 - 1 - 1 = -2 \\
s_C &= (2)(1) + (-1)(-1) - 1 = 2 + 1 - 1 = +2 \\
s_D &= (2)(0) + (-1)(3) - 1 = 0 - 3 - 1 = -4
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 4 — labels and distances.** Divide each score by $\sqrt5$
(<ApparatusRef ref="0.LA.05" />):

| Point | Score | Distance | Class |
|---|---|---|---|
| $A = (2,1)$ | $+2$ | $+0.8944$ | $+1$ |
| $B = (0,1)$ | $-2$ | $-0.8944$ | $-1$ |
| $C = (1,-1)$ | $+2$ | $+0.8944$ | $+1$ |
| $D = (0,3)$ | $-4$ | $-1.7889$ | $-1$ |

</Solution>

<Answer>
Boundary: $2x_1 - x_2 - 1 = 0$.

$$
\text{dist}(A) = +0.8944,\quad
\text{dist}(B) = -0.8944,\quad
\text{dist}(C) = +0.8944,\quad
\text{dist}(D) = -1.7889
$$

Classes: $A, C \to +1$ and $B, D \to -1$. Distances are scalars in the units of
the coordinate axes; the scores are not, which is why the division by
$\lVert\vec{w}\rVert$ is not optional.
</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
**A point on the boundary has distance zero.** Take $x = (0.5, 0)$, which
satisfies $x_2 = 2x_1 - 1$. Its score is $(2)(0.5) - 0 - 1 = 0$, so its distance
is $0$. The formula agrees with the definition it came from.

**Rescaling the parameters changes nothing geometric.** Double both:
$\vec{w} = (4,-2)$, $b = -2$. Then $s_A = 4$ and $\lVert\vec{w}\rVert = 2\sqrt5$,
so the distance is $4/(2\sqrt5) = 0.8944$ — unchanged. The scores doubled and
the distances did not, which is exactly the difference between the functional
margin (Definition 4) and the geometric one (Definition 5).

**Moving a point along the boundary direction changes nothing.** Shift $A$ by
$(1,2)$, the direction along the line, to get $(3,3)$: its score is
$6 - 3 - 1 = 2$, identical. Moving perpendicular to $\vec{w}$ cannot change the
score, because $\vec{w}$ is what the score measures against.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
The distance formula requires $\lVert\vec{w}\rVert \neq 0$. At $\vec{w} = \vec{0}$
the score is the constant $b$ for every input, the "boundary" is empty or all of
$\R^2$, and the division is undefined. That is not a pathological corner: it is
the state a perceptron is initialised in, which is why the first update of
I.2.B02 has margin exactly zero and the rule must treat $\le 0$ rather than
$< 0$ as a mistake.
</Breaks>
