<Statement>
Fit labels drawn by coin flips exactly with a small ReLU network, compare
it with a straight line, and show that the zero training loss predicts
nothing about new points.
</Statement>

<Given>
Inputs $x=0,1,2,3$ with labels $(0,1,0,1)$, which were drawn by fair coin
flips. The network is
$f(x)=x-2\operatorname{relu}(x-1)+2\operatorname{relu}(x-2)$. The line is
the least-squares fit $y=a+bx$. New inputs $0.5$, $1.5$ and $2.5$ get labels
from fresh coin flips. Losses are mean squared errors.
</Given>

<Find>
The network's training loss; the line and its training loss; the expected
loss of each, and of the constant $0.5$, on the new points.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Evaluate the network piece by piece. For a prediction $p$ and a label that
is $0$ or $1$ with probability one half, the expected squared error is
$(p-\tfrac12)^2+\tfrac14$.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**The network.** Its slopes are $1$ before $x=1$, $-1$ between $1$ and $2$,
and $1$ after $2$. So $f(0)=0$, $f(1)=1$, $f(2)=0$, $f(3)=1$: the labels
exactly, training loss $0$. Two ReLU units and a linear term were enough
to put a kink at every change of label (I.5.P02).

**The line.** With $\bar x=1.5$ and $\bar y=0.5$, the slope is
$\sum(x-\bar x)(y-\bar y)/\sum(x-\bar x)^2=1/5=0.2$ and the intercept
$0.5-0.2\cdot1.5=0.2$. Its residuals are $-0.2$, $0.6$, $-0.6$ and $0.2$,
so its training loss is $0.2$.

**New points.** The network predicts $0.5$ at all three, so its expected
loss is $\tfrac14=0.25$, exactly that of the constant $0.5$. The line
predicts $0.3$, $0.5$ and $0.7$, with expected loss
$\tfrac13(0.04+0+0.04)+0.25=0.276667$.

So the network with zero training loss does no better than a constant on
new points, and the line, which fitted worse, does slightly worse still.
Neither number on the training set said anything about the new points,
because the labels carried no pattern to learn. That is I.1.P05: the
training loss cannot tell memorisation from generalisation.

**The same failure, twice more.** Chapter I.12 met it in the autoencoder
as wide as its input, whose zero loss reconstructed points far from the
data just as exactly (I.12.X04), and in the width-one decoder that threaded
a curve through every training point (I.12.B04). In all three the
capacity to fit was the problem, and only held-out data revealed it.

</Solution>

<Answer>
Network training loss $0$; line $y=0.2+0.2x$ with training loss $0.2$. On
new coin-flip labels: network $0.25$, line $0.276667$, constant $0.25$.
</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
No predictor can beat $0.25$ in expectation on fair coin flips, since
$(p-\tfrac12)^2+\tfrac14$ is smallest at $p=\tfrac12$. Both the network and
the constant reach that floor on these three points.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
With labels that follow a pattern, the same architecture can generalise:
capacity permits memorisation without forcing it. What separates the two
cases is not the training loss but a held-out evaluation drawn from the
same distribution.
</Breaks>

<Variation>
With $n$ distinct inputs on a line, a network with $n-2$ ReLU kinks and a
linear term interpolates any labels, so a network of that size reaches
zero training loss on every labelling, patterned or not.
</Variation>
