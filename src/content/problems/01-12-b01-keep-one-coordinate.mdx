<Statement>
Run a width-one linear autoencoder by hand on four points. Then improve the
decoder alone, and after it the encoder alone, using their closed forms.
</Statement>

<Given>
The data are $(1,2)$, $(2.2,0.4)$, $(-1,-2)$ and $(-2.2,-0.4)$. They are
centred, with

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mat S&=\begin{bmatrix}2.92&1.44\\1.44&2.08\end{bmatrix},\\
\operatorname{tr}\mat S&=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

The autoencoder starts at $\mat E=(1,\,0)$ and $\mat D=(1,\,0)^{\mathsf T}$:
it keeps the first coordinate and writes it back.
</Given>

<Find>
The four codes, the reconstructions and the loss. The best decoder for this
encoder and its loss. The best encoder for that decoder and its loss. Where
the sequence goes if the two repairs are alternated.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Evaluate (I.12.1) directly, then apply the two lines of (I.12.3) in turn.
After a decoder repair the loss is
$\operatorname{tr}\mat S-\operatorname{tr}(\mat D\mat E\mat S)$.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**The starting point.** The codes are the first coordinates:
$1$, $2.2$, $-1$ and $-2.2$. Each reconstruction is $(z,0)$, so each error
is the square of the second coordinate: $4$, $0.16$, $4$ and $0.16$. The
loss is their mean, $2.08$. That is $S_{22}$, the variance of the
coordinate that was dropped.

**Repair the decoder.** With $\mat E=(1,0)$ the code variance is
$\mat E\mat S\mat E^{\mathsf T}=2.92$ and
$\mat S\mat E^{\mathsf T}=(2.92,\,1.44)^{\mathsf T}$. The first line of
(I.12.3) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mat D&=\frac{1}{2.92}\begin{bmatrix}2.92\\1.44\end{bmatrix}\\[2pt]
 &=\begin{bmatrix}1\\0.493151\end{bmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The decoder now predicts the second coordinate from the first by
regression. The loss falls to

$$
\begin{aligned}
5-\frac{2.92^2+1.44^2}{2.92}
 &=5-\frac{10.6}{2.92}\\
 &=1.369863.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Repair the encoder.** For this decoder
$\mat D^{\mathsf T}\mat D=1.243198$, and the second line of (I.12.3) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mat E&=\frac{\mat D^{\mathsf T}}{1.243198}\\
 &=(0.804377,\;0.396679).
\end{aligned}
$$

The autoencoder is now the projection onto the direction of $\mat D$. By
(I.12.4) its loss is $5$ minus the variance along that direction, which is
$1.101887$.

**Alternate.** Two more repairs give $1.026137$ and $1.006577$, and the
sequence converges to $1$.

The reason is visible in the two formulas. The decoder repair sets
$\mat D$ proportional to $\mat S\mat E^{\mathsf T}$, and the encoder repair
sets $\mat E$ proportional to $\mat D^{\mathsf T}$. One full sweep
therefore multiplies the direction by $\mat S$, which is the power method.
The starting direction $(1,0)$ equals $0.8\,\vec u_1-0.6\,\vec u_2$ for the
eigenvectors $\vec u_1=(0.8,0.6)$ and $\vec u_2=(-0.6,0.8)$ of $\mat S$,
with eigenvalues $4$ and $1$. Each sweep divides the tangent of the angle
to $\vec u_1$ by $4$: it is $0.75$, then $0.1875$, then $0.046875$. A
projection at angle $\varphi$ to $\vec u_1$ has loss $1+3\sin^2\varphi$,
which reproduces $1.101887$ and $1.006577$.

</Solution>

<Answer>
Codes $(1,\,2.2,\,-1,\,-2.2)$ and loss $2.08$. Best decoder
$(1,\,0.493151)^{\mathsf T}$ with loss $1.369863$. Best encoder
$(0.804377,\,0.396679)$ with loss $1.101887$. Then $1.026137$, $1.006577$,
and the limit $1$, the smaller eigenvalue of $\mat S$.
</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
The losses decrease at every step, because each repair is an exact
minimisation over one matrix with the other held fixed. None falls below
$1$, the bound of I.12.T1.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
Start the encoder at $\vec u_2^{\mathsf T}=(-0.6,\,0.8)$. Then
$\mat S\mat E^{\mathsf T}$ is a multiple of $\vec u_2$, the repaired decoder
is $\vec u_2$ again, and the alternation never moves. The loss stays at
$4$. This is the saddle of I.12.T2, and an exact start on it is the only
way to remain there.
</Breaks>

<Variation>
Keep the second coordinate instead, $\mat E=(0,1)$ and
$\mat D=(0,1)^{\mathsf T}$. The loss is $S_{11}=2.92$, worse than before
because the first coordinate has the larger variance. One decoder repair
gives $\mat D=(0.692308,\,1)^{\mathsf T}$ and a loss of
$5-6.4/2.08=1.923077$.
</Variation>
