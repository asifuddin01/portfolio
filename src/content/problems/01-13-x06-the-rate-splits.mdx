At $\beta=1$ the four points have encoder distributions
$\mathcal N(\pm0.866025,\,0.25)$, two corners at each sign. Find the
average KL to the prior, the mutual information between the example and
its code, and the KL from the aggregate posterior to the prior, and check
that (I.13.9) holds. Compare the aggregate posterior with the prior at
$z=0$.

<Hint>
Two corners share each distribution, so the information between the
example and the code equals the information between the sign and the code.
That is $\mathcal H(q(z))-\mathcal H(q(z\mid\text{sign}))$
(<ApparatusRef ref="0.IT.04" />). The first entropy has no closed form;
integrate it numerically.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

**Average KL.** Every corner has $m^2=0.75$ and $v=0.25$, so each KL is
$\tfrac12(0.75+0.25-1-\log0.25)=\log2=0.693147$, and so is the average.

**Information.** The aggregate posterior is the mixture
$q(z)=\tfrac12\mathcal N(0.866025,0.25)+\tfrac12\mathcal N(-0.866025,0.25)$.
Each component has entropy
$\tfrac12\log(2\pi e\cdot0.25)=0.725791$. Numerical integration of
$-q\log q$ gives the mixture's entropy, and the difference is

$$
I_q(n;z)=0.585939.
$$

It is below $\log2=0.693147$, the most a binary sign could carry, because
the two components overlap.

**Mismatch.** $\KL(q(z)\,\|\,\mathcal N(0,1))
=-\mathcal H(q)+\tfrac12\log2\pi+\tfrac12\E_q[z^2]$, and
$\E_q[z^2]=0.75+0.25=1$. Numerically it is $0.107208$.

**The identity.** $0.585939+0.107208=0.693147$, the average KL. Most of
the rate pays for information about the example; about a sixth pays for
the aggregate posterior not being the prior.

**At $z=0$.** The mixture's density is
$2\cdot\tfrac12\mathcal N(0;0.866025,0.25)=0.178032$, and the prior's is
$1/\sqrt{2\pi}=0.398942$. The mixture has the prior's variance but not its
shape. Codes drawn from the prior near zero, which are the commonest, are
codes the encoder produced less than half as often. That is a hole, and
the second term of (I.13.9) is its price.
