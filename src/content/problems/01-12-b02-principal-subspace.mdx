<Statement>
Prove that the smallest loss of a linear autoencoder is the sum of the
discarded eigenvalues, find every pair of matrices that attains it, and
evaluate the result on four points.
</Statement>

<Given>
Centred data with covariance
$\mat S=\sum_{i=1}^d\lambda_i\vec u_i\vec u_i^{\mathsf T}$, where
$\lambda_1\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_d>0$ and the $\vec u_i$ are orthonormal. The
loss is the first line of (I.12.2), with
$\mat E\in\R^{k\times d}$, $\mat D\in\R^{d\times k}$ and $k<d$. Assume
$\lambda_k>\lambda_{k+1}$. For the numbers, use the four points of
[I.12.B01](/elementa/neural-networks/autoencoders/problems/b01) with $k=1$.
</Given>

<Find>
(a) The best encoder for a fixed decoder, and the end-to-end map it gives.
(b) The loss of that map in terms of an orthonormal basis.
(c) The lower bound and the condition for equality.
(d) All minimising pairs.
(e) The eigenvalues, the optimal map, its loss and two different
factorisations for the four points.
(f) The other stationary projection and a path that leaves it downhill.
</Find>

<Strategy>
Reduce pairs of matrices to subspaces by solving for the encoder. Then
write the retained variance as a weighted sum of eigenvalues and bound the
weights.
</Strategy>

<Solution>

**(a)** Fix $\mat D$ with independent columns. For one input, the
derivative of $\|\vec x-\mat D\vec z\|^2$ with respect to $\vec z$ is
$-2\mat D^{\mathsf T}(\vec x-\mat D\vec z)$. It vanishes at

$$
\vec z=(\mat D^{\mathsf T}\mat D)^{-1}\mat D^{\mathsf T}\vec x,
$$

which is the unique minimum because $\mat D^{\mathsf T}\mat D$ is positive
definite. The same matrix serves every input, so
$\mat E=(\mat D^{\mathsf T}\mat D)^{-1}\mat D^{\mathsf T}$ and

$$
\mat D\mat E=\mat D(\mat D^{\mathsf T}\mat D)^{-1}\mat D^{\mathsf T}=\mat P.
$$

Direct multiplication gives $\mat P^2=\mat P$ and
$\mat P^{\mathsf T}=\mat P$: the orthogonal projection onto the columns of
$\mat D$. No other encoder gives any input a better code, so no encoder
has a smaller loss than $\mat P$. Because $\lambda_d>0$ the data span every
direction, and a different encoder gives some of them a strictly worse one.

**(b)** Choose $\mat Q\in\R^{d\times k}$ with orthonormal columns spanning
the same subspace. Then $\mat P=\mat Q\mat Q^{\mathsf T}$, and
$\mat I-\mat P$ is symmetric with $(\mat I-\mat P)^2=\mat I-\mat P$.
Moving one factor around the trace,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L(\mat P)
 &=\operatorname{tr}\big((\mat I-\mat P)\mat S(\mat I-\mat P)\big)\\
 &=\operatorname{tr}\big(\mat S(\mat I-\mat P)\big)\\
 &=\operatorname{tr}\mat S-\operatorname{tr}(\mat Q^{\mathsf T}\mat S\mat Q).
\end{aligned}
$$

This is (I.12.4).

**(c)** Substitute the eigendecomposition:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{tr}(\mat Q^{\mathsf T}\mat S\mat Q)
 &=\sum_{i=1}^d\lambda_i c_i,\\
c_i&=\|\mat Q^{\mathsf T}\vec u_i\|^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $\mat Q\mat Q^{\mathsf T}$ is a projection and $\vec u_i$ a unit
vector, $0\leq c_i\leq1$. Since the $\vec u_i$ are an orthonormal basis,
$\sum_ic_i=\operatorname{tr}(\mat Q^{\mathsf T}\mat Q)=k$. Therefore
$\sum_{i\leq k}(1-c_i)=\sum_{i>k}c_i$, and

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{i\leq k}\lambda_i-\sum_{i=1}^d\lambda_ic_i\\
&\;=\sum_{i\leq k}\lambda_i(1-c_i)
 -\sum_{i>k}\lambda_ic_i\\
&\;\geq\lambda_k\sum_{i\leq k}(1-c_i)\\
&\;\quad-\lambda_{k+1}\sum_{i>k}c_i\\
&\;=(\lambda_k-\lambda_{k+1})\sum_{i>k}c_i\\
&\;\geq0.
\end{aligned}
$$

So the retained variance is at most $\lambda_1+\cdots+\lambda_k$, and

$$
\mathcal L\geq\sum_{i>k}\lambda_i.
$$

Because $\lambda_k>\lambda_{k+1}$, equality requires $c_i=0$ for every
$i>k$, hence $c_i=1$ for every $i\leq k$. A unit vector whose projection
has length one lies in the subspace. The subspace is therefore the span of
$\vec u_1,\ldots,\vec u_k$, and $\mat P=\mat U_k\mat U_k^{\mathsf T}$.

**(d)** A decoder attains the bound exactly when its columns span that
subspace, that is, $\mat D=\mat U_k\mat G$ with
$\mat G=\mat U_k^{\mathsf T}\mat D$ invertible. By (a) the encoder must then
be

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mat E&=(\mat G^{\mathsf T}\mat G)^{-1}\mat G^{\mathsf T}\mat U_k^{\mathsf T}\\
 &=\mat G^{-1}\mat U_k^{\mathsf T}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Every invertible $\mat G$ gives a minimiser, and these are all of them.
This is (I.12.5).

A decoder of rank below $k$ spans fewer than $k$ dimensions. Its retained
variance is at most $\lambda_1+\cdots+\lambda_{k-1}$, which is strictly
less because $\lambda_k>0$.

**(e)** For the four points, $\mat S$ has trace $5$ and determinant
$2.92\cdot2.08-1.44^2=4$, so its eigenvalues solve
$\lambda^2-5\lambda+4=0$: they are $4$ and $1$. Solving
$(\mat S-4\mat I)\vec u=\vec 0$ gives $\vec u_1=(0.8,0.6)$, and then
$\vec u_2=(-0.6,0.8)$. With $k=1$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mat D\mat E&=\vec u_1\vec u_1^{\mathsf T}
 =\begin{bmatrix}0.64&0.48\\0.48&0.36\end{bmatrix},\\
\mathcal L&=\lambda_2=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

For the point $(1,2)$ the code under $\mat G=1$ is $0.8+1.2=2$, the
reconstruction is $(1.6,1.2)$ and the residual is $(-0.6,0.8)$, of squared
length $1$. The same holds at every corner.

Two factorisations of the same map: $\mat G=1$ gives
$\mat D=(0.8,0.6)^{\mathsf T}$ and $\mat E=(0.8,\,0.6)$. $\mat G=1/5$
gives $\mat D=(0.16,0.12)^{\mathsf T}$ and $\mat E=(4,\,3)$.

**(f)** Setting $\mat D=\vec u_2$ and $\mat E=\vec u_2^{\mathsf T}$ makes
both gradients of (I.12.2) vanish, with loss $\lambda_1=4$. Rotate the
direction to $\cos\theta\,\vec u_2+\sin\theta\,\vec u_1$. By (b) the loss
is $5-(\cos^2\theta+4\sin^2\theta)=4-3\sin^2\theta$. At $\theta=0.1$ it is
$3.970100$. The point is stationary and is not a minimum.

</Solution>

<Answer>
The minimum is $\sum_{i>k}\lambda_i$, attained exactly by
$\mat D=\mat U_k\mat G$, $\mat E=\mat G^{-1}\mat U_k^{\mathsf T}$ for
invertible $\mat G$. For the four points the eigenvalues are $4$ and $1$,
the optimal map is $\begin{bmatrix}0.64&0.48\\0.48&0.36\end{bmatrix}$ and
the loss is $1$. The projection onto $\vec u_2$ is a saddle with loss $4$.
</Answer>

<CheckSanity>
The optimal map has trace $1$, the dimension of the subspace, and squares
to itself: its first entry is $0.64^2+0.48^2=0.64$. The retained variance
$4$ and the loss $1$ add to the total $5$.
</CheckSanity>

<Breaks>
With $\lambda_k=\lambda_{k+1}$ the last step fails: weight can move between
the tied directions without changing the sum, and the optimal subspace is
not unique
([I.12.X05](/elementa/neural-networks/autoencoders/problems/x05)). With a
zero eigenvalue the encoder is determined only on the directions in which
the data vary. With uncentred data $\mat S$ is not a covariance and the
subspace found is a different one
([I.12.X03](/elementa/neural-networks/autoencoders/problems/x03)).
</Breaks>

<Variation>
The script scans $3600$ directions and confirms that the loss of the
projection onto each lies between $1$ and $4$, the two eigenvalues. For
$k=d$ the bound is an empty sum: the loss is $0$ and the map is the
identity
([I.12.X04](/elementa/neural-networks/autoencoders/problems/x04)).
</Variation>
