For fixed nonzero $h$, examine (I.8.8) as $q$ tends to one and as $q$ tends to
zero. Next let $h$ be equally likely to be $-1$ or $1$ and keep it exactly
when $h=1$. The overall keep fraction is $1/2$. Does dividing by that fraction
preserve the mean?

<Hint>
The theorem conditions on $h$. An overall keep fraction does not establish
independence between the mask and the activation.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

At $q=1$, dropout is the identity and its induced variance is zero.
As $q$ tends to zero through positive values, the mean remains $h$ but the
variance $h^2(1-q)/q$ diverges. Rare surviving activations become arbitrarily
large. The formula cannot be evaluated at $q=0$.

In the dependent-mask example, $Mh/q$ is zero when $h=-1$ and two when
$h=1$. Its mean is one, whereas $\mathbb E[h]=0$.
The mask kept half the examples but preferentially selected the positive ones.
Conditional on $h=-1$, its keep probability is zero, not the advertised $q$.

The usual expectation calculation needs a mask sampled independently of the
fixed activation, or a separately specified conditional sampling and weighting
scheme. Matching an aggregate drop fraction is insufficient.

</SolutionToggle>
