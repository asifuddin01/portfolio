A GRU's update gate sits at $z=0.9$ for ten steps while the candidate is zero.
How much of the starting state survives under PyTorch's convention,
$h_t=(1-z)n_t+z\,h_{t-1}$, and under Chung and colleagues',
$h_t=(1-z)h_{t-1}+z\,n_t$? How do you convert trained weights from one
convention to the other?

<Hint>
With a zero candidate, each step multiplies the state by whichever factor
sits next to $h_{t-1}$.
</Hint>

<SolutionToggle>

Under PyTorch's convention each step keeps $z$: after ten steps
$0.9^{10}=0.348678$ of the starting state remains. Under the other, each step
keeps $1-z$: $0.1^{10}=10^{-10}$. The direct Jacobians are $\operatorname{diag}(\vec z)$
and $\operatorname{diag}(1-\vec z)$ respectively.

The two conventions describe the same functions. Since
$1-\sigma(a)=\sigma(-a)$, negating the update gate's input weights, recurrent
weights and bias turns $z$ into $1-z$. Loading weights without that step
silently swaps keeping and overwriting.

</SolutionToggle>
